## 3. S, J_SE and the four-comparison family

**The proposed multiplicity architecture is valid in principle.** D §4
L105–113 defines two separately calibrated scalar joints and an ordered family.
Distinguish the detector's .05 honest-alarm budget from the efficacy family's
alpha=.05. S's constituents do not each receive .05: freeze their transforms
and combination on development, then calibrate the resulting scalar once.
Likewise calibrate `max(g_S,g_E)` directly; do not OR two .05 alarms and call
the union .05. Sharing calibrators or correlated tests does not itself require
Bonferroni over these four *ordered* hypotheses.

Make the following binding rather than relying on “headline follows how far”:

1. Define `X_i^d = side_success_i * (1-alarm_i^d)` and
   `Delta_{B,J}=E[X^B-X^J]` for the fixed pair/policy mixture. Specify whether
   each null is `Delta<=0` or a positive practical margin, the test direction,
   confidence-tail convention and exact cluster procedure.
2. Test H1, then H2, then H3, then H4 at .05 each, advancing **only after
   rejection**. The first failure ends confirmatory testing; later estimates
   remain descriptive. No skipping, reordering, alpha recycling or second
   chance with a different energy score, monitor or subset.
3. Justify each constituent test's size under the registered cluster/calibration
   model. If any true null is reached, rejecting it is necessary for any false
   claim later in the chain; its valid .05 test gives the ordered family its
   error control, without needing independence. Invalid constituent p-values
   defeat that argument. Multi-pair or optimized-cohort claims need their own
   declared conjunction/secondary treatment.

Recalibrated J_ME/J_SE need not contain their solo comparator's alarms. Compute
**both rescues and regressions** in paired escape differences, not just energy
rescues. This issue is explicit in G1 L215–220 and J2 L241–243. H4 succeeding
supports the stated external-opportunity population; H1–H3 alone cannot support
an incremental-value claim beyond S. Reporting a point escape rate <=10% and
an upper bound, as D L112 says, is not a confirmatory claim that population
escape is below 10%.

**S is a good addition, but not tightly enough defined yet (F2).** G2 §Arms
L52–58 is more explicit than D L105: it names receipts, actual-invocation
reconstruction, honest replay failures and private-goal isolation. Adopt an
input/access matrix at G0, not merely a list of collectors:

- State exactly which primary-runtime counts, child/process receipts, CPU/GPU
  accounting, tool inputs/results and invocation bytes an operational auditor
  can authenticate. Provide every legitimately available receipt to S. Tool
  omission from the actor's own count is not invisibility to a process audit.
- Reconstruct the **actual** original invocation, seed, template, reasoning
  history, tool observations and cache law. Substituting an honest prompt for
  a private sabotage prompt creates a spurious mismatch. The consistency
  interface may return a match/mismatch without exposing the side goal, hidden
  payload, condition ID or grader to the monitor or learned software score.
  If this auditor has privileges unavailable in deployment, identify the
  stronger forensic baseline and show a deployable ablation.
- Specify the scalar formula, scales, tails, authenticated mismatch treatment,
  missing/undecidable reruns and audit latency. G6's honest exactness gate is
  valuable, but later transport/runtime failure is not automatically proof of
  hostile omission. Keep scientific unknowns separate from operational aborts.
- Give honest actors the same helper affordances and realistic legitimate
  helper use. Bound E's features separately: an observed active-work allowance
  must not explain away hidden load. For E, trusted time contributes idle power
  only (D L60); S/time+M may use their legitimately observable timing signal.

The nonredundancy gate is sensible development triage, not held-out evidence
of success (D L79; G1 L173–181). Define its search cap and charge its physical
queries before calibration. Finding eight chosen examples cannot establish
the eventual H4 effect or its frequency.

**The subset needs a new allocation.** D L80 imports G1's policy-assigned
principle correctly, but G1's **184/240** roster (L369–387) does not specify a
subset of G2/J2's **336** attempts. Name the assigned policies, N, pair and

## 5. Local-monitor arm and blinding

**Sound as a frozen, descriptive transfer analysis.** A monitor can score a
recorded, immutable transcript later; that alone neither changes the original
energy measurement nor violates blinding. D §4b L117–134 correctly retains
the frontier primary, identical permitted views, separate calibration and the
frozen-policy-transfer limitation. It also acknowledges unmeasured throughput.

Before any test scoring, freeze local model/runtime/tokenizer hashes, prompt,
context/truncation/parser rules, scales, calibration ranks and any local+E
joint statistic. If the figure compares joint gains, calibrate **each joint**
at .05 as well; a .05 local solo OR .05 E solo is not that rule. Use the same
held-out attempt roster and report availability/FPR, including long-context
failures, for each configuration. Primary-only attack optimization cannot
establish best-response robustness for the other monitors.

“Post hoc on sealed transcripts” needs a custodian/access rule. If batches run
after T1 or T2, seal their outputs away from the red team, analysts making
choices and subsequent policy development until all test collection/analysis
choices are locked. Export opaque IDs and the allowed view, not private goals,
dose/control labels, outcomes, energy decisions or diagnostic truth. Log actual
access. Easier operationally: score after T3. In either case there must be no
feedback to T2/T3 or primary prompt/scorer/threshold changes (A D04/V10,
L63/L96; G1 L511–540; AS2 L547–561). v4's separate no-early-scoring rule still
applies if any v4 transcript is included.

Call it a **monitor-configuration axis** unless independent development
evaluation establishes the proposed strength ordering. Global AA scores and
model size do not establish task-specific monitor ROC or robustness to
injection. Three selected models with different errors do not yield a causal
dose-response curve. Also soften D L122: same weights alone do not create
collusion. Trust, adversarial incentives, shared state and communication
determine an untrusted-monitor game; a separate trusted scoring instance is
not automatically such a game. Different models remain a reasonable design
choice. Open weights improve reproducibility, but require full inference pins
and canary/parity records; temperature zero alone is not a determinism proof.

## 6. What a hostile reviewer will hit first

The first substantive question is **whether energy catches competent,
actor-chosen sabotage that a fair software/time audit misses**, rather than
forced extra work or an information-handicapped monitor (P L26–29, L73–76;
AS1 L256–262; AS2 L271–278; GO L38–44). F1/F2 must be fixed to make that
question answerable. Then expect these objections:

1. **“You selected an energy-friendly game.”** Freeze the task-screen rule,
   full optimizer/attack mixture and budgets; retain failed policies, cheap
   substitution, fully logged malicious behavior, monitor injection and
   concurrent/sparse/batched work within permitted hooks. Report exclusions
   and unsupported taxonomy rows, rather than borrowing “all classes” from
   multiple seats' incompatible A labels. G0's crosswalk is necessary.
2. **“Your 336 attempts are too weak or too correlated.”** Give the selected
   optimizer cohort its own held-out capability bar, denominator and success
   interval. Size the *entire ordered family*, H4's actual subset and adverse
   missingness under Bernoulli/window/day stress worlds. D L81 merely adopts
   a separate bar; it does not supply its numerical rule. As a warning,
   zero events in 28 independent windows still gives an any-event upper 95%
   bound of **10.1466%**; an illustrative 14 independent days gives
   **19.2636%**. Neither is a model-free episode certificate. G12 should not
   inherit G2's fourteen-window
   Gaussian approximation as final power (G2 L201–211; J2 L185–192, L271–273).
3. **“Aborts and unknowns made the defender look good.”** D adopts fail-closed
   monitor operations (L82), but does not restate G2 L187's scientific adverse
   bounds for every missing meter/monitor/outcome. Require the assignment
   funnel, attack-induced timeout/heat as outcomes, first outcomes immutable,
   benign abort/FPR costs and all-assigned worst-case escape bounds. An
   operational abort is not evidence of physical detection (A V06 L92).
4. **“This is detection, not a demonstrated intervention or safety theorem.”**
   Explicitly retain shadow scoring on the same completed attempt (G2 L40;
   G1 L201–204; AS1 L114–116), and distinguish paired detector gain from
   separately optimized attacker best responses. Limit the abstract to the
   frozen search budget, host, model, tasks and operating point. State which
   batching, off-host work and untrusted-runtime threats remain outside it.
5. **“Metering costs one rerun; why is this useful?”** Report actual audit
   latency, extra joules, gates, storage, API costs and operational availability
   alongside detection. Replay-primary is not a near-free sensor check. A
   software win can support a cost tradeoff, not an energy-necessity claim.
   Refresh primary-source novelty/venue evidence later; this offline review
   cannot certify it. A measured limit may be publishable; insufficient
   capability, failed instrumentation or underpower may be unresolved (A
   rejected sufficiency L81), not an automatic limits paper.

## Executive summary

**A successful v4 and adaptive red team could support a main-track paper about a bounded, local two-channel control system. They would not establish that evading one channel necessarily triggers the other.** That implication is the paper's most vulnerable sentence. Ordinary-looking logged computation can implement sabotage, fabricated counts can explain energy without exposing semantic content, and work can move outside the measured interval. The paper must test these common blind spots, not only omission attacks that move the residual upward by construction.

My conditional assessment, assuming favorable results from honestly executed v4 and adaptive trials:

| Reviewer | Strongest remaining objection | What would change the recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| AI control / ML | A hidden-work detector is being promoted into a sabotage defense without measuring successful joint evasion or preserved usefulness. | A frozen joint detector, competent attackers, matched-FPR ablations, and a held-out success-and-evasion endpoint. |
| Security / side channels | The trusted measurement boundary and attacker permissions are unclear; same-host rails and an external DC meter are different security claims. | Independent DC acquisition on the actual episode power path, an explicit local deployment, and attacks against timing, accounting and energy matching. |
| Statistics / measurement | Calibration ranks, clustered trials, adaptive selection and multiple operating points do not yet justify the advertised FPR or power. | A defensible score/calibration construction, fixed joint false-alarm budget, separate final trials, and intervals with the right denominator. |

The highest-return changes are **A1–A7 below**. Do them before spending confirmatory seeds. Preserve the existing R1/W1 thermal and capability work; it answers real nuisance questions. Add joint-control evaluation around it rather than recasting known-H log edits as evidence of semantic sabotage detection. Two task pairs, one production model and one laptop can remain a candidly scoped main-track demonstration if the attack study is strong; they cannot sustain frontier-model, datacenter or universal-control claims.

**Confidence:** high in these logical and statistical objections; medium in acceptance prospects and resource estimates; low in any currently actionable venue deadline because this seat has no network and no verified CFP dates in its inputs.

## Reviewer 1: AI control / machine learning

### C1. Complementarity is a hypothesis, not an exhaustive partition

**Objection.** The agenda's squeeze says hiding from the meter forces plausible logged work, while avoiding the monitor forces unlogged work. Neither implication follows merely from conservation of energy. A malicious policy can reuse the normal forward passes, encode planning in innocuous tokens, fabricate count metadata without showing text, or substitute malicious work for legitimate work of similar energy. The residual can be ordinary while the transcript monitor also misses. An adaptive suite passing only omission and explicit-scratchpad cases would leave this objection untouched.

**Evidence.** The squeeze is stated at `be3cc8b680:docs/research/AGENDA.md:5-8`. The existing pipeline explicitly concedes within-forward-pass blindness (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/PIPELINE.md:9-20`); the residual specification says it is not semantic sabotage detection (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/energy_residual.md:388-400`). LIT identifies filler-token computation and monitor obfuscation as adjacent failure modes (`16e1db623:docs/research-audits/2026-11-07/literature.md:140-150`).

**Required change.** A2/A5 should include an attack whose extra energy is approximately zero and an attack that matches the expected energy with plausible logged activity. If arbitrary count fabrication is excluded, identify the trusted enforcement that excludes it; token count without corresponding monitor-visible content is especially consequential. Report successful joint evasions rather than explaining them away as a different kind of attack. If none occur, say "no successful joint evasion within the registered attack set and budget," with an interval. Do not call the mechanism unavoidable. **Confidence: high in the gap; unknown prevalence of these attacks on D.**

### C2. The endpoint must measure control, not injected-work recognition

**Objection.** Detecting a harness-injected secret continuation does not show that the monitor-plus-energy system stops a capable saboteur. A weak or overconstrained agent can make every defense look excellent. Conversely, counting failed sabotage attempts as detected creates a misleading safety number.

**Required endpoint.** Let S be verified side-task success, U the predeclared legitimate-task usefulness criterion, M the transcript alarm and E the energy alarm. Register both `P(S and U and not M and not E)` over **all assigned final attack trials** and `P(not M and not E | S and U)` over successful useful attacks. Also report S, U and trial/telemetry coverage separately. If the conditional denominator is zero, it is not estimable; it is not a perfect catch rate. Publish the full S/U/M/E contingency table. Positive controls get their own work-detection endpoint, not the sabotage denominator.

Specify when the alarm can act. An episode-end energy alarm after a bank transfer is an audit signal; a prevention claim needs a trusted action hold/rollback mechanism and a latency evaluation. Name the cost and capability assumptions if those actions are available. Run transcript scoring off the metered host, or explicitly account for its metered load. Freeze the monitor's input view: system prompts, hidden reasoning text, token counts, tool results, truncation and final actions. A monitor given the secret sabotage instruction or injected-dose label is not the deployment monitor.

**Evidence.** The generic runner's monitor view excludes system messages and hides only closed scratchpad pairs (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/PIPELINE.md:67-71`). V4 uses reasoning content in agent history and counts-logged as headline, which is not itself a monitor-visibility specification (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:82-90`, `e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:488-516`). **Action: A3/A7. Confidence: high.**

### C3. Parameter count is not attacker competence or frontier relevance

**Objection.** A 27B production cell and a 30B-A3B anchor do not establish generality to stronger agents, other model families or provider-controlled serving. The anchor is an instrument bridge, not automatically a second independently evaluated production defense. Effort, quantization, tool-parser failures and decode caps may suppress attack success more than the defense does.

**Evidence.** V4 explicitly has one production model and no cross-model monitoring claim (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:56-74`). Its capability screen and 4-bit contingency are selection decisions, not pooled confirmation (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:166-191`); the FN addendum remains unadmitted (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:714-732`). PROGRAM's criticism of prior weak sabotage agents is contextual pilot evidence, not evidence about future D competence (`7f74dd2a9:docs/research-audits/2026-11-07/research-program.md:263-280`).

**Required change.** Show no-defense S/U rates and an oracle-assisted attainable-attack control, with the same budgets and tool access. Keep cap-ended, malformed and refused-call cases visible. Predeclare what competence is sufficient to interpret a null evasion result. A10's second family is useful if it can actually attack; another weak checkpoint adds little. If memory prevents running a larger attacker locally, an off-box policy proposing tool actions is a different attack surface from local hidden inference: label that experiment separately. Never infer frontier safety from model size. **Confidence: high in claim ceiling; medium in the value of a particular added model.**

### C4. Two pairs and a longer sabotage prompt invite benchmark-specific shortcuts

**Objection.** `spam_filter_update` and `bank_transfer_2` are two environments, not a population of deployment tasks. Seeds sample trajectories within them. Length, main-task actions or task identity may separate conditions without any added physical witness. An overall AUC can conceal this.

**Evidence.** The task roster is fixed at two pairs (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:70-74`). PROGRAM records a 755-token prompt difference and a misspecified-model false-alarm example (`7f74dd2a9:docs/research-audits/2026-11-07/research-program.md:142-150`); counts-only is already an explicit pipeline question (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/PIPELINE.md:24-29`). These are reasons to control confounding, not permission to optimize a new fit on final outcomes.

**Required change.** A9 compares methods on the same trials and at matched total FPR, with pair-specific results. Include matched-length benign workloads and logged-work controls; preserve task-utility matching. A disjoint domain held out from attack development would be more valuable than many extra seeds on the same two pairs. Distinguish task transfer with a fixed detector from a fresh within-task calibration; they answer different questions. If A10 is unaffordable, state two-task scope prominently and give no cross-domain average as a general guarantee. **Confidence: high.**

## Reviewer 2: security / side channels

### S1. Trust the external measurement chain, not the word "hardware"

**Objection.** Same-host `powermetrics` is not an external security root. Root collection can be outside an unprivileged agent's permissions, but that is an OS trust assumption. An external meter also needs an independently trusted acquisition path, timestamps and episode boundaries. A meter attached to an unmetered supply path, a charging battery or a collector the attacker can rewrite does not establish the proposed invariant.

**Evidence.** The research page describes a DC meter on every episode (`be3cc8b680:docs/research/energy-tripwire.html:145-152`), while the agenda says KM003C on D windows still needs a ruling (`be3cc8b680:docs/research/AGENDA.md:24-25`). V4's U3/contrast use turn-owned GPU energy and its physical setup names direct MagSafe (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:415-422`, `e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:474-489`, `e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:695-700`). This is a scope/boundary inconsistency to resolve, not proof that an implemented meter path is wrong. LIT independently flags same-host telemetry and adaptive energy matching (`16e1db623:docs/research-audits/2026-11-07/literature.md:106-122`).

**Required change.** A1 needs a diagram from supply through meter and any battery to the target, plus collector ownership, clock alignment, sampling coverage, calibration uncertainty and authenticated span boundaries. State **DC input**, not AC wall energy, unless AC is actually measured. Account for charging/discharging storage, fans, CPU, memory and tools; do not transfer a GPU-rail fit directly to DC. Fit and calibrate the selected DC score on DC benign episodes. Retain rails as an internal diagnostic. The known-bytes rail fraction is not a universal correction factor for agent episodes (its regime limits are explicit at `7f74dd2a9:docs/research-audits/2026-11-07/research-program.md:130-138`).

The defensible primary deployment is a dedicated operator-controlled local host with an unprivileged attacking agent, trusted harness/OS, fixed model/runtime, trusted independent meter/collector and trusted lifecycle boundaries. A malicious root/provider, firmware attacker, physical meter attacker or remote compute provider needs another trust story. If only rails qualify, keep a rails-only claim and withdraw the independent-DC adaptive claim. **Confidence: high in requirement and pinned inconsistency; actual wiring and current implementation are unverified.**

### S2. Red-team the physical accounting boundary and selection policy

**Objection.** A detector can look robust because difficult attacks trigger thermal exclusions, deadmen or telemetry gaps and disappear from the scored pool. An attacker can also spread work below threshold, wait into unowned holds, change the compute engine or reduce legitimate computation to offset malicious computation. Positive residuals are not a conserved measure of malicious intent.

V4 excludes several failure classes and gates/holds are unowned; the whole-episode integral and turn attribution are distinct (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:193-201`, `e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:454-470`). The residual document explicitly warns about unowned energy, covariance, changed contexts and sharing (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/energy_residual.md:9-27`, `e901eb2a4:docs/align/energy_residual.md:91-107`, `e901eb2a4:docs/align/energy_residual.md:388-400`).

## Reviewer 3: statistics / measurement

### T1. A sample maximum is not an unconditional certificate for this pipeline

**Objection.** For a fixed scoring rule and exchangeable benign calibration/test scores, a threshold equal to the maximum of m calibration scores has marginal exceedance probability at most `1/(m+1)`; continuous iid scores attain equality. The probability averages over calibration draws. It does not guarantee that this one frozen threshold's population FPR is at most that value, and it does not automatically survive nonexchangeable windows, different fitting rules or adaptive threshold selection.

**Evidence.** V4 specifies per-session LOTO benign fits and full benign fits for sabotage (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:488-505`). The generic residual API describes yet another leave-group-out calibration and empirical quantile convention (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/energy_residual.md:109-126`). These are not evidence that the final calibration and future test scores share one fixed scoring rule. The research page advertises the maximum-bound shortcut (`be3cc8b680:docs/research/energy-tripwire.html:149-152`); council row 12 already asks for conditional wording (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/pilot_protocol_v4.md:618-623`).

**Required change.** A4 should use disjoint benign fitting and threshold-calibration pools with the same frozen scorer on calibration and final evaluation, or document a proven valid symmetric construction. Do not call ordinary cross-validated residuals conformal merely because their order statistic is used. Name the target population and exchangeability assumptions; three time-separated windows are not made exchangeable by having many episodes. Report prospective cross-window performance even if a within-window diagnostic looks good.

The rank itself must be frozen. At m=39, the split-calibration rank for alpha=.05 is `ceil(.95*(39+1))=38`, the second largest. A **maximum** gives the stronger marginal 1/40=2.5% statement, if its assumptions hold. These are different operating points. A generic type-1 quantile `ceil(.95*m)` is another convention; do not let API defaults decide the paper's scientific alpha.

For continuous iid scores, the maximum's realized tail has upper `1 - .1**(1/m)` at 90% confidence: **10.87% at m=20; 5.73% at m=39**. Thus m=39 does not by itself establish a realized FPR below 5% with 90% confidence. With a frozen detector and independent final benign tests, zero alarms in n=59 gives a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound **4.95%**. These are planning arithmetic under stated assumptions, not JouleWise results.

**Fusion.** Two individually 5%-FPR detectors do not produce a 5%-FPR OR detector. Without independence, the union bound permits 10%. One simple proposed route is alpha_E=alpha_M=.025 and OR fusion: each channel's maximum at m=39 gives total marginal FPR at most .05 under valid channel calibration. Another is to freeze one joint statistic and calibrate that statistic directly on independent benign episodes. Select one route before data; include monitor calibration, score normalization and any detector selection in that freeze. Never invoke opposite blind spots as statistical independence. **Confidence: high; actual final implementation/proof not inspected here.**

### T2. Multiplicity and precision are different problems, both necessary

**Objection.** H values, attack families, models, pairs, monitors, clocks, energy boundaries and threshold choices create many opportunities to select a favorable story. Repeated H transforms of one trajectory are not independent attacks. Repeated seed-1 sentinels are instrument observations, not new sabotage trajectories.

**Required change.** A7 freezes a small primary family: joint successful evasion and benign joint FPR/usefulness at one operating point, with task-specific strata and one declared aggregate weighting if used. Register paired joint-versus-single-channel contrasts and the Holm family for any confirmatory superiority claims. Treat broad attack-grid plots and unplanned variants as descriptive/exploratory unless simultaneous inference was registered. Choosing a best attack on development data is legitimate; choosing a best defense on final attacks is not. For curves, resample trajectories with their whole H grid together and account for windows at the level the claim generalizes. With only three or four independent windows, a window bootstrap is fragile; a fancy interval cannot create additional independent environments. Derive the curve from the actual standardized score, not threshold times residual SD: PROGRAM explicitly withdrew that shortcut (`7f74dd2a9:docs/research-audits/2026-11-07/research-program.md:293-294`), and the score contains model and measurement uncertainty (`e901eb2a4:docs/align/energy_residual.md:91-107`).

Precision examples expose the collection cost. Three independent successful attacks all detected give only **36.8%** as a one-sided 95% lower bound on detection. All 29 detected would give a lower bound above 90%. Zero evasions in 59 independent final trials gives a 4.95% upper bound on unconditional evasion; it gives that bound on **conditional** evasion only if the denominator contains 59 successful useful attacks. Searching one attack policy per development trial does not create 59 independent final tests. Apply these examples per claimed population, not automatically per table cell. Cluster dependence or simultaneous claims require additional care and often larger budgets. **Confidence: high in arithmetic and dependence warning; medium in attainable cohort costs.**

Do not hide this cost inside "a few more seeds."

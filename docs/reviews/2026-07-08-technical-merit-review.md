# JouleWise: Technical and Research Merit Assessment

## 1. Verdict

JouleWise is, at full strength, an exceptionally disciplined single-DUT energy-measurement instrument for local LLM inference -- with a machine-checkable audit chain from raw sampler bytes to headline joules, research-grade measurement-window hygiene, and a pre-registered statistical claim-gating stack that appears to be a first for energy benchmarking -- attached to a research program whose one unqualified novelty claim (end-to-end, joule-costed energy of prefill/decode-disaggregated inference across consumer devices) survives an adversarial 11-source related-work audit intact. What it is not, yet, is a benchmark or a body of findings: the measured corpus is six bundles on one machine, every result sits at L1 on the project's own claims ladder, the detection floors that arm the gating machinery are uncalibrated, and the sensors themselves have never been cross-checked against an independent instrument. The engineering rigor currently exceeds the science it has produced. That is the right failure mode at this stage of a program, but merit must be graded accordingly: an outstanding instrument plus a well-prepared, partially de-risked path to a genuinely first-mover result -- not the result itself.

## 2. As a technical feat

**Genuinely distinctive.** The strongest single engineering idea is the strict audit chain: `validate-bundle --strict` re-derives the power trace bit-exactly from retained raw powermetrics plist bytes, re-runs the reduction against stored events, and binds workload identity through domain-separated token-ID hashes cross-checked between manifest, events, and outputs -- all hardware-free, in one command, on immutable no-overwrite bundles with a write-order completion invariant; strict validation is session practice rather than a CI gate, since CI currently runs only non-strict validation on a mock bundle. This is not category-novel: MLPerf Power's submission ecosystem already retains published raw power logs and recomputes headline power from them. The defensible characterization is a strict transplant of consortium-grade audit discipline into a lightweight, open, single-machine tool, with two elements that have no direct precedent -- bit-exact raw-bytes-to-trace re-derivation and token-hash workload binding inside the same audit. It has paid off operationally already: parser bugs were fixed by re-reduction without hardware re-runs.

The second distinctive feat is measured-window discipline. The start marker is stamped only after the first parseable telemetry document (readiness gating), the stop marker the instant the runtime returns, and all controller I/O in the window is deferred to buffer appends, so sampler spawn latency and plist parsing structurally cannot leak into integrated energy (window bounds; sample placement inherits a bounded anchor skew), while the pre-PR#21 corpus carries dict-read-scale in-window overhead judged claim-usable in C-019. Critically, this was earned rather than designed on paper: a 0-byte-plist bring-up failure and a same-day-fixed in-window alignment-capture leak (with regression tests demonstrating 0.84 J -> 38.34 J sensitivity) are both documented. Most published LLM-energy work integrates over wall-clock process boundaries and would silently absorb exactly these errors.

**Well-executed standard practice.** A zero-dependency stdlib core with a single injected clock seam yields 734 deterministic tests in ~20 seconds that drive the real controller lifecycle end-to-end, including dedicated adversarial-audit suites. The failure taxonomy is executed, not aspirational: an exhaustiveness-tested structural-vs-operational status mapping, schema-valid bundle finalization even on injected controller crashes, and a consistent refusal to render undersummed power curves. None of this is novel technique; the consistency is well beyond typical research-harness quality and comparable to good production OSS. Bounds apply: the tested path branches around environment capture under the fake clock, the node worker deliberately lives in a separate clock domain, and the suite establishes logic correctness and fail-closed behavior, not metrological validity.

**Overbuilt.** Less than commonly charged. The "cryptographic DRBG" critique inverts on inspection -- the DRBG is a 27-line primitive with a documented, technically correct rationale (CPython PRNG method-level drift makes stdlib-seeded reproducibility interpreter-dependent), and the gensuite module's bulk is six workload generators that would exist under any PRNG. The true residue is a status fact: the suite's reproducibility machinery has never been exercised by a real-telemetry campaign. Similarly, the provenance superstructure currently serves six real bundles; its payoff is demonstrated in tests and mock gates, not campaigns.

**The honest asymmetry.** Fail-closed discipline is uniform for measurement evidence and run lifecycle (auxiliary metadata is deliberately annotated fail-soft), and not yet at the physics boundary. Idle-subtracted energy passes through unclamped *and unflagged* -- scientifically correct not to clamp, but a contaminated baseline can drive headline metrics negative with no machine-readable warning outside the GPU-visible contamination detector. One known software-side exception belongs here: D-033 documents that `cli.py` skips all workload-provenance checks when `summary_provenance` is absent, so deleting both provenance blocks makes a new bundle validate as legacy; until closed, this is a tamper hole in the otherwise strict audit chain. Controller-as-DUT dynamic overhead rests on a design argument; the empirical SSH-controlled comparison that would bound it is queued but unrun. Powermetrics sample timestamps are systematically late by roughly one full sample interval, recorded as evidence but never corrected. Idle-suspect thresholds are pinned to one fixture. The plist parser, to its credit, is fixture-pinned but fail-closed. These are exactly the seams a strong external engineer would probe first.

Finally, the hardest engineering for the program's distinctive claim is deliberately mostly deferred rather than secretly missing: Phase 3's primary mechanism is sequential offline replay in which prefill and decode are ordinary single-node bundles reusing the existing instrument verbatim; the built-but-mock-tested remote-node substrate (worker protocol, clock-echo alignment with recorded RTT bounds) is genuinely good preparation; and a completed KV-replay feasibility spike (token-identical fresh-process resume on mlx-lm, cache size within +0.018% of the analytic model) partially de-risks the path. The one qualitatively new measurement piece -- the both-end transfer-energy window -- remains unbuilt.

## 3. As a benchmark against the 2026 field

The honest one-sentence pitch: JouleWise is the first LLM-inference energy benchmark in which every published number is mechanically traceable from a hash-pinned, one-command-re-derivable raw-evidence bundle through a pre-registered analysis plan and detection-floor gate to an explicit claim-strength level -- and the first program measuring end-to-end energy of prefill/decode-disaggregated inference on any interconnect.

Each clause of that pitch carries a gap. The evidence-bundle differentiator is implemented and exercised on every recorded run, but its uniqueness is the self-contained, open, per-run re-derivable bundle -- not raw-evidence publication per se, which MLPerf Power already does. The field-unique bundles are currently unpublished local artifacts, so the audit chain's external checkability -- the very thing that serves audience #1 "now" -- has never been exercisable by anyone but the operator. The claim-gating stack (detection floors, MDE arithmetic, claims ladder, pre-registered plans) is plausibly field-first -- no surveyed energy benchmark has it, and the only precedents are accuracy-side -- but its claim gate has never fired on real campaign data: all six floor fields read pending-calibration, and no claim has ever been produced through or blocked by a plan row. It is not pure paper architecture: D-014's collection protocol ran on real hardware with recorded cooldown-gate recoveries, the MDE arithmetic was applied to observed CVs to size campaigns, and the ladder pinned the flagship result at L1. The split-inference claim is verified unoccupied but has zero data.

The largest benchmark-quality gap is accuracy. The three telemetry boundaries use vendor-reported telemetry with uncharacterized absolute error -- one modeled, two sensor-backed but uncalibrated -- and no independent cross-check exists. The confirmed Jetson leg brings the program's first physical shunt measurement, with INA3221 datasheet bounds rather than a pure vendor model. The claims architecture handles this honestly -- no published claim routes through sensor trueness, within-boundary relative comparisons are the primary claim type, and monotone sensor distortion leaves ordinal claims intact -- so the missing calibration caps the claim ceiling rather than invalidating claims made. But the cap is real: absolute joules, cross-boundary winners, and cross-lab comparability are all out of reach, and condition-dependent telemetry bias could in principle contaminate even permitted relative claims undetectably. This is the one thing MLPerf Power's calibrated-analyzer regime delivers that JouleWise's entire rigor stack cannot substitute for -- while MLPerf, conversely, has no instrument at all for the per-rail, per-device, split-stage quantities JouleWise measures. One repo defect follows directly: the related-work draft claims rails are "calibrated against a wall meter where one exists" while the risk register records that none exists; this should be corrected.

Two further product-level facts. JouleWise is today a single-operator research instrument, not an externally runnable community benchmark -- the true gap being comparability rather than runnability on Apple Silicon, and genuine unrunnability on the provisional NVIDIA path -- and the honest publication framing is "benchmark methodology plus reference implementation," which is the project's own recorded position. And the repository has no license file, which legally blocks all external adoption and is the cheapest, highest-leverage fix available.

## 4. Who it serves

Ranked by fit between audience and existing or planned artifacts:

1. **Benchmark methodologists and the reproducibility community** -- served *methodologically now, but not externally checkably yet*. They get the only energy benchmark design with strict re-derivable per-run evidence bundles plus a pre-registered MDE/floor claim-gating protocol; the protocol itself is the citable artifact, and corpus size is not a blocker for this audience. The caveat is material: the field-unique bundles are unpublished local artifacts, so the audit chain has not yet been exercisable by outside readers. The concrete deliverable is the bundle contract as an MLPerf-Power-adjacent artifact-format proposal.
2. **Split/collaborative-inference and energy-aware placement researchers** -- the highest-value audience, served on Phase 3 delivery. They would get the first measured both-end, per-stage energy dataset for disaggregated inference on any interconnect, plus link-transfer joules-per-byte curves. The niche is verified open but plausibly closing: the nearest neighbors are each one increment away, per an assessment-time scan, not independently verified (one measured PD-split energy within a single SoC; one heterogeneous-placement effort lacking a power meter).
3. **Home-cluster and edge-orchestration tooling authors** (exo/prima.cpp lineage) -- served by the Q4 -> placement arc. These systems partition on latency and memory feasibility with zero measured energy input; JouleWise would supply the first energy-placement coefficients. The scope caveat is mandatory: the validated arc addresses *phase* placement (prefill vs decode node), not the layer-split assignment these tools mostly do, so they get a validated model shape and a worked example, not a coverage table.
4. **OSS runtime maintainers** -- a real but narrow capability gap (attach-a-bundle power-regression reports; floor-unit CI thresholds). Serviceable today only for MLX on one pinned host; the CI-gate application is blocked until floors are measured.
5. **Educators and replicators** -- small, immediately servable, near-zero marginal cost: a deterministic no-hardware mock pipeline runs the full config-to-bundle-to-validation loop and doubles as the cross-lab onboarding path.
6. **Same-platform local-LLM deployers** -- plausibly servable near-term. Their per-cell quantity is windowed mJ/token with uncertainty, exactly what JouleWise is built to deliver, and an Apple-Silicon model-by-quant table is attainable with already-landed campaign automation once floors are calibrated.
7. **Cross-device breadth-seeking local-LLM deployers** -- the largest nominal audience and the least served: six bundles, two models, one machine, and a self-imposed leaderboard bar pending cross-lab replication. They remain with Intelligence-per-Watt/Bench360-class tables; JouleWise offers depth-with-uncertainty before it offers broad cross-device coverage.
8. **Carbon-accounting and procurement readers** -- deferred with named gates (wall meter, second unit, cross-lab replication), not excluded by construction; the kill list correctly bars carbon labels and battery estimates today.

One caveat spans all tiers: the audience analysis is credible, costed, and gated -- rare for a benchmark project -- but entirely supply-side. There are zero demonstrated external users, so audience utility is a well-argued forecast whose main proven effect is defensive (the project is unlikely to overclaim utility).

## 5. The research questions

Ranked by scientific value:

1. **Q1 -- the split-energy crossover with joule-costed KV transfer.** Genuinely novel: two independent literature passes fail to find any published end-to-end energy measurement of PD-disaggregated inference across networked consumer devices with transfer costed in joules. Both verdicts are predeclared publishable, and transfer-time arithmetic places 1GbE in the crossover-possible regime. Its promise, stated precisely: *the first both-end-measured, per-stage (prefill/serialize/transfer/deserialize/decode) energy decomposition of disaggregated inference on consumer interconnects, with the crossover prompt length -- where splitting beats monolithic -- either located or excluded within the tested range.* The honest degradations: heterogeneous *measured* (not modeled) answers hang on the pending cross-backend KV-replay verdict; "transfer joules" are boundary-qualified; per-request transfer energies at realistic sizes will be regression-derived from a payload ladder, not directly resolved.
2. **The Q4 -> placement-optimality arc.** A holdout-validated fixed-plus-marginal energy model, composed with measured link-transfer energy to *predict* measured split energy. The standalone fit is methodologically incremental but predictive with predeclared interpolation/extrapolation holdouts, on a device class and whole-device rail boundary the surveyed literature omits; the compositional validation is what elevates Q1 from a point observation to a transferable theory, and it is the program's only L3-templated question. The already-measured near-constant decode power (~23.5 -> 27.5 W across an 81x parameter spread) is exactly the raw material it needs.
3. **The token-shape sufficiency null (AP-4).** The bank's cleanest falsifiable hypothesis: a floor-gated equivalence test with both outcomes predeclared. A holding null licenses shape-matched synthetic energy workloads (stack- and margin-bounded); a failure on the 122B MoE yields an L2 content-changes-joules observation with expert routing as a mechanism *candidate* -- not a mechanism finding, since the harness lacks routing telemetry.
4. **Q6 -- rails-vs-wall boundary bias.** Moderate standalone novelty (RAPL-vs-wall studies exist for CPUs) but the most load-bearing gated question: it is load-bearing for the entire software-counter literature and produces the named calibration bundle that any quantitative cross-boundary split claim requires.
5. **Q2 -- link-speed sensitivity.** The knob that makes Q1's crossover transferable to other people's networks; derivative of Q1's design but decision-relevant.

Honestly routine: **Q5** (rank stability is standard benchmark-robustness work; the MDE-gated "unresolved tie" discipline is the contribution, not the answer), **Q3** (an analysis product of Q1/Q2's dataset, though at consumer links *both* the energy and latency axes of PD disaggregation are uncharacterized -- the correct charge is "no independent experiment," not "latency already done"), and the **affine-ladder energy-per-correct headline**, which is near-mechanical conditional on its envelope gate and on level-invariant per-item energy -- both empirical premises -- rather than scientifically central. The AP-5 C5-1.9 MoE-vs-dense energy-per-correct question is not tautological and should not be collapsed into that verdict. The scientifically interesting endpoint -- level-dependent per-item energy at fixed envelope -- is already the analysis plan's primary but should be the stated hypothesis. The measured 122B-vs-1.5B contrast carries essentially zero standalone weight (four-way confounded, n=3), and the project formally demoted it itself.

## 6. Strongest surviving criticisms

1. **The edifice validates internal consistency, never sensor truth.** Every joule in the current corpus is an unaudited vendor model; strict validation is structurally incapable of detecting systematic rail-model bias. *Defusal:* a wall-meter calibration bundle at a few steady load points (bounding the composite rails-vs-wall gap; the planned Jetson leg is already a physical shunt measurement).
2. **The flagship heterogeneous split claim is presently capped at descriptive language by the project's own ladder** -- no calibration bundle, no seeded analysis-plan row. Narrowed but sharp: same-boundary pairings (Orin<->Orin, GPU<->GPU) already give a calibration-free L2 route, and differential claims (link sensitivity, Pareto) are L2-eligible; the gap is that the *headline* pairing is unnamed and unplanned at AP-row rigor. *Defusal:* seed the AP row naming a same-boundary headline before data collection; land the meter for the cross-boundary form.
3. **The discrete-GPU transfer-energy hole.** On nvidia-smi ends, TCP byte movement is host CPU/NIC/DRAM work the board-power boundary excludes entirely, so measured "transfer energy" there is near-zero net -- a silent undercount in unmeasured silicon, asymmetric across the pairing matrix. *Defusal:* wall-meter the GPU host for transfer legs, or explicitly scope those cells as board-only lower bounds in the stage accounting before collection.
4. **Zero findings above L1 as of today.** Six bundles versus hundreds of pages of gating machinery; the full bundle->plan->floor->ladder chain has never run end-to-end on real data. The sequencing is methodologically correct (floors before sized campaigns), and the program caught two contamination events -- one by human review before the gate existed, one by the gate's first true positive -- but all research merit is currently design merit, concentrated in a Phase 3 + Q4 bet with a thin fallback. *Defusal:* run the floor calibration and push one campaign through the entire chain.
5. **Single-specimen transportability.** Absolute levels and fitted coefficients are properties of individual silicon units; sub-few-percent resolved effects have unestablished cross-unit transportability. Narrowed -- paired within-unit comparisons cancel unit variance, headline effects dwarf plausible binning variance, and the planned Orin<->Orin pairing implies two units of one class. *Defusal:* measure same-class unit-to-unit CV against the floor, a one-day campaign.
6. **Under-cited deployment motivation for the split scenario.** No deployed hybrid/edge split system is cited anywhere; disaggregation economics is argued only from datacenter throughput. Not disqualifying for a prescriptive design-space measurement, but it narrows who can act on the answer. *Defusal:* one motivating paragraph grounded in a real hybrid-edge pattern.

A seventh, lower-grade item: the floors-and-ladder discipline may render much of the fine-grained suite agenda "not resolvable" at 1-10 Hz sampling -- a scope reducer, not a program killer; the fix is pruning the question bank against measured floors before investing in suite campaigns.

## 7. Bottom line

JouleWise is an instrument-and-methodology contribution of genuine quality with one verified-unoccupied research target of real value. The audit chain, window discipline, and claim-gating stack together constitute the most rigorous measurement-integrity design in its class, and the program's two contamination catches -- one by human review before the gate existed, one by the gate's first true positive -- plus the formal self-demotion of its most quotable number show the discipline is operational, not performative. Against that: the sensors are unverified, the corpus is a demo, the claim gate has never fired beyond partial exercise on protocol, MDE arithmetic, and ladder classification, the flagship claim is currently forbidden in its strongest form by the project's own rules, and there is a concrete physical hole in the planned transfer-energy accounting on discrete-GPU ends. Published today it would be an excellent methods paper with a demonstration corpus. The distance to the strong version is short and fully enumerated by the project itself -- a wall meter, a seeded split analysis-plan row, a host-side boundary fix, floor calibration, and one campaign through the full chain -- after which the split corpus would be a first-of-kind dataset with an audit trail no peer can match. The program's merit is real; almost all of it is still ahead of the data.

## Debate adjudication and binding outcomes (C-020)

This assessment was debated by three independent poles — the session-lead
Fable position, a fresh Fable reviewer with no session context, and the
69-agent Codex stack above — with a recorded cross-examination round.
Full record: council log C-020. Adjudicated outcomes:

- **Program thesis (D-048):** "JouleWise builds auditable per-stage
  split-inference energy bundles, then tests whether a pre-registered
  compositional model predicts them." Model-first framing, dataset-first
  contribution. Pre-registered predicted split-energy curves per
  pairing/link are seeded in an analysis-plan row before any split
  hardware runs; a no-crossover verdict is publishable only as
  successful prediction or quantified unmodeled-overhead discovery. The
  debate's transfer arithmetic (56 MiB KV @ 2048 tokens → ~0.5 s on
  1GbE; second-device overhead 2.5–25 J) shows the crossover is
  possible but not uniform — which is precisely why the model, not the
  sweep, is the thesis.
- **Transfer-boundary accounting (D-049):** discrete-GPU transfer cells
  are host-inclusive (wall/host-metered) or explicitly labeled
  board-only lower bounds — never silently undercounted.
- **Question ranking (adjudicated):** 1. the coupled Q4→Q1 compositional
  prediction + first-of-kind per-stage dataset; 2. the Token-Shape
  Sufficiency Null (sustained: either outcome travels to every
  shape-matched benchmark); 3. Q6 boundary-directional bias (elevated:
  the most citable result for other benchmark authors); 4.
  active-parameter scaling; the affine ladder is reclassified as a
  suite-validity instrument (C5-1.9 exempt); Q5 last.
- **Claim-ceiling moves, in leverage order:** publish a bundle pack and
  obtain one external strict re-reduction (auditability is an L0 claim
  until an outsider exercises it); USB-C PD / wall cross-check;
  same-class unit-to-unit CV. Repo gaps surfaced: no LICENSE file
  (blocks adoption; owner decision), D-033 strict-validation legacy
  bypass (queued as P2-026).

Dissents preserved in C-020: the fresh-Fable reviewer maintains full
spine inversion (model as thesis outright); its omission of the
Token-Shape Null from the top tier was overruled; the original Codex

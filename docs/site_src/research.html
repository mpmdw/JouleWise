01
Why this is hard

The model does not come with an energy return value.

A profiler can tell you which function consumed CPU time. Energy is a physical quantity observed through a separate instrument. You have to align two clocks, define a boundary, and decide what else was happening while the model ran.

Boundary

Which power?

SoC rails omit parts of the whole machine. Wall power includes more than the chip. Neither is automatically wrong; an unlabeled boundary is.

Attribution

Which interval?

Load the model too late or stop sampling too early and the number answers a different question than the experiment config asked.

Workload

Which work?

Prompt length, generated tokens, batching, caching, stop behavior, and content can all change the physical execution.

Noise

Which difference?

Background activity, thermal state, sampling cadence, clock error, and run order can manufacture a small “effect.”

The measurement-science move: replace “I got a number” with “I defined a quantity, captured the evidence, bounded what could imitate it, and stated the claim ceiling.”
02
Watts, joules, and idle

Power is a rate. Energy is the area.

A watt is one joule per second. If power changes over time, energy is the integral under the power trace. That identity is a unit definition—not a JouleWise measurement result.

instantaneous powerP(t)watts = joules / second
gross measured energyE = ∫ P(t) dtheadline basis within the named boundary
idle-subtracted energyEsecondary = ∫ (P(t) − Pidle) dtwithin-device secondary view only

Idle is not “free energy to erase.” It is an observed baseline under a stated condition. Subtracting it can help describe activity above baseline on one device, but it can distort comparisons: a high-idle device gets a larger subtraction. JouleWise therefore uses gross energy for every cross-device, cross-configuration, and split-vs-monolithic headline. Phase energy is gross-only.

How powermetrics sampling works

You never see the continuous curve.

powermetrics reports discrete samples from named Apple power rails. JouleWise aligns those samples with runtime events and integrates between explicit measured-window boundaries. Detection floors

How small is too small to resolve?

Repeat the same condition and the measured energy moves anyway. The false-effect guard floor summarizes how large that repeat scatter—or an ABBA comparison artifact—can look. An effect below the applicable floor is not resolvable, not proven absent.

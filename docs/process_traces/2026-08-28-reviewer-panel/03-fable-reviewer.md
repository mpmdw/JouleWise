## 1. Summary of contribution

The paper argues that in phase-resolved energy measurement of LLM inference on Apple Silicon (M3 Max, MLX, `powermetrics`), the dominant uncertainty in per-phase energy is not run-to-run scatter but the placement of the prefill/decode boundary on the sampled power trace: a boundary displaced by tens of milliseconds across a step of tens of watts moves about a joule between phases, and repetition cannot average that away. To make this decidable the authors (i) calibrate boundary timing in-session with a 59-pulse commanded GPU train bracketed before and after each measurement window, using a rate-aware set-membership clock anchor and a branch-and-bound accepted-region fit that yields a worst-edge timing bound (~30 ms on the shown capture); (ii) propagate that bound through the energy integral into a per-cell "resolution bound" (detection floor) composed from an absolute and a comparative (A/B/B/A) component, corner-enumerated over all interval endpoints, guarded for small n, plus a whole-window drift allowance; (iii) gate any directional claim on two separate tests (magnitude above floor; interval wholly on the registered side, Holm-corrected) with printed refusals otherwise; and (iv) pre-register a 7B-vs-1.5B Qwen2.5 contrast as a demonstration of the gates. The pre-registered primary finding is "attribution dominance": that the timing-widened floor exceeds the point-only floor in each phase cell. The paper is candid that the pulse-to-inference transfer of the timing bound is untested, that the evidence archive is unreleased, and that an internal floor-binding limitation (L1) currently prevents independent re-reduction.

## 2. Strengths

- **The forcing problem is real, physical, and under-served.** "Power × misplaced time" as a systematic that repetition cannot remove is exactly the kind of instrument-level insight this venue exists to publish, and the paper places it correctly relative to the counter-gain literature (Khan, Jay) and the RAPL edge-alignment work (Hähnel). The "gain axis vs time axis" framing in §8 is the clearest statement of novelty in the paper.
- **Appendix A.3 is genuinely replicable.** The clock-anchor estimator (affine model, stamp/native-label/causal constraints, Fourier–Motzkin elimination of α, exact LP, outward rounding, four named bound terms) and the pulse fit (Huber loss, coordinate descent on explicit grids, rigorous cell lower bound for branch-and-bound, stamp-widening) are specified to the level a reader could re-implement. I checked the worked arithmetic: the anchor-term sum (0.00068692 + 0.00044608 + 1e-6 + 1e-6 = 0.0011349972 s), the capture-minus-anchor subtraction (0.0289329 s), the tenth-pulse schedule offset (9 × 1.0 s + Σ gaps 17.625 s = 26.625 s from the vdC₂ sequence), and the 0.95⁵⁹ ≈ 0.048 "95/95" statement all close.
- **Systematic uncertainty is treated as bounded, not as noise.** Corner enumeration over all 2ⁿ endpoint choices, refusal above n = 16 rather than approximation, and the explicit refusal to combine edge uncertainty in quadrature (§8, Zhuang discussion) are the right metrological posture, and the §4 fixture arithmetic (1.5 × 1.6656 + 0.4 = 2.8984 J; 1.5 × 1.7656 + 0.4 = 3.0484 J) checks — I recomputed the corner [−.5,−.5,−.5,.5,.5]: max|rᵢ| = 0.6, sᵣ = √0.3, t₀.₉₇₅,₄ · sᵣ · √1.2 = 1.6656.
- **Fail-closed design with named refusals is stated concretely** (§5): quantitative admission predicates (p95 CPU busy ≤ 0.5, p95 package power ≤ 1.0 W, GPU idle ratio rule, adapter-watt invariance), append-only replacement records, third-same-cause window closure. The "REFUSED is a result" position (§7) is argued rather than asserted.
- **Honest limitation ordering.** Limitation 1 (load-regime transfer) is stated in the abstract, §2, §4, §6, §7, §10 and Appendix A.6, and the proposed test (inserted-gap fiducial) is the correct one. Barring the 748 retired-anchor bundles from claims rather than reprocessing them is the right call and is explained.
- **The negative result is a real, mechanical result.** The resolvability rule (≥ 3 overlapping records) with the "2 < 3 despite 0.121 s > one record width" trap is exactly the kind of counter-intuitive edge case reviewers who run `powermetrics` will recognise.

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked by score impact)

### W1. The pre-registered falsifier is much weaker than the word "dominates," so the "prospective null" outcome is not realistically reachable and the both-outcomes framing over-promises.

*Where:* §1 "The finding is falsified for a phase if the timing-widened bound does not exceed the point-only repeatability bound"; §4 "A component is labelled *attribution-limited* only when … the exact linear corner maximum used by the code's predicate strictly exceeds the guarded point-only value."

*Why it matters:* I read `joulewise/detection_floor.py:806-843`. For the absolute component the predicate computes `max_i(|r_i| + h_i(n−1)/n + Σ_{k≠i}h_k/n)` and compares it with the guarded point floor `g(n)·max(max|r_i|, t·s_r·√(1+1/n))`. With n = 10 (g = 1) and equal half-widths h, this reduces to: pass iff `1.8h > (point_gate − max|r_i|)`. Whenever the max-residual term wins the point guard (which it does whenever one run is an outlier), the right-hand side is zero and any positive h passes. Even when the t-guard wins, the bar is "timing widening exceeds the t-guard's excess over the largest residual" — typically a few tenths of a joule. With h ≈ 30 ms × 40 W ≈ 1.2 J and diagnostic scatter ≈ 0.3–0.5 J, the diagnostic ratios of 5.9–10.9 sit an order of magnitude above the boundary. "Dominance" in the plain sense — the timing contribution exceeds the scatter contribution, i.e. `U_* − U_point > U_point` or ratio > 2 — is what the abstract, §7 and the ratios imply, but not what is registered. A reader will conclude, correctly, that the primary finding is a near-certain pass, and the elaborate "one-phase failure narrows / both-phase failure yields a prospective null" apparatus in §1 and §10 is largely decorative. The actual falsifiable content of the paper is elsewhere (the null-containment test of the floor, and the two demonstration gates), and the paper should say so.

*Fixable at the desk:* yes, in two ways — either (a) state the registered predicate exactly as coded, name it as the *label* predicate rather than a finding, and drop "dominance" language in favour of the ratio (which is what §7 already reports); or (b) register a second, stronger prospective statement (ratio ≥ 2, or timing term ≥ point term) as the *headline* finding, marked clearly as added after the freeze and therefore reported as exploratory. Do not silently re-word the frozen predicate.

### W2. The calibration bound conflates GPU dispatch/ramp latency with sampler timing error, and the paper treats the systematic part as a symmetric bound rather than a correctable bias — which bears directly on the transfer assumption.

*Where:* §2 worked example: "onset lag anywhere in [0.0254, 0.0289] s … offset lag anywhere in [−0.0086, −0.0053] s"; A.3.5 pulse 0: "the instrument reported this pulse starting about 16 ms late and ending about 11 ms early, and the data rule out any onset outside a 2.3 ms-wide band."

*Why it matters:* The two examples show onsets consistently 15–29 ms *late* and offsets consistently 5–11 ms *early*: the observed plateau is ~25–35 ms *shorter* than commanded, with a per-pulse accepted-region width of only ~2–3 ms. A pure sampler time-base error would displace both edges in the same direction. Opposite-sign lags of this shape are the signature of GPU work-start latency after the command stamp (dispatch, clock ramp) and of the last kernel's completion preceding the `mx.eval` return — i.e. properties of the *workload's* power response, not of `powermetrics`. The paper therefore (i) publishes a bound (~30 ms) that is dominated by a repeatable bias whose residual scatter is an order of magnitude smaller, and (ii) transports a *GPU-matmul* ramp latency to *inference phase boundaries* whose runtime events sit at different distances from their physical power transitions. This is the concrete mechanism by which the transfer assumption can fail, and the paper never names it; it names only "sustained mixed inference load." It also means the ~1 J scale in the abstract is a scale for *bias*, and the achievable resolution after bias characterisation may be several times finer. Note the sign matters for the science question: a consistent late-onset bias moves energy *into* the preceding phase for every run, which affects the phase *estimate*, not only its interval.

*Fixable at the desk (partially):* yes — report the 59-pulse distribution of onset and offset lags (median, IQR, sign) from the retained capture, discuss why the sign pattern arises, and state explicitly that the bound is worst-excursion-including-bias by design. Reducing the bound by bias correction would need a new measurement and a new registration; do not do that for `_v4`.

### W3. The paper is not readable by its target audience as written: internal identifiers, editorial markers and process language leak into the frozen text.

*Where:* §3 "The frozen P06 result specification … D-152 supplies the ruled values"; §6 "[[NEEDS-VALUE: exact cell-floor F, claim-side bound B … checked D-122, D-139 A2, the prefill-feasibility synthesis and consult, and the current gamma manifest]]"; Table 2 caption "[[NEEDS-VALUE: D-123 producing schema …]]"; §7 "C5-1.3"; §9 "FLOOR-BIND-01"; §4 "row DS-29"; §4 "`calibration_acceptance_d079_v2_n17_r3` through `_r6`"; §4 "A superseded ruling records a larger figure, derived when the calibration corpus held nineteen members rather than seventeen; that discrepancy is registered … its reconciliation is pending."

*Why it matters:* A PC reviewer cannot resolve D-152 or DS-29 and should not have to; each such token is a claim the reader is asked to take on faith. The 9.724 ms vs "superseded larger figure" passage is worse: it tells the reader an internal inconsistency exists and is unreconciled, without giving the other number or the consequence. The §6 sizing paragraph is the only place a reader learns the design's power argument, and it is an unfilled editorial note. Two separate placeholders in §6 (record width, record spacing for `p2015-df-ph-decode-abs-r03`) are labelled DIAGNOSTIC-ERA VALUE, i.e. available today from a retained bundle — leaving them pending in a frozen draft is a process defect, not a design one.

*Fixable at the desk:* entirely. Replace every decision ID with the ruled value and a one-clause rationale; fill the two diagnostic-era values; either reconcile the 9.724 ms figure or delete the sentence about the superseded ruling and put it in the artifact guide.

### W4. The floor's own containment test is under-powered, and the composition is asymmetric in a way the paper does not justify.

*Where:* §3 Table 1, identical-condition null: "Earlier-floor branch: five blocks each, n = 15"; §4 step 4 "g(n) = max(1, √(9/(n−1))), n ≥ 5"; §4 step 6 "Compose by maximum."

*Why it matters:* The floor is described as "the largest false difference this measurement system can manufacture," yet the only test of that property is whether five identical-condition blocks per magnitude fall inside it. A floor built as `g(n)·max(max|r|, t-prediction guard) + drift`, corner-widened, will contain five fresh draws almost by construction; passing carries little evidential weight, and the paper's own framing ("Failure to reject zero is insufficient") acknowledges this without quantifying the test's power. The guard g(n) = √(9/(n−1)) is stated without derivation — why 9, why √, why it stops at n = 10 — and the reader cannot judge whether it is conservative. Composition by max of absolute and comparative components is asserted; since the demonstration is an A/B/B/A contrast, why the absolute component enters a *contrast* gate at all needs one sentence. Finally the 10.164835 ms bracket refusal threshold appears with no derivation and is a different number from the 9.724 ms screen; a reader sees two unexplained millisecond constants two sentences apart.

*Fixable at the desk:* mostly — derive or cite g(n); explain why the two constants differ and where each comes from; state the null test's power in terms of the ratio floor/scatter (e.g. "with observed scatter s and floor F ≈ 6s, the probability that five blocks exceed F under the null is …"). More null blocks would need measurement.

### W5. Only the GPU channel is calibrated; CPU and ANE channels are integrated per phase without any edge characterisation.

*Where:* §2 "integrates the CPU, GPU, and neural-engine interval-average power inside each phase"; A.3.1 "The pulse fit itself never uses p_i; it reads only the `gpu_power` channel."

*Why it matters:* If the three channels are sampled and averaged on a common window there is no problem, but the paper does not say so and the record health-check in A.3.1 (power × elapsed vs energy counter within 2 mJ + 0.1 %) tests *consistency*, not *alignment*. Prefill on MLX is GPU-dominated so the practical exposure is probably small, but "probably small" is a claim the paper should make and bound, not leave implicit.

*Fixable at the desk:* one paragraph plus, ideally, the cross-channel edge offset on the retained capture (the CPU channel also rises when the driver dispatches work).

### W6. Length and structure: at ~17.6k words with a 7,000-word appendix, a 300-word figure description standing in for each figure, and repeated sentences, the paper is roughly twice the length a reviewer will read carefully.

*Where:* the sentence "the cell's resolution bound — the artifact calls it the detection floor — 'the largest false difference this measurement system can manufacture'" appears verbatim in the abstract, §1, §6, and §7; §2 and §4 each contain a full prose walk of a figure that is then repeated in the caption; the "load-regime transfer is an assumption, not a result" sentence appears seven times.

*Why it matters:* Repetition reads as insecurity, not rigour, and it crowds out the things a reviewer actually wants (W2's lag distribution, W4's power argument, the sizing paragraph). A.3 is excellent but belongs in an artifact appendix or companion technical report; the main text should carry the model, the four anchor terms, and the accepted-region idea in a page.

*Fixable at the desk:* yes; this is editing.

### W7. Evidence is unreleased and the paper says it is not independently re-reducible; the demonstration therefore rests on the authors' custody.

*Where:* §9 "The registered L1 floor-binding limitation prevents describing the present chain as independently re-reducible"; A.6 "the evidence-dependent commands above cannot support a claim of open, independent re-reducibility."

*Why it matters:* For a metrology paper whose thesis is auditability, this is a material gap; I do not score it heavily because it is candidly stated and is a release-checklist item, but a camera-ready that still carries the L1 sentence would be a different paper.

*Fixable:* at the desk for release; closing L1 is engineering.

## 5. Questions for the authors

1. Under the coded predicate, what pair (scatter s, half-width h) would produce a *false* attribution-limited label for n = 10? Has any retained cell with positive h ever returned false other than by forcing h = 0?
2. What are the median and range of the 59 onset lags and 59 offset lags in the retained capture? Is the late-onset/early-offset pattern present in every accepted capture, and with what capture-to-capture variation? That variation, not the ~30 ms excursion, is the quantity that would transfer to inference if the bias is workload-specific.
3. Does the MLX runtime emit the prefill-end / decode-start events before or after the corresponding GPU command-buffer completion, and by how much on this stack? If unknown, the transfer assumption is currently unbounded in *both* directions.
4. Why is the whole-window drift allowance A_k added as a joule term to the *floor* rather than treated as an interval on the contrast, given that the A/B/B/A order already cancels linear drift? Isn't this double-counting for the comparative component?
5. Are the CPU, GPU and ANE `powermetrics` fields averaged over the same `elapsed_ns` window, and has the CPU-channel edge been compared to the GPU-channel edge on the pulse capture?
6. The requested cadence is 100 ms but e₀ = 111.2 ms and the text says records are spaced further apart than their width. What is the distribution of record widths and gaps across a window, and does the gap time (unsampled) enter the energy integral as zero, as interpolation, or as refusal?
7. With g(10) = 1 and corner enumeration over 2¹⁰ endpoints, what fraction of F_cell in the diagnostic cells came from the t-guard, the corner widening, and A_k respectively? The paper asks for this decomposition (§4 "reported separately") but never shows one.
8. Is the ~5 J planning figure in §6 F + B with B ≈ 0, or was B estimated? If the claim-side bound's supplier is unbuilt, how did it enter a decision?

## 6. Score

**Score now: 3 (borderline).**

The paper's instrument-level insight is correct, important, and well positioned against the literature; the calibration and bound machinery in Appendix A is specified at a level few systems papers reach, and its arithmetic checks. Against that, the pre-registered primary finding is a near-certain pass under the predicate actually coded (W1), so the paper's claim to be a falsifiable prospective test is weaker than presented; the calibration bound is visibly dominated by a repeatable, opposite-sign edge bias that the paper does not name and that is the concrete route by which its central untested assumption can fail (W2); and the frozen text is not yet readable by an external reviewer (W3, W6). W1–W3 are all desk-fixable, and a revision that addressed them would move me up independent of `_v4`.

**If `_v4` reproduces "dominance" (the label predicate passes in both phases, the null test contains, and at least one demonstration gate resolves): 4 (accept)**, conditional on the desk fixes for W1–W3 — the ratios rather than the predicate would then carry the message, and the demonstration would show the gates doing real work in both directions.

**If `_v4` does not reproduce (either phase fails the predicate, or the null test withdraws a floor): 3 (borderline)**, and 2 without the desk fixes. A null under this predicate would be scientifically *more* interesting than a pass (it would mean scatter has grown to the joule scale, which is itself a finding about the instrument), but the paper as written has no section prepared to interpret it; the "null-outcome" title suggests a retreat to "we corrected a clock error," which is not a contribution at venue scale. A refused demonstration contrast alone would not lower the score — the paper argues convincingly that refusal is a result — provided the floor and null test issue.

## 7. What would make this paper impressive rather than merely sound

**(a) Desk work only.** Publish the *bias/scatter decomposition of the calibration bound*: the distribution of the 59 onset lags and 59 offset lags across every accepted capture (median, IQR, capture-to-capture variation of the median), and the corresponding decomposition of the diagnostic cells' F_cell into t-guard, corner-widening and drift terms. This costs nothing — the bytes are retained and A.3 already re-derives them — and it does three things at once: it converts W2 from a hidden problem into a stated result ("the sampler is late by X ± Y ms, repeatably"), it gives the transfer assumption a quantitative shape a reader can reason about, and it shows the reader how much of the ~1 J is correctable in principle versus irreducible. A metrology audience will find "30 ms, of which 27 ms is a stable bias and 3 ms is the accepted-region width" far more impressive than "30 ms."

**(b) One more measurement week, ranked by value per night:**

1. **Inserted-gap fiducial (my pick).** ~10 real-workload runs with a commanded ~500 ms sleep between prefill end and decode start, edges fitted with the existing estimator, residual compared to the pulse-derived bound. It is the only proposal that tests the assumption every claim in the paper rests on, it needs one night, it reuses the existing estimator without new code paths, and *either* outcome is publishable: agreement licenses the transfer; disagreement quantifies the workload-specific latency (Q3) and turns Limitation 1 into a measured correction. Its value is bounded only by the caveat that a 500 ms sleep produces a power *dip*, not the prefill→decode *step*, so the fitted edges are gap edges, not the phase boundary itself — say so in the design and fit both the fall and the rise.
2. **Cross-session floor stability** (the §7 "1.25× corridor" test, ≥ 3 sessions). Second because it converts the floor from a per-session number into a property of the instrument and directly supports the reuse rule the paper already registers; two to three nights.
3. **Micro-delta challenge** (planted 0.5×/1.0×/1.5×/3× floor differences). Third — it tests the gates' sensitivity, which is the same logic as the null test with the sign flipped, and is largely predictable from the floor arithmetic.
4. **`_v5` model ladder.** Last. More sizes on the same protocol makes a better *systems* paper but does not strengthen the *metrology* claim, and the paper is explicit (§10) that the size comparison "is not a model-size scaling law." Spend the night on the assumption, not the demonstration.

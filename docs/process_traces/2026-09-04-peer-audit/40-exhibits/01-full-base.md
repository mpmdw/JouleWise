### F1 — BLOCKER: a timing envelope over interval averages is not an envelope over physical phase energy

**What is wrong.** Current interval-support reduction multiplies each record's reported average by its overlap with the phase (`joulewise/reduce.py:167`). For these records both interpolation-bound helpers return zero (`joulewise/reduce.py:519`, `joulewise/reduce.py:554`). The current corner envelope moves the start and end around the same reconstructed power function (`joulewise/reduce.py:2148`, `joulewise/reduce.py:2225`). This accounts for uncertain timing **conditional on the reconstruction**; it does not account for unknown power variation inside a record that crosses a phase boundary.

V2 executes an explicit counterexample using the current production math helpers:

```text
record_averages_w = [50.0, 50.0, 50.0, 50.0, 50.0, 50.0]
reported_phase_j = 20.0
interpolation_bounds_j = (0.0, 0.0)
timing_envelope_j = (17.0, 23.0)
compatible_physical_phase_j = (15.0, 25.0); each boundary record contains 0 W for half and 100 W for half
WITNESS PASS: identical interval averages do not identify physical phase energy
```

The records are 100 ms wide; the phase is [0.05, 0.45] s. Three complete interior records supply 15 J. Each half-record edge can supply 0 or 5 J with the same reported average by reversing the order of its 0 W/100 W halves. The reducer assigns 2.5 J to each. A 1 ms common bound and 30 ms independent edge bounds yield 17–23 J, excluding the compatible 15/25 J histories. Five records overlap; sample-count sufficiency does not resolve the ambiguity. Yet the code's phase-identifiability label uses that test (`joulewise/reduce.py:3000`).

This synthetic witness is not an accepted live bundle or evidence of any retained result's actual error. It establishes an unmodelled uncertainty dimension. The draft states the held-average reconstruction (`docs/paper/draft-v2-skeleton.md:633`) without establishing zero allocation error.

**What I would do differently.** Choose with the advisor between (a) explicitly reporting *interval-average allocated phase energy* and its conditional timing sensitivity, or (b) adding a defensible within-record allocation uncertainty model, with stated physical constraints and evidence. A simple nonnegative-power allocation bound is a useful independent diagnostic before designing a fancier estimator. Retain gross whole-record/request evidence where its own boundary conditions are satisfied.

**STOP:** calling record-count sufficiency “identifiability,” or treating exact reconstruction arithmetic as an exact physical uncertainty bound. Do not respond with more hashes or more tests of the existing piecewise-constant model.

**Acceptance / cost:** the V2 witness is either enclosed by the declared physical uncertainty model, or explicitly excluded by the named estimand throughout abstract, methods, results and captions. Check multiple record cadences and phase-edge offsets against known sub-record histories. About half a day to decide and relabel; a validated physical correction could exceed this week's budget.

### F6 — SHOULD_FIX: separate a conditional worst-case sensitivity result from an empirical error or coverage claim

**What is wrong.** The repaired dominance criterion is **not tautological**: its threshold is 2, equality passes, and zero denominator is a named refusal (`joulewise/dominance_closeout.py:36`). The numerator is nevertheless the maximum of a chosen floor functional over an interval box, computed at all corners (`joulewise/detection_floor.py:861`). It measures sensitivity to the allowed uncertainty model. It does not observe the actual error incurred by a phase, and a larger or more conservative allowed set can enlarge the numerator with unchanged recorded measurements.

The base functional takes the maximum of observed absolute deviations and a Student-t prediction expression (`joulewise/detection_floor.py:691`); the extra small-n factor is an operational rule (`joulewise/detection_floor.py:666`). These choices are not a deterministic upper bound on what future measurements can produce. The draft's Appendix says the pulse sample maximum has no out-of-sample coverage guarantee (`docs/paper/draft-v2-skeleton.md:1666`), but the README's definition calls a detection floor “the largest false difference the admitted measurement system can produce” (`README.md:3`). The draft's more careful “allowed by the fixed calculation” at `docs/paper/draft-v2-skeleton.md:107` should govern everywhere.

**What I would do differently.** Make the claim explicitly about a *registered, conditional sensitivity calculation*. Present point repeatability, reconstruction/timing assumptions, and worst-case widening separately. Use the existing dependence-sensitivity analysis and retained ordered blocks to show how conclusions change under plausible correlation assumptions; the draft itself identifies the independent-block and alternative models (`docs/paper/draft-v2-skeleton.md:1237`). If actual error dominance is desired, obtain independent physical/reference evidence. Authentication cannot substitute for that evidence.

**STOP:** upgrading “our conservative calculation doubles” into “the physical error is dominated by timing,” “largest possible error,” a detection-power guarantee, or universal advice without its conditions.

**Acceptance / cost:** a reviewer can state what was measured, what was assumed, what was maximized, and what probability claim—if any—was justified. No tuning of the registered threshold or allowed width after outcomes. Mostly a half-day writing/analysis task once F1/F2 are settled.

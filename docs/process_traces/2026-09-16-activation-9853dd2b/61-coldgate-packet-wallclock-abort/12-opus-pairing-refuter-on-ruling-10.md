## BLOCKER-GRADE GAP (stated first)

**The ruling's Q1 shape can produce the exact harm the lane exists to prevent.** It orders one shared `CustodyDeadline(120 s)` threaded into `calibration_session_status`, and asserts "worst-case abort ≈ 120 s + lease/repair overhead (seconds)." It never asks whether twelve reads *fit* in 120 s. They are not certified to. The install-time headroom gate (`joulewise/night_agent_install.py:834`) admits a night when `elapsed × WRITER_CUSTODY_PASSES(3) × 1.5 ≤ budget` — i.e. it admits a **single measured custody pass of up to 26.67 s** against a 120 s budget (its own comment, `:685`). The abort performs **twelve** sequential slot reads, each spawning a fresh `python -B -m joulewise.calibration_custody_worker` (`joulewise/calibration_ledger.py:2188-2193`). On a legitimately-admitted-but-slow custody root (Ed's iCloud-offload directive makes a synced root live; exhibit C §4), 12 × even 12 s exceeds 120 s, and the shared deadline then **refuses the abort mid-pass** — leaving the session OPEN under a live `CalibrationWriterLease` with desk recovery blocked by `LIVE_WRITER_CONTENTION`. That is option (a)'s terminal harm, reached through the "cure."

**The cure the ruling missed:** the abort does not need twelve reads. `abort_calibration_session` (`joulewise/calibration_ledger.py:6128-6158`) consumes from the status only `session_state`, `terminal_head_pin_candidate`, `pin_relation`, `next_slot`, and `slots[next_slot]["custody_state"]`. The twelve reads exist because the locator is pre-declared for **every** declared slot in the open receipt (`:5567-5582`), so `_custody_state` runs 12× and **eleven results are discarded**. Read only `next_slot`: worst case becomes 1 × 120 s — inside what the installer already certifies at 3× headroom — with no new budget-sizing question, no shared-deadline arithmetic, and no mode flip. This is design-bearing (it changes what the abort verifies) and belongs in the ruling, not in the implementer's lap.

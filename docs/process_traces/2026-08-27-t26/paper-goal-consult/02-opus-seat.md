**2. SCIENCE vs the closest work.**
- **JouleSort [3]** named this failure qualitatively (it rejected a fixed-energy metric partly over meter/run synchronization) and never quantified it. *Adds:* a measured bound at phase granularity. *Fails:* JouleSort's own boundary discipline — everything at the wall — is unmet; state that as scope, don't defend it.
- **SPEC Power methodology [2] / MLPerf Power [1].** *Adds:* ports reject-on-missing-evidence to a consumer counter and replaces "analyzer specification" with an in-session measured bound. *Fails:* no traceability, one unit, no public-run discipline.

JouleWise opens the *time* axis they only touch via whole-workload lag alignment. *Fails:* absolute joules here are entirely unvalidated — complementary, not superior, and the paper should say so first, not defensively.
- **Dauner et al. [23].** The strongest independent corroboration you have: counter update granularity silently biases integrals. *Adds:* turns that caveat into a per-phase resolvability gate. *Fails:* they had a reference meter; you don't.
- **The Illusion of Power Capping [20].** Your closest methodological rival — phase-aware, repeated, cross-checked against a separate hardware counter. *Adds:* composition into one decision rule, plus counterbalanced order (they don't counterbalance). *Fails:* they have an independent energy cross-check; you have none. - **TokenPowerBench [7] / ML.ENERGY [8].** *Adds:* boundary events and error bars they lack. *Fails:* breadth — one machine, two models, sequential single requests; says nothing about serving.
- **Apple vs Oranges [9] / Benazir & Lin [13].** Same instrument or same silicon, no calibration or no energy. This is your cleanest novelty claim: first characterization of *powermetrics* as a phase-attribution instrument. Lead with it.
- **Zhuang et al. [26].** *Adds:* worst-case (non-RSS) composition for a systematic edge bound. *Fails:* your Student-*t* terms rest on independence/normality the draft admits are unestablished.

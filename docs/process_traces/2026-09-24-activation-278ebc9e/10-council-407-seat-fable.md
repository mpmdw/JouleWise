## Q1 — A3 (J/token along a long trace) as the first model night

**(a) AMEND: yes, first model night, but re-aim the estimand and drop the third-architecture contrast.**

**(b)** The review's test "b ≈ 0 ⇒ J/correct is token arithmetic" (record 02:128-131) is the wrong null. Under PC-4 (decode at near-flat 23–28 W, `docs/research_question_bank.md:1608`), J/token = P̄ × s/token, and s/token grows with position because each decoded token re-reads the KV cache. From the pinned configs, KV per token is 36×8×128×2×2 B = 147 KB (8B) and 28×8×128×2×2 B = 115 KB (1.7B) (`/Users/edr/jw_models/mlx-community/Qwen3-{8B,1.7B}-4bit/config.json`), against 4.3 GB and 0.94 GB of weights (packet 06 inventory). Bytes read per token at position n relative to position 0: 8B +14 % at 4k, +110 % at 32k; 1.7B +50 % at 4k, +390 % at 32k. So b ≠ 0 is close to guaranteed and tells us nothing about whether the *power meter* adds information. What decides that is whether P̄ varies: along the trace, between models, between phases. The planning stand-ins already suggest it barely does: 0.092 J/token × 231 tok/s ≈ 21 W (1.5B) and 0.375 × 49 ≈ 18 W (7B) (`docs/research_question_bank.md:1615-1616`; throughput planning figures `docs/process_traces/2026-09-01-fresh-model-review/24-luna-design-g2a.md:206`). If P̄ is flat, the MATH headline is stopwatch arithmetic, and only prefill, batching and long-context attention compute can rescue the meter. That is the premise to test, and the same night tests it if per-bin P̄ and per-bin s/token are both reported.

The KV effect is also a first-order MATH covariate: a 1.7B thinking trace of ~4,000 tokens averages ≈ +25 % J/token over its length, an 8B trace of ~2,500 tokens ≈ +4 %; that shifts R by ~20 %, the same order as SE(log R) ≈ 0.24 (AP-5M 07d:300-302), but systematic.

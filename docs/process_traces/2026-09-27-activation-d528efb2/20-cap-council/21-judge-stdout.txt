## Two findings from my own runs

- **Cell need does not rise steadily with frame length.** Four 2026-09-19 captures at 244–254 ms frames need only 45,000–53,000 cells, against 144,000–171,000 at 128–130 ms. The steepest growth law any seat fitted predicts about 20.9 million cells at 245 ms, so it is wrong there by a factor of about 440. No seat reported this. It is why I rejected both the "measure the top of the range first" rules (Sol, Astra) and the exponent checks (Opus, Fable seat).
- **The three clock-step losses are macOS network-time corrections.** My own system-log query shows a correction inside each of the three captures: +53.198 ms, +4.386 ms and −35.939 ms. A fourth loss (W1-d01) probably has the same cause; that is an inference, not established.

Network time was on for both windows, and the W1 chain script never checked it. The clock method's own text calls such captures validation-only material (`joulewise/uncertainty_evidence.py:903–909`). I referred that to the `dbad7cc7` issuance gate and did not rule on it.

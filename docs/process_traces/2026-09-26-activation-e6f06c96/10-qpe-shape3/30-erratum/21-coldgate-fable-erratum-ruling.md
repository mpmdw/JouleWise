## 1. The words this ruling uses

Every term below is used later with exactly this meaning. The first seven are the ruling under review's own terms, restated so this file stands alone.

- **Envelope.** One 600 s capture. A night runs 12 of them, one every 620 s. The **interior** is the 480 s from second 60 to second 540 of an envelope; the energy integrated over it is the envelope's published number (`joules`).
- **Collector.** The process that records one envelope. It writes the file `session.json` twice on the capture path: a **first write** before any measurement and a **final write** after all reduction. Only the final write carries the key `end_stamp` (the clock reading at the end of the capture).
- **Executor.** The parent process that launches each collector, waits for it, tears its process group down, and keeps one **entry** per envelope in memory. The entry's `collector_exit` is the collector's exit code as the executor saw it, or 124 if the executor's wait ran out.
- **Battery read.** One run of `ioreg` on the battery, stored as a record (a JSON object) beside the raw output's sha256 digest. The **pre read** is taken at the start of the envelope and is in the first write. The **post read** is taken at the end and is only in the final write. A read is **at float** when the battery reports `IsCharging = No`, a current of at most 200 mA in magnitude, and a **reading** (the battery gauge's own latest sample, stamped by the gauge with `UpdateTime`) that is at most 180 s old when the read is taken. A reading older than that is **stale**.
- **Verdict.** What the merged helper `authenticate_quiet_session` returns for one envelope. `pass`: both reads are present, authentic and at float. `battery_float_confounded`: at least one read was judged and shows the battery was not at float. `battery_float_evidence_missing`: no read shows a problem, but at least one read is absent or could not be judged.
- **Custody failure.** A recorded file no longer matches what was recorded about it. It is an exception (`CustodyFailure`, or its subclass `CustodyUnreadable`), never a verdict: it propagates and no summary is written.
- **Blank the night.** Text 6's rule: the night's summary is written with its status set to the battery verdict and every energy-derived field set to null.
- **Refusal record, completed record, unfinished record.** The three classes of `session.json`. *Completed*: it has the key `end_stamp`. *Refusal*: it has no `end_stamp` and its `error_class` is `network_time_provenance` (the collector found that the night's clock-discipline control record could not be read, declined to capture, and exited with code 3). *Unfinished*: neither.
- **Observer floor.** A night-level statistic: the measuring apparatus's own CPU use, in cores, computed as the sum of each envelope's recorded CPU seconds (`whole_envelope_observer_cpu_s`) divided by the sum of each envelope's span (end stamp minus start stamp). The summary computes it over every envelope whose summary row has no `error` text (the list `readable`, `:1321`) and raises `ValueError` if such an envelope lacks the CPU total or the span (`:1324-1327`). The executor turns that `ValueError` into a refused night (`:1685-1686`).
- **Unreadable-envelope path.** The existing branch of the summary (`:1221-1224`) that appends an envelope's row with an `error` text, the exclusion reason `incomplete_interior_support`, and no energy, and then moves to the next envelope. A row appended this way is outside `readable`, so the observer floor leaves it out.
- **Executor witness.** An entry whose `collector_exit` is an integer, is not a boolean, and is not 0. It is evidence held by a process other than the collector that the collector did not end normally.

## 3. Rulings

### B-1. UPHOLD, with the key changed from the missing field to the record's class

**The defect.** Amendment 35's item 3 says a passing envelope is "summarized as today". A refusal record can pass, because text 5 obliges the refusal path to take both battery reads (E5, last row). "As today" then carries it into the observer floor, which raises, and the night is refused (E2, third row). The collector did nothing wrong: it declined to capture under an unknown clock regime, which is what it is for.

**Worked example.** Envelope 7's collector finds the control record unreadable, writes a refusal record with both reads at float, and exits 3 within a second. The other 11 envelopes complete. Under the ruled text: `pilot_summary` raises, the executor books the night refused, 0 of 12 envelopes yield a number. Under the replacement: envelope 7 is excluded as `collect_error`, its pair partner 8 loses its pair, and the night keeps 11 envelopes and 5 pairs against registered minimums of 8 and 4.

**Could a published number be wrong?** No. The refused envelope has no energy (it never started a recorder). The observer floor leaves it out, and that is the correct statistic: the collector ran for about a second and recorded neither a CPU total nor a span, so its share is undefined, and the registration defines the floor "over all readable envelopes". The summary's own comment already states the intent that a refusal record excludes itself and does not cost the night (S3); the floor raise contradicts that intent by accident.

**Why the refuter's key is not adopted.** The refuter keys the new route on "`session.json` has no `whole_envelope_observer_cpu_s`". That names the symptom. A completed record that has lost that one field would be routed quietly into an exclusion, where today it raises (J4, third row). Keying on the refusal record (no `end_stamp`, `error_class` equal to the refusal class) routes exactly the honest case and leaves today's raise in place for everything else. A refusal record whose entry lacks the executor witness (exit 0: the collector's `main` returns 3 for every refusal, `:1686-1688`, so exit 0 contradicts the record) is likewise left to today's raise.

**Text change.** Item 3 is replaced by §5's item 3. Row T6-p is adopted with a second sub-row (exit 0), and row T6-u is added (the completed record with the CPU total removed).

### B-2. UPHOLD, with one clause added to the refuter's

**The defect.** The ruled item 4 excuses any unfinished record whose entry carries the executor witness and whose verdict is `evidence_missing`. Two records that no collector writes meet that test: one holding a post read (E3, E5 second row) and one holding no battery record (E5 third row). The first matters for custody. A completed record whose journal fails its digest check raises `CustodyFailure` (E5 first row). Delete `end_stamp` from it and the helper classes it as unfinished, does not open the journal (by amendment 30, because an unfinished record's journal is provisional), and returns `evidence_missing`. The ruled item 4 then excludes the envelope and the night proceeds. A custody failure has become an exclusion. The addendum-3 erratum checked exactly this edit and found it safe only because "text 6 blanks"; amendment 35 removed that premise without re-checking it.

**Could a published number be wrong?** Not directly: the envelope has a non-zero exit, so it is excluded as `collect_error` either way, and every retained envelope passes on its own pair. What breaks is the rule the charge tells me to keep intact, that custody is never converted into a status or an exclusion. The fix costs no honest night anything, so there is no reason to leave it open.

**Why the clause is right.** The collector writes the pre read in the first write, and writes the post read, `journal_rows` (the count of journal rows, amendment 33) and `end_stamp` together in the final write, which amendment 34 makes atomic. So every record an honest collector leaves unfinished holds a pre read and none of the other three. A failed pre probe is still recorded as a dict (S1). The excuse should admit that record and nothing else.

**What I add to the refuter's clause.** The refuter requires "`pre` present, `post` absent". J1 shows that deleting `end_stamp` and `post` together, and leaving `journal_rows`, still reaches the excuse under that clause. `journal_rows` is the one field that says "a final journal of this many rows was written". A record that carries it is declaring that its journal is final, and the helper's reason for not opening the journal (it is provisional) does not apply. I therefore require `journal_rows` to be absent, and `pre` to be a dict.

**What the clause does not do, stated plainly.** `session.json` has no digest, by design (the executor rewrites it to add the clock attestation). Someone who deletes all three of `end_stamp`, `post` and `journal_rows` produces bytes identical in form to an honest unfinished record, and no test of the file's content can tell them apart (J1, third row). The erratum ruling already placed a consistent rewrite by the machine's operator out of scope. The clause's job is to make the excuse match what the collector writes, so that no record which contradicts itself is excused.

**Text change.** Item 4's condition is replaced by §5's item 4 with the defined term "first-write record". Item 5's list gains the unfinished records that are not first-write records. Rows T6-q, T6-r and T6-s are adopted; T6-q gains the two-deletion sub-row and T6-r gains the `{}`, `[]` and `pre: null` sub-rows.

### B-4. UPHOLD, with the refuter's sentence corrected and its precondition stated

**What is right.** The ruling under review says a stale pre read "is absence, not evidence". E4 shows why that reason is incomplete: the stale reading has content, which may show charging, and the parser refuses it for age before looking. The excuse for a stale pre read is safe for a different reason, which the ruling should carry.

**The mechanism, with numbers.** Use seconds since envelope 4's scheduled start. Envelope 4's post read is taken at about 600; envelope 5 starts at 620 and takes its pre read then. Let U be the `UpdateTime` of the reading that the pre read of envelope 5 sees. It is stale there when 620 − U > 180, that is U < 440. Since the gauge produced nothing newer by 620, the post read of envelope 4 at 600 saw the same reading, at age 600 − U.

```
U, the reading's own time (s)      420               440                     620
                                    |                 |                       |
stale at post4 (taken at 600)?  yes |       no        |          no
stale at pre5  (taken at 620)?  yes |       yes       |          no
whose read judges the content   none|      post4      |         pre5
consequence if it shows         envelope 4 is         envelope 4 is           envelope 5 is
charging                        evidence_missing;     confounded;             confounded;
                                night blanked         night blanked           night blanked
```

Every element, named: the top line is U; 420 is 600 − 180, the oldest reading the post read of envelope 4 accepts; 440 is 620 − 180, the oldest the pre read of envelope 5 accepts; 620 is the pre read's own time. The three columns are the three ranges of U. `post4` and `pre5` are the two battery reads. Row three says which read, if any, judges the reading's content. Row four is what happens to the night when that content shows charging.

In the left column no read judges the content, but envelope 4 is a completed envelope with a non-pass, so text 6 blanks the night. In the middle column the post read of envelope 4 judges it. In the right column the reading is fresh and the pre read of envelope 5 judges it, which is the ruling's own Q2 case. J2 executes the left and middle columns through the merged helper: at every age tested the completed envelope is a non-pass. The argument does not depend on the 20 s: for any gap g ≥ 0 between the two reads, the earlier read sees the reading at age (age at pre) − g, and either judges it or is stale itself.

**The refuter's sentence, corrected.** The refuter writes that the post read of the previous envelope "read that same reading or a newer one". It cannot have read a newer one: readings only advance and that read is the earlier of the two. It read the same one.

**The precondition.** The argument needs the previous envelope to have a post read. If envelope k−1 is itself excluded without one taken near its end (it was excused, or it was a refused envelope, whose two reads are both taken at its start), then a reading with U between envelope k−1's start and 180 s before envelope k's start is judged by no read. I considered closing this by rule and decline to, for a reason a reader can check: that reading is evidence about an instant inside envelope k−1's own span, where nothing is published. The nearest published interior before it (envelope k−2's) ended at least 80 s before envelope k−1 started and is followed by two reads of its own that were judged (its post read and envelope k−1's pre read, or the night is blanked through them). The nearest after it (envelope k+1's) begins 680 s after envelope k's start and has its own pair. So no published number is supported less well than every other number under the gate's own 180 s tolerance. The case needs two consecutive failed collectors, a gauge that stopped refreshing for over three minutes, and a last reading that shows charging. It is recorded as a limitation in §6, Flag 2.

**Text change.** No change to amendment 35's rule. The following paragraph replaces the last sentence of the ruling under review's Q2 ("A pre read that failed or is stale is absence, not evidence, and is excused with the rest"), and is carried in the amendment's *Rationale to keep* paragraph in §5:

> A pre read whose probe failed produced no reading and is absence. A pre read whose reading is stale has content that was never judged, and it is excused for a different reason: the previous envelope's post read, taken earlier, saw the same reading at a smaller age, and either judged its content or was stale itself, and a completed envelope with a non-pass blanks the night under text 6. This holds for any gap between the two reads. It requires the previous envelope to be completed. Any change to when the post read is taken, or any rule that lets a completed envelope with a stale post read continue, must be checked against this paragraph first.

Row T6-v pins it.

## 5. Amendment 35, restated in full (supersedes the text in `21-coldgate-fable-ruling.md` §5 and §6; S2's brief quotes this section verbatim)

35. **A collector that failed or refused costs its own envelope, not the night (amends text 6 and amendment 32; S2; `pilot_summary` only).**

    *Definitions.* Each is decided from the envelope's `session.json`, read after `authenticate_quiet_session` has returned for that envelope, and from the executor's entry.
    - The record is **completed** when it has the key `end_stamp`.
    - The record is a **refusal record** when it has no `end_stamp` and its `error_class` equals `battery_float.QUIET_REFUSAL_ERROR_CLASS`.
    - The record is **unfinished** when it is neither completed nor a refusal record.
    - An unfinished record is a **first-write record** when it has no `journal_rows` key, and its `battery_float` value is a `dict` that holds the key `pre` with a `dict` value and does not hold the key `post`. This is the only unfinished record a collector writes: the pre read is in the first write, and the post read, `journal_rows` and `end_stamp` are written together in the final write (text 5, amendments 33 and 34).
    - The entry carries the **executor witness** when its `collector_exit` is an `int` that is not a `bool` and is not 0.
    - `<code>`, in the `error` texts below, is the entry's `collector_exit` rendered with `repr`, or the word `absent` when the entry has no such key.

    *Rule.* In `pilot_summary` (`quiet_predicate_campaign.py:1139`), for every entry the executor lists, in this order:

    1. Amendment 32 step (1) is unchanged: executor witness, and no `session.json`, no `rounds.jsonl` (both by `lstat`) and no `raw/round-*` directory → excluded `collect_error`, no authentication.
    2. Otherwise `authenticate_quiet_session(out)` runs first. A `CustodyFailure` (including `CustodyUnreadable`) propagates and no summary is written. ## 6. Disposition and flags

- **B-1 UPHOLD**, keyed on the refusal record, not on the missing field (item 3; T6-p, T6-u). **J-1** (judge's own): routing by record class, blanking by verdict (item 6). **B-2 UPHOLD**, with `journal_rows` absent and `pre` a dict added to the refuter's clause (item 4; T6-q, T6-r, T6-s). **B-3 UPHOLD** (item 2). **B-4 UPHOLD**, sentence corrected and precondition stated (rationale (ii); T6-v). **B-5 UPHOLD** (T6-n, T6-t). **B-6 UPHOLD** as an S2 brief obligation (X-1).
- **Flag 1 (scope).** B-1 and J-1 change how the summary treats a refusal record, which predates the battery rule. They are ruled here because amendment 35's own item 3 and its own test rows depend on them, and because S2 edits `pilot_summary` in the same change. If the magistrate wants them carried as a separate amendment number, the text does not change.
- **Flag 2 (limitation, from B-4).** When two consecutive envelopes are both excluded without a post read near the first one's end, a stale reading seen by the second one's pre read is judged by no read. The reasons this does not weaken a published number are in §3 B-4. It is recorded so that a later reader does not rediscover it as a defect.
- **Flag 3 (carried from the ruling under review, unchanged).** Its Flags 1 to 4 stand: the original charge's bench fact F2 is wrong; a timeout on envelopes 1 to 11 ends the night through the start-drift abort whatever the battery rule says; a completed envelope whose post probe failed still blanks the night; fixture rows carrying `abort` must be filtered.
- **Flag 4 (not ruled).** Whether the night's refusal document should carry a custody failure's text is a courier change outside S2's write scope.

## 7. Plain summary for Ed (5 lines)

1. A night is twelve 10-minute recordings, each with a battery check at its start and end. The earlier ruling said a recording whose process died is dropped on its own and the night keeps the rest. The reviewer found six problems with that ruling's wording, and I reproduced every one on the real code and upheld all six, two of them with a tighter fix than the reviewer proposed.
2. First fix: a recording that correctly declined to start (its clock check failed) passed the battery rule and then crashed the night's summary on an unrelated missing CPU figure. It is now dropped on its own like a crashed one. I also found that a night voided for a battery reason would have hit the same crash and been logged under the wrong reason, and fixed that.
3. Second fix: the "process died" exception was loose enough that deleting one field from a finished recording's file turned "this file was tampered with or lost, stop everything" into "skip this recording". The exception now applies only to a file that looks exactly like what a dying process leaves behind. Nothing can fully stop someone who rewrites the whole file, and the ruling says so.
4. The rule that a start-of-recording check showing the battery charging voids the night is unchanged. I corrected the argument for why an out-of-date battery reading can be forgiven (the previous recording's end check saw the same reading and already judged it), wrote down the one rare case where that argument does not reach, and showed why no published number is weaker for it.
5. The remaining three were wording and test-quality fixes: a contradiction between two sentences, a test that depended on timing (now deterministic, 5 of 5), and a missing log line when a tampering failure stops a night. The full rule and its 17 test cases are restated in one place for the implementer.

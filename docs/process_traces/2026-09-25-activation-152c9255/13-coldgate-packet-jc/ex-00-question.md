# 10/00 — Four-model council JCORRECT-FLOOR-01: the noise floor for "energy per correct answer", and whose floor a cross-model contrast uses

Assembled 2026-09-25 ≈05:30 PDT by the resident magistrate (Opus 5.5, activation 152c9255) under D-184: important science, so four seats. Nothing is armed.

## The physical problem, from the ground up

- **The headline question.** How does the energy to get a *correct* answer change with problem difficulty and model size? The runs use MATH problems at five difficulty levels and two Qwen3 models (1.7B and 8B, 4-bit MLX) on one Apple M3 Max. The draft analysis plan AP-5M v5 (`docs/process_traces/2026-09-24-activation-278ebc9e/52-ap5m-v5-draft/01-ap5m-draft-v5.md`, with its change ledger `02-` and open questions `03-`) makes the primary estimand **J/correct**: joules per correct answer, an additive form.
- **The claim rule.** A difference θ between two arms may be claimed only if |θ̂| exceeds a **noise floor**. That floor is the smallest difference this instrument produces when *nothing* differs. Under the ruled claim gate v2 (CG-1..CG-4, `docs/process_traces/2026-09-24-activation-278ebc9e/91-claimgate-final-texts-v2.md`), the floor for mean-scale claims is **F_est**, computed from k_cal ≥ 5 same-model ABBA *null* envelopes. In an ABBA null, the same model and configuration run in both arms in A-B-B-A order inside one quiet window, so any measured difference is instrument and run-to-run noise. Each envelope holds n_reg = 10 such blocks.
- **The gap.** The only existing null producer, the P2-015 detection-floor artifact's comparative component, measures null differences in **joules** (J), on a fixed-token workload with no correctness. No calibration records correctness, so no J/correct null exists. The wiring ruling (WR-9 in `docs/process_traces/2026-09-25-activation-152c9255/06-coldgate-packet-cgw/30-addendum/21-coldgate-fable-cgw-addendum-ruling.md`) forbids dividing a J floor by a correct count, and requires this council to choose before the AP-5M v5 registration can freeze. The choices are:
  - **(a)** a graded-workload null calibration with a J/correct producer: null ABBA envelopes on the scored MATH workload itself, so each null block has both energy and a correct count;
  - **(c)** CG-4(f)'s registered dimensionless margin with a derived, matched floor. Read CG-4(f) for its exact words.
  - **(b)**, registering J as primary instead of J/correct, is outside CG-4(f) and needs a cold amendment. Argue for it only if the science demands it.
- **A worked number to reason with.** Suppose a MATH level-3 block of 10 problems costs ≈ 400 J on the 8B model, with 6 of 10 correct: 66.7 J/correct. A null run-to-run spread of ±4 J in energy and ±1 in correct count moves J/correct by about ±1 % from energy but about ±17 % from the count. The ratio's noise is dominated by the *discrete correctness count*, not the joules. These numbers are illustrative; verify the real magnitudes from the repo's data and plans where they exist.

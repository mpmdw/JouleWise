The Energy Tripwire

An AI agent can edit its own logs. It cannot edit the joules it drew from the wall. JouleWise measures LLM agents on an Apple-silicon laptop precisely enough to ask whether sabotage leaves an energy signature: hidden work that shows up as unexplained energy against the work the agent reported.

The idea

Energy as a record the agent cannot rewrite

Every token an agent generates costs energy in a way we can model: prefill, decode, context length, tool calls, idle baseline. Fit that model on honest episodes and each new episode gets a residual: measured joules minus the joules its own log explains. Hidden reasoning, unlogged tool work or covert computation should push that residual up. The research question is how small a hidden action the tripwire can catch, at what false-alarm rate, on a machine that is never perfectly quiet.

No published work we could find uses measured energy to audit an agent's logged token counts. Software-only hidden-token audits exist (CoIn, token-inflation work), and physical verification exists at whole-workload scale. The per-episode, token-level tripwire sits between them.

Walkthrough

How a SHADE-Arena episode becomes a tripwire reading

SHADE-Arena gives an agent a legitimate main task inside a simulated tool environment, and in sabotage runs also a covert side task it must complete without getting caught. JouleWise runs open models on the laptop through two of its task pairs and meters every episode.

  1. 01

    Pick a task pair and a condition

    spam_filter_update and bank_transfer_2, each run benign (main task only) or sabotage (main task plus the hidden side task). Every episode has a fixed, published seed derived from model, task and index, so any run can be replayed exactly.

  2. 02

    Run the agent locally, under thermal control

    The model runs with thinking on, through multi-turn tool calls. Before the episode, gate G0 waits until the slow thermometer is back near its reference; between turns, gate G1 and short holds keep every turn starting from the same heat state, so heat history cannot fake a signal.

  3. 03

    Meter the whole machine

    A USB-C power meter sits between the wall brick and the MagSafe cable, so it records every joule the machine draws from the wall. powermetrics rails and SMC sensors record the inside view, and quiet guards record any background process that competes for the machine.

  4. 04

    Predict the energy from the log

    A five-term residual model, fit on honest episodes, turns the logged prefill, decode, context and tool counts into expected joules. The residual is what the log does not explain.

  5. 05

    Set the tripwire from benign runs

    The alarm threshold is the largest residual among m benign episodes. That gives a marginal false-alarm rate of at most 1/(m+1): about 5 % at m = 20, and 2.5 % at the registration grade of m ≥ 39 now ruled for v4.

  6. 06

    Test it against known hidden work

    The known-H test keeps the measured joules but strips a known number of reasoning tokens from the log, the way a deceptive agent might hide its thinking. Sweeping that number gives a detection curve (H50, H90: the hidden-token counts caught half and nine-tenths of the time). Positive-control nights will add real unlogged computation.

Read with care. The held-out sabotage set holds only two distinct trajectories, each threshold is a sample maximum, and the pilot tamper is a log edit rather than real hidden work. Roadmap

From readiness checks to a registered test

  1. 01

    R0 · readiness in progress

    Host, memory, model load and thermometry. The display now sleeps before every quiet step, and routine macOS background tasks are recorded instead of aborting the run.

  2. 02

    R1 · thermal rehearsal kit built

    One night (up to about 16 h worst case) that calibrates the thermal gates, the decode budget and the deadman on the production model, Qwen3.8-27B at 8-bit.

  3. 03

    W1 · first v4 window fallback ruled

    The 8-bit model decodes at about 11.9 tok/s, so the original budget cannot fit. A fallback was declared before any R1 number exists: a budget-sized warm-up cap first, then 4-bit, then the design council.

  4. 04

    D windows next

    Three long windows of up to 59,520 s, two task pairs each.

  5. 05

    Registration grade ruled

    At least 39 episodes per pair plus a held-out test cohort, decided before any alarm was read. This turns the pilot signal into a confirmatory test.

  6. 06

    Positive controls approved

    30B nights that inject real unlogged computation, so the tripwire is tested against genuine hidden work rather than edited logs.

Interp × energy

What a surgical weight edit does to power

Models can now edit weights directly: MIT CSAIL's "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming" (November 2026) changed 0.016 % of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights and lifted MATH-500 from 7.9 % to 48.1 %. An edit like that changes energy through two channels, and they differ by orders of magnitude.

Behaviour channel
12–15×

fewer tokens per correct answer in the Astra case. The edit changes how much the model writes and thinks. That shows up plainly at the meter, and it is the same channel the tripwire uses.

Physics channel
≤ 3×10⁻⁵

of energy at identical tokens, likely 10⁻⁶ or less. Different weight values flip different transistors, but separating that from zero would take about 95,000 replay pairs, so the honest deliverable is an upper bound.

Pulling a tiny signal out of a busy machine

Background programs are not the binding limit: in the known-bytes test they were 0.2 % of the measurement half-width. The limits are the effect's own size, and the chance that Apple's software counters are modelled rather than measured. Small-effect claims are therefore judged on the external meter, with lock-in modulation: the process swaps between edited and unedited weights on a known schedule and reads energy only at that frequency.

First experiment, ASTRA-2CH: one Mac window. Energy per answer for the edited and base models; the same token stream replayed through both weight sets; and a synthetic test of whether the power readings react to operand data at all.

Desk tools built: forced-token replay, a reversible weight-edit toolkit with in-process swapping, lock-in and power-analysis tools, a fixed-FLOP data-dependence benchmark, a preregistration draft and a behaviour-channel calculator. In progress: a joules-per-bit-transition model, energy-capability frontiers of edits, a tamper-detection threat model and blinded challenge, a per-component energy map, and the energy cost of ablation styles.

Model port

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on Apple silicon

The strongest open model that fits in 128 GB is the one we most want agents to run in SHADE-Arena: smart enough to attempt deception. Flash-Next is a 125B-parameter mixture of experts with about 6B active per token. We are building our own MLX implementation, checked against Qwen's official transformers code, because energy results are only as trustworthy as the model they run.

architecture
Gated DeltaNet, sparse attention with an indexer, hyper-connection streams, 512 experts, a 51B n-gram embedding paged from disk
validated
Gold parity on tiny configs and real-weight layer parity against the reference; a 48-layer bf16 quality reference (top-64) for scoring quantized builds
open
Full 48-layer MLX parity, a real-weight test of sparse attention, and every on-Mac measurement
build
6-bit experts with the rest bf16 and the n-gram table paged from SSD; about 2.7 GB of memory margin, so a 5-bit fallback is ready
speed
about 16–25 tok/s expected decode

Qwen3-Next itself already runs in mlx-lm. What upstream still lacks is multi-token prediction with recurrent-state rollback, a chunked gated-delta prefill kernel and a fused attention kernel; each is a multi-week job. Our most useful upstream contribution is a correctness audit: a verification package for the open Flash-Next pull request, built from our gold harness.

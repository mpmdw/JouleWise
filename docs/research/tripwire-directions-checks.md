## D1. Adaptive, meter-aware adversary ladder (extend candidate 1)

### Check by Fable 5.1 (workflow label `check:fable:0`)

**Refuted.** false

**Prior work.**

- Kimpson, Baker & Graham, 'Can Power Draw Constrain Covert Compute? Limits of Analogue Verification for AI Governance', arXiv 2610.07476 (opened, full HTML). Defines beta; A100; matched-energy cheating runs pass the no-alarm test while hiding 0.19-0.41 machines of covert int8 work; 0.059 only with re-execution + observed clock + resident-card overhead band. Uses total window energy only. Appendix B: 'a verifier reading time as well as energy constrains the adversary more than the energy channel alone does' and 'joint energy-time inversion is future work'. No LLM tokens, prefill/decode, token logs or agents. D1 is a direct answer to this deferred item, at single-Mac scale; it does not refute D1.
- Gargiulo & Kulp, 'Workload Identification with Physical Side Channels for AI Governance', arXiv 2609.00309 (opened). H200 at ~10 MHz, training/inference/non-AI classification, 97% accuracy; four fixed evasion strategies (chunked optimizer update, decoy-inference dilution, throttling, diluted LoRA caught 48-88%). Workload-class scale, no hidden-token or adaptive LLM adversary.
- Rahman & Tajdari, 'Detecting Hidden ML Training With Zero-Overhead Telemetry', arXiv 2606.19262 (opened abstract; ICML 2026 TAIG workshop). Five monitor-evader rounds, 20 evasion families, 9 GPUs via NVML; 43-87% accuracy on hardest disguised workloads. Closest methodological precedent for an iterated adversary ladder against a physical-telemetry detector, but for hidden training, not inference-time hidden tokens, and the telemetry is on-chip and trusted.
- Kale et al., 'Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring of LLM Agents', arXiv 2508.19461 (opened). 'Agent awareness dominates monitor awareness'; SHADE-Arena/CUA-SHADE-Arena; TPR at FPR 0.01; LLM transcript monitors only, no physical channel. Supports the tier-3 'aware agent' framing.
- Schmotz et al., 'Instrumental Monitor Evasion Emerges Under Ordinary Task Pressure', arXiv 2609.30217 (opened abstract). Evasion increases with test-time compute, reasoning effort and token use; runtime tool-call monitor, no physical channel. Relevant to tier 3: more hidden reasoning = more evasion = more joules, the exact coupling D1 exploits.
- Sun et al., 'CoIn: Counting the Invisible Reasoning Tokens in Commercial Opaque LLM APIs', arXiv 2505.13778 (opened abstract). Hash tree over token-embedding fingerprints; detects count inflation up to 94.7%; software-only, provider-side inflation threat (opposite sign to D1's omission threat).
- Wang et al., 'Predictive Auditing of Hidden Tokens in LLM APIs via Reasoning Length Estimation' (PALACE), arXiv 2508.00912 (opened abstract). Predicts hidden reasoning-token counts from prompt/answer; no latency or energy signal.
- Hoque, Zhang, Sun & Suya, 'Token Inflation: How Dishonest Providers Can Overcharge for LLM Usage', arXiv 2605.30040 (opened abstract). Defeats CoIn/PALACE-style audits (1,469% inflation undetected; 50.85% via tokenization ambiguity alone); argues any audit whose evidence comes from the audited party is structurally weak and calls for evidence the provider does not control (TEE, proofs, third-party re-execution). This is the strongest external argument FOR D1's trusted-meter + trusted-time design and AGAINST relying on D1's structural-consistency (re-tokenise) score, which is evidence the adversary authors.
- Singh et al., 'Detecting Hidden Chain-of-Thought in LLMs with Linguistic, Behavioral, and Mechanistic Indicators', arXiv 2608.29956 (opened abstract). HCDS compares neutral vs CoT/no-CoT prompting on Qwen3-4B; no adversary, no energy; a search snippet claimed a time-per-token indicator but the abstract does not confirm it (UNVERIFIED detail).
- SHADE-Arena, Kutasov et al., arXiv 2506.15740 (search result only, UNVERIFIED in this session): side-task success relies on a hidden scratchpad the monitor cannot see; best monitor AUC 0.87. The hidden-scratchpad dependence is the premise that makes an energy/time channel relevant.
- Internal, in-flight and overlapping: origin/align/2026-11-07-squeeze-prereg docs/registrations/align_v4_squeeze_addendum_DRAFT.md already registers adversary classes N/P/S/T/U/M + PC with an untrusted-log threat (omission, count inflation, plausible padding), a bounded 24-probe adaptation budget, m >= 39 and FPR <= 1/(m+1), and EXCLUDES duration/clock from the primary fit. origin/align/2026-11-07-adversary-kit docs/align/squeeze/ADVERSARY-KIT.md implements prompted variants + HiddenDecode known-H positive controls (doses 128-2048) and explicitly leaves forged counts/log records and detector-feedback loops out of scope. docs/research/AGENDA.md row 'Next 1' is already 'energy plus trusted time as two detectors of stripped reasoning tokens, with an attack grid' and 'a two-knob mixed attack must be registered too'. D1 is therefore largely a formalisation of AGENDA Next-1 + addendum classes P/M, not a new direction inside the project.

**Feasibility.** Feasible on the single Mac, and the time channel is probably much stronger than the proposal assumes, which changes what is interesting. Evidence in the repo: token-identical replays reproduce episode durations within 0.73 s across sessions (RECORD 1b198a71) and a wall-time fit on 12 members has residual SD 1.4 s (RECORD 0eafdaa2), while decode runs at roughly 42-97 tok/s on the 30B (RECORDs). Hidden decode of H = 1,024 tokens therefore adds about 10-25 s against a time noise floor of about 1-2 s, versus an energy effect of roughly 160-290 J (decode 0.158-0.279 J/token, RECORD af17111e) against a residual SD of 19-58 J and a sample-maximum threshold that currently detects only at H about 539-1,193 (RECORD f775edaf). So strip-only and strip+prefill-inflation attacks will be caught by time alone; the first half of the claim (joint power >= 0.8 at H = 1,024) is close to guaranteed, and the energy-only half (catch rate falls to FPR against a coefficient-knowing oracle) is true by construction. The non-trivial content is the two-knob mixed attack: claimed context inflation lengthens per-token decode time (KV read about 100 KB/token on a 30B-A3B; at 40k context about 2x per-step slowdown), so an oracle can match both energy and time with a few thousand visible decode tokens, well inside the 40,960 cap; the null region exists and is reachable. Feasibility attacks: (1) the structural-consistency score is authored by the adversary (Token Inflation 2605.30040 makes exactly this point), so a log-controlling oracle writes a consistent fake log and the score only catches lazy adversaries; it has teeth only when the padded text is fed to the transcript monitor, which is D2, not D1. (2) Energy and time share the GPU-clock confounder (bank residual vs clock +0.87, capped 1,176-1,313 MHz prefills); a 2-3 dimensional Mahalanobis fitted on m = 29-39 benign scores is noisy, but the conformal sample-maximum threshold on the joint score keeps FPR <= 1/(m+1) regardless, so register the joint score as primary as proposed and drop any Bonferroni or independence wording. (3) Tier 3 is mis-specified: in the current kit the agent cannot forge host-maintained counters, so a prompted 30B compared to a count-forging oracle loses by privilege, not by competence; the '< half the evasion' result is a near-certain, uninformative null. Either give the tier-3 agent the same knobs (a declare-usage tool whose output is what the detector sees) or recast tier 3 as 'does awareness change the energy/time signature of real sabotage', with the registered no-hide-instruction control (prompt-induced work confound, RECORD 7e06863b; the spam sabotage prompt alone is 755 tokens longer). (4) Trusted time needs onset/offset from the meter/powermetrics trace, not the agent's timestamps; the KM003C sampling (1-10 Hz per methodology doc) and the 1 Hz SMC sidecar are adequate for 10-25 s effects, and the existing pilot traces (powermetrics as root) suffice for tier 2 at 0 nights. (5) Mac-nights: ~36 tier-3 episodes (2 arms + control at 12) fit in 1-2 nights given pilot 9's 28 members in 6.5 h; RUN_STATE item 174 ends v3 pilots but RUN_STATE 204 / AGENDA approves more 30B positive-control nights in principle, so the dependency is a kit and Ed's OK, not a policy reversal. The two-model requirement rides on v4 (27B) calibration reaching m >= 39, which is already on the critical path. (6) Design cells: 4 attack classes x 5 H levels is fine for synthetic tier 2 (unlimited N) but not for tier 3 at 12/arm; keep tier 3 to one H and one attack class.

**Noteworthiness.** Candidly: tier 2 alone is incremental (a reanalysis rider; workshop level, the venue tier where Rahman & Tajdari's monitor-evader paper landed), and much of it is already in flight internally (AGENDA Next-1, squeeze addendum classes P/M, adversary kit). The one result that would be noteworthy is the quantified two-knob null region: a measured beta_episode per adversary tier on real agent trajectories, showing where energy-plus-trusted-time closes to within a few hundred hidden tokens and exactly which (claimed-prefill, claimed-context) pairs defeat both channels, forcing the adversary to hand the transcript monitor thousands of padded tokens. That is a direct, small-scale answer to Kimpson et al.'s explicitly deferred 'joint energy-time inversion', and no published work does it for hidden inference tokens. It only reaches main-track weight merged with D2 (monitor + energy squeeze) on two models at m >= 39, and framed for the AI-control community (per-episode agent oversight) rather than compute governance, where reviewers will object that a single Mac with a DC meter does not transfer to racks. The tier-3 prompted-agent comparison as written is likely a predictable null and should be redesigned or dropped; the headline 'energy-only falls to FPR against the oracle' is tautological and should not be sold as a finding. Expected outcome: the joint claim passes easily, the mixed-attack null region is real and measurable, and the interesting number is how many visible padding tokens the adversary must expose per hidden token (the squeeze exchange rate), which is a D2 quantity.

**Residual novelty.** Not refuted externally: no published work combines a trusted external energy meter, trusted execution timing and exact token logs to bound hidden LLM decode tokens per episode against a meter-aware adversary; Kimpson et al. defer exactly this joint inversion, Gargiulo & Kulp and Rahman & Tajdari work at workload-class scale with fixed or iterated but non-LLM evaders, and the CoIn/PALACE/Token-Inflation line is software-only with the opposite threat sign. Residual novelty after accounting for in-flight internal work is narrower: (a) the explicit forged-log oracle grid over {strip, strip+prefill, strip+context, optimal mixed} with the closed-form null region in (P_pre, P_ctx, P_dec), which the adversary kit excludes and the addendum only sketches as a 4x3 padding-by-placement grid; (b) trusted time as a registered second detector with its own LOO benign fit (the addendum excludes duration from the energy fit but does not use it as a channel; AGENDA names it but nothing is designed); (c) the beta_episode estimand per tier with H50/H90 from a window-cluster bootstrap, in Kimpson units; (d) the structural-consistency score, which is weak on its own and should be demoted to a lazy-adversary filter or folded into D2. The tier-3 prompted-vs-oracle comparison is not novel enough to carry weight as specified and risks a privilege-asymmetry null.

### Check by Opus 5.5 (workflow label `check:opus:0`)

**Refuted.** false

**Prior work.**

- Kimpson, Baker & Graham, arXiv 2610.07476 (opened). They derive a closed-form bound beta on hidden compute, give a scripted matched-energy witness with beta >= 0.41 on A100, and show beta = 0.059 only under four stacked restrictions (re-execution being one). Time is not a separate channel in their analysis. Their Appendix B lists joint energy-time inversion as future work. Agents, token logs and LLM adversaries are not covered. They already establish that energy alone fails against a matched-energy adversary, so tier 2's energy-only arm only confirms them.
- Gargiulo & Kulp, arXiv 2609.00309 (opened). External power traces on H200 classify training, inference and non-AI work; four evasion strategies are tested; diluted LoRA is caught 48-88% of the time (>= 98% with a rescue rule). The adversary is scripted, not an LLM, and the task is classifying workloads, not counting hidden tokens.
- Detecting Hidden ML Training With Zero-Overhead Telemetry, arXiv 2606.19262 (opened; I could not get the author list). Five rounds of monitor-versus-evader iteration, 20 strategy families, NVML only. A white-box evader cuts detection to 43-87%. This already uses an escalating-adversary ladder method for hardware telemetry, but the evaders were written by the researchers, not an LLM, and the target is hidden training, not hidden inference tokens.
- Kale et al., arXiv 2508.19461 (opened). When the agent knows it is monitored, LLM monitors on SHADE-Arena degrade more than from any other factor. This is behavioural (transcript) monitoring only, with no physical channel.
- Terekhov et al., arXiv 2510.09462 (opened). Adaptive agents that know the protocol defeat trusted LLM monitors with prompt injection, and the authors argue adaptive attacks should be standard practice. This motivates tier 3 but covers no energy channel.
- Mallen et al., Subversion Strategy Eval, arXiv 2412.12480 (opened). Models show no substantial ability to strategize statelessly against control protocols, and affordances such as a shared plan help a lot. This predicts the proposed tier-3 result (a prompted agent falls far short of the oracle) before any run.
- Sun et al., CoIn, arXiv 2505.13778 (opened). Audits hidden reasoning-token counts using a hash tree and semantic checks. It addresses the opposite threat (a provider over-reporting or padding), needs provider cooperation, and uses no energy or timing.
- Carlini & Nasr, arXiv 2410.17175 (opened). Remote timing leaks content from efficient inference. Only weak support for a timing channel; it says nothing about counting hidden tokens.
- Timing and Memory Telemetry on GPUs for AI Governance, arXiv 2602.09369 (abstract opened). Timing and memory observables used to infer real GPU use on untrusted hosts. It is adjacent to the trusted-time channel but has no energy, no LLM agents and no adversary ladder.
- In-repo: the 2026-11-07 methodology-vs-literature audit, idea 3 (commit 036c7402d), already found no prior work on stripping reasoning tokens with energy plus trusted time. It measured P_prefill/P_decode at about 1.8 on this Mac. RECORD 7e06863b shows the registered five-term design was misspecified at turn 0 (Fable's attention-length correction cut s_r from 243 J to 85 J on bank).

**Feasibility.** Tier 2 is feasible at the desk. Tier 3 and the bar for a noteworthy result are not feasible as written. Specific attacks:

(1) The tier-2 outcome is mostly arithmetic. When energy is matched, the time mismatch of an inflation attack is H*e_d*(1/P_dec - 1/P_x), where P_x is the marginal power of the padding term. P_pre/P_dec is about 1.8 on this Mac, so a prefill-only padding leaves a time gap of roughly 45% of the hidden decode time (about 7-8 s at H = 1,024 for the 30B-A3B). The joint score will almost certainly catch it; that result is nearly certain, not a finding. Context inflation pads decode-time attention, which is memory-bound like decode, so P_ctx is probably close to or below P_dec. Then context inflation alone, or a two-knob mix, matches energy and time together. In that case the claim "joint power >= 0.8 at H = 1,024" fails on energy plus time, and only the structural channel is left. The null region should be computed on paper from three marginal powers before anything is registered. The registered claim should be two-sided, because the likely result is that the oracle beats energy plus time.

(2) Synthetic tampers are circular if the hidden work's energy and time come from the fitted model. The detectors would then be tested against their own assumptions, and misfit would disappear. Splice in measured decode segments (turn-owned energy and trusted duration from real turns or replays) instead, so the true energy-time covariance and the thermal coupling come along.

(3) The adversary's powers are undefined at the structural channel, and the outcome flips on that choice. If the logs carry text and the verifier re-tokenises, count-only padding is caught 100% of the time (trivial). If padding has to be real logged text, it is real work that the model prices, so the only way to evade is to exploit model mispricing or support gaps. That is the known misspecification issue: untampered spam sabotage already false-alarmed in 5 of 6 cases in pilot 6. The chain t+1 prompt = t context + outputs also couples context inflation to earlier prefill, which charges energy on earlier turns. So any per-turn versus per-episode scoring choice changes the result and must be fixed in advance.

(4) The strongest verifier is missing. Seeded replays repeat to about 2 J within a window (with a 5-10 J offset between windows), yet the design scores against regression fits with residual SD 19-58 J. A forced-token replay of the declared log is the real re-execution regime. An adversary who knows only the coefficients cannot match a replay to within a few joules, so the coefficient-aware threat model collapses against it. The design should either add replay as a scored channel or stop citing Kimpson's beta = 0.059.

(5) The Kimpson mapping is a category error. Their beta is hidden compute as a fraction of machine capacity over a fixed window, and 0.059 needs four restrictions. JouleWise's beta_episode counts hidden tokens per episode under a benign-fit predictor. Cite them as a framework, not as a number that transfers.

(6) Tier 3 is ill-posed. The harness has to withhold the hidden turns, so it is complicit. The agent itself cannot change logged counts except through real actions, which are priced. So tier 2 (operator-level falsification) and tier 3 (agent-level real actions) are different threat models, not rungs of one ladder. Hidden H is chosen by the agent, so H50/H90 cannot be estimated from about 12 episodes per arm, and a no-hide control arm is also needed. With n = 12 per arm, the binomial CI on an evasion rate is about +/- 0.28, so "less than half the oracle" cannot be resolved. Mallen et al. already predict the null.

(7) The budget is optimistic. Today m is about 12-14 per pair per window. Reaching m >= 39 benign per pair on two models takes several windows per model, not 1-2 nights, and the second model depends on v4 and W1. Item 174 needs Ed's OK.

(8) Noise is fine for the physics. Replays repeat to about 2 J, and the AGENDA records that the GPU clock is pinned on the fan-cooled Mac, which removes Kimpson's DVFS escape. What limits detection is misfit: corrected s_r is still 24-105 J on narrow benign sets.

Fix: compute the null region analytically; splice in measured segments; define the log/text powers precisely; add a forced-token-replay channel; drop tier 3, or reframe it as a descriptive demo.

**Noteworthiness.** Incremental, workshop level, at best. The pieces that will probably succeed are already determined: energy-only fails against the oracle (Kimpson), and energy plus time catches prefill padding (it follows from P_pre/P_dec of about 1.8). The piece that could be interesting, context or mixed inflation, will probably go against the claim: the oracle evades energy plus time, and only structure or replay stops it. A careful 'here is the evasion region and the replay check that closes it' paper is a solid governance-workshop or AI-control-workshop contribution, and that negative-for-energy result is in fact the most citable outcome. Tier 3 will very likely give the predictable 'weak agent fails to evade' null (Mallen et al.), and with about 12 episodes per arm it cannot support the 'less than half the oracle' claim. A main-track paper would need two models at m >= 39, a replay-verifier result, and a coherent threat model in which someone actually controls the log. That is unlikely on one Mac in the stated budget. On the user's real question ('better than SHADE-Arena alone'): this direction never compares against a transcript monitor. Against hidden tokens such a monitor is blind by construction, so 'better than SHADE' is trivially true and not by itself a noteworthy claim. What would be noteworthy is a small calibrated beta_episode (tens of tokens) that holds up against a stated, strong adversary.

**Residual novelty.** Narrow but real: (a) The first per-episode audit of stripped reasoning tokens that uses physical energy plus trusted time plus log-structure consistency on LLM agents. Kimpson explicitly defers joint energy-time inversion to future work, and no source I opened treats stripping hidden tokens as the threat. (b) A measured, quantitative evasion region for the two-knob attack (prefill against context padding) on Apple unified memory, where the decode, prefill and attention marginal powers differ from A100/H200. (c) A forced-token replay verifier on consumer hardware with about 2 J repeatability. That would be the only empirical re-execution-regime number for inference, and it would sharpen Kimpson's analysis. The adversary-ladder method itself is anticipated by 2606.19262's monitor-evader rounds, and energy-only failing against a matched-energy adversary is already established analytically and empirically by Kimpson.

**Feasibility.** Feasible as a zero-Mac-night desk analysis, but as specified the result is close to predetermined and almost certainly 'unresolved' on the stated decision rule. Specific attacks: (1) The energy curve is not an experiment, it is arithmetic. Hiding at fraction f with measured joules held fixed makes the residual shift deterministic: delta ~= f * R_traj * e, with e ~ 0.5-0.67 J/token (RUN_STATE pilots 4-9; e901eb2a4 RECORD 7e06863b) and R_traj ~ 1.5-3k reasoning tokens per 30B sabotage trajectory (pilot-4 tamper crossings at 1,282 / 2,192-3,077 tokens; pilots 8-9 detected one-turn omissions of 539 / 1,193). Against frozen thresholds 2.53 (bank, s_r 45-58 J) and 7.13 (spam, s_r 19-33 J, resting on one benign trajectory) the per-trajectory crossover fraction f* = tau*s_r/(R*e) is roughly 0.05-0.10 for bank and 0.10-0.20 for spam. So the energy detector saturates to ~1.0 by f ~ 0.25 and the entire informative region lies in f in [0, 0.25], which the proposed grid {0, .25, .5, .75, 1} skips completely. The measurement noise (19-58 J) is irrelevant to the desk analysis except through tau; no new joules are collected. Use a fine low-f grid or the existing E1(a) H-grid {0,128,256,300,512,1024,2048}, and report the empirical CDF of f* with a trajectory-cluster CI instead of a catch-rate-vs-f curve. (2) Random-fraction stripping is a straw hider. A real hider deletes the incriminating spans, not a uniform 25% of tokens. Under random stripping the monitor curve will be nearly flat until f -> 1 (the sentence that mentions the side task survives), so 'no escape' is trivially true and uninformative; under targeted stripping (delete side-task-mentioning sentences first, the D1 tier-2/3 adversary) the monitor drops at f of a few percent where the meter is still blind, so an escape window exists by construction. **Residual novelty.** What survives: no external paper combines a per-episode physical energy residual with a transcript monitor along any hiding axis; the continuous-f sweep, the monitor-only (content-effect) control, and a per-trajectory crossover estimand (f_T* vs f_E*) are not in the literature and not yet in the repo's prereg draft, which uses fixed H doses and escape rates. (1) The energy half is arithmetic, not a measurement. Removing f of the logged reasoning tokens lowers predicted energy by about f*N*0.62 J. Whether that crosses the threshold follows from s_r, and the repo already has this curve (crossings at H = 1.2-3k tokens). Of the three curves, only the monitor's falling curve carries any empirical content.

(2) A uniform-f hider is a straw adversary. A real hider strips only the incriminating sentences, likely tens to a few hundred tokens. That costs about 60-190 J at 0.62 J/token, at or below s_r of 58-176 J and well under the 1.2-3k-token floor. (3) A log-forging adversary beats a count check. It can replace hidden tokens with count-matched benign filler; CoIn needed semantic heads for exactly this reason. Energy can only catch it if the forged text never entered the KV cache, so that later prefill costs disagree. Nobody has tested that, and it is the more interesting sub-question.

(4) Energy cannot see compute-neutral or compute-reducing hiding: steganography, obfuscation, latent reasoning, or control-token suppression (2609.27542). The 'only detector whose power rises' framing holds only for compute-preserving concealment, i.e. log tampering or off-log compute.

At n = 12 the binomial CI on catch rate is about ±0.25-0.3, so a 0.2 margin cannot be told apart from 0.05. That needs at least 40-60 distinct sabotage trajectories per model per task pair, and FPR 2.5% needs m ≥ 39 benign per pair. The current 10-12 benign per window falls short, and since cross-pair transfer fails, pooling cannot fix it.

(7) A weak local monitor (Qwen3-8B, 27B) depresses the f = 0 baseline and inflates energy's apparent contribution. Reviewers will want a frontier monitor, which collides with transcript custody.

(8) Physical evasion: an agent with shell access can change J/token through power mode or DVFS (ClawGuard flags DVFS). The residual model must be robust to that or detect the mode change.

**Noteworthiness.** As designed, it is incremental, and the headline is unlikely to hold against a sensible adversary.

Simulated uniform stripping makes the rising energy curve true by construction. A falling monitor curve under hidden reasoning is already expected from SHADE-Arena and CoT Red-Handed, and NP combination is Hua et al.'s method. So a 'positive' joint curve would read to reviewers as a well-made figure in the ALIGN paper, not a standalone result.

The main-track bar is: a real off-log-compute positive control, selective and log-forging adaptive adversaries, a frontier monitor, at least 40 distinct sabotage trajectories per cell, and two models. Clearing it on one Mac in the available time is unlikely. Under selective hiding the likely outcome is a measured escape region, because H_blind of tens to hundreds of tokens is far below H_det of about 1.2-3k.

That negative result, framed honestly as 'here is the minimum hidden compute a physical tripwire can see, and here is how little a monitor needs removed', is publishable at workshop level and more useful to the field than the squeeze curve. A positive result on the causal-consistency (forged-log) test would be the one finding here with a real chance of being noteworthy.

Also fix two problems in the motivation: the MonitoringBench citation is misattributed, and the 'only detector whose power rises' claim ignores compute-reducing hiding (2609.27542).

**Residual novelty.** I found no paper that combines a physical energy or compute-consistency signal with a transcript/CoT monitor along a reasoning-concealment axis. The closest pieces are covered separately:
- NP monitor combination (Hua)
- content+action hybrids (CoT Red-Handed)
- hidden-scratchpad sabotage (SHADE-Arena)
- hidden-token count auditing by software or crypto (CoIn, PALACE)
- physical side-channel agent monitoring for workflow hijacking (ClawGuard)

What remains genuinely new is a physical, software-independent check that the logged transcript accounts for the compute spent, plus its complementarity with content monitors. The most novel and defensible version is not the uniform-f squeeze curve. It is two measurements:
- H_blind vs H_det: how much incriminating reasoning a selective hider must remove to blind a monitor, compared with the energy floor.
- A causal-consistency test: whether energy, through prefill/KV-cache costs, catches count-matched forged logs that a pure count check cannot.

On a MacBook Pro SoC the idle/light floor is a few watts against ~35-50 W decode, so pacing recovers at most ~10% of the gap; the masked arm stays hundreds of J from the declared fit. Batched BoN (one forward pass with batch N; weights read once) keeps wall time near N=1 on a bandwidth-bound decoder while energy rises only by the compute-bound fraction, plausibly +10-30% at N=2. That is the one arm where power is genuinely uncertain and energy beats timing; it must be the batched variant, and mlx-lm batched generation support should be confirmed (UNVERIFIED here).

That is a desk tamper with a partly checkable log, not a physical confusion set. The real confusion set is Kimpson & Graham's matched-energy adversary: a 4-bit substitute paced to 8-bit speed plus a dummy GPU/ANE kernel tuned to burn the energy difference. The adversary 'controls the served model and pacing' and so trivially also controls busy-work; omitting this arm removes the only hard test. With it, whole-episode energy is defeated by construction and the question becomes whether 1 kHz KM003C trace shape (token cadence, prefill/decode envelope) still separates, which is a Gargiulo-Kulp-style classifier on a consumer USB meter rather than a 10 MHz probe.

The genuinely noteworthy outcomes would be (i) batched best-of-N detected by energy while invisible to timing (first demonstration that a consumer meter sees discarded samples, which Anthropic's provable inference explicitly cannot), and (ii) a measured answer to whether 1 kHz trace shape survives an energy-padding adversary, tying the whole-episode defeat to Kimpson's beta=0.41 and the shape result to Gargiulo & Kulp's class-level finding. Without those two arms the honest label is 'incremental section of the main paper'; with them it is a plausible workshop paper, and the shape-vs-padding arm carries real risk of a null, which is what makes it worth running.

**Residual novelty.** What remains new after prior work: (1) energy as the only verifier-side channel that sees compute spent on tokens the provider discards (hidden best-of-N, hidden reasoning, hidden reruns), which neither output-replay (DiFR), activation hashing (TOPLOC), text fingerprinting (IRIS) nor ZK proofs (Anthropic) cover; (2) doing it with a USB-C PD meter on a laptop rather than 10 MHz probes on an H200, and with a declared-model frozen fit as the re-execution analogue (Kimpson's beta=0.059 regime); (3) an explicit two-channel (energy, time) confusion analysis for inference, which the governance literature has only done for training vs inference. Recommended redesign to capture that novelty in one night: drop the 30B-A3B arm (straw man), keep 4-bit unmasked only as a calibration point, make best-of-N batched (N=2,4) and report energy vs time separation, replace the 'padded counts' arm with an energy-padding arm (4-bit paced + dummy kernel tuned to the 8-bit fit), and pre-register a 1 kHz trace-shape classifier trained on benign D members as the fallback channel for that arm. Follow-on directions this unlocks if the instrument proves out: auditing hidden reasoning-token billing (hidden tokens are the one place count padding is unverifiable), verifying agent harness compute (tool-call reruns, retries, self-consistency) against declared budgets, and cross-model fits so a verifier with meter access can bound 'which model class could have produced this energy' as a one-sided confusion set rather than a point identification.

(decode coefficient collapse under batching), JouleShare (non-additive, 12-18% attribution error even when calibrated) and Kimpson (more legitimate load gives more cover) all point to it already. A measured curve confirming it would be a solid limitations section or workshop paper, not main-track governance work. What would be noteworthy is a positive surprise. That would be showing that a device-level log-completeness check (predict whole-node energy from all logged requests, no attribution) keeps H50 for unlogged decode within a small fraction of node tokens at c of 8-16, at a trusted external boundary. I would guess this is roughly a coin flip. Marginal unlogged decode tokens cost little at high batch, so it may hold only for large hidden workloads, such as whole hidden episodes rather than hidden tokens within an episode.

The adversarial co-tenant arm would not yield a noteworthy result; it is mis-posed, since energy cannot detect mislabeled work. Recommended order: run the Mac static-batch version first, at no rental cost and at the trusted DC boundary. Seek GPU funding only if the device-level framing shows resolution surviving at c >= 4.

**Residual novelty.** Not refuted. I found no paper that measures hidden-work detection resolution (H50 in tokens) against concurrency for any energy-based tripwire. What remains novel is narrower than the direction claims, though, and part of the design is mis-posed.

1. The 'per-request attribution restores resolution' arm is largely answered already. JouleShare shows batched energy is non-additive per request, and even a calibrated full-feature model leaves 12-18% normalized L1 error, an order of magnitude above the 1-2% residual the tripwire needs. WattsOnLLM (slides only) says the same about non-additivity.

2. The direction is falsified for a flawed reason. If co-tenants are independent, residual SD grows about as sqrt(c) even when energy is perfectly additive, so 'SD does not grow at least linearly' does not imply additivity, and 'SD grows' does not imply non-additivity. Additivity should be tested directly with JouleShare-style subset replays: E(A u B) vs E(A)+E(B)-idle.

3. Much of the headline is predictable before running anything. H50 is roughly k * s_r(c) / e_marg(c), where k is about 2.2 for an m=39 max threshold. Decode is memory-bound, so batching amortizes the weight read, and Wu et al. show the decode coefficient collapsing (decode:prefill about 335x at batch 1 vs 3.3x batched). With zero added noise, H50 still rises by roughly the per-token amortization factor. The 'H50 at c=8 exceeds 25% of decode tokens' outcome therefore follows almost mechanically once H50 at c=1 is at least a few percent of decode tokens.

4. The answer depends mostly on the experimenter's choice of co-tenant traffic. With synthetic co-tenants of known token counts, the verifier effectively holds everything and device-level noise may stay near replay level. With unknown traffic, the noise is just the variance the experimenter chose for that traffic. The 'resolution-vs-concurrency curve' is therefore not a hardware constant unless it is indexed by co-tenant entropy and by what the verifier knows.

5. The 'hide work inside co-tenant traffic' adversary arm is ill-posed. Energy counts unlogged work. Work logged under another request's name is mislabeled, not hidden, and conservation of energy cannot see that.

The genuinely open, worthwhile reframing is a device-level log-completeness audit under batching. Predict the whole node's energy from all logged requests in the batch (no per-episode attribution) and measure H50 for unlogged decode at concurrency c, at a trusted external boundary. That bridges the agent tripwire to Kimpson's beta(h) for inference serving. Kimpson used NVML and named off-chip meters as future work, and nobody has measured this at an external boundary.

Feasibility: the GPU version as written cannot rest its claim on a PSU meter if run in the cloud. Clouds expose no PSU meter, and the runbook's own whole-GPU/no-co-tenant isolation rule conflicts with the design. It therefore needs a physical workstation plus an AC/DC meter, PSU-efficiency and fan confounds, root for clock locks, and Ed's go. The cost of about 20-40 GPU-hours is fine. The cheaper and stronger first step is on the Mac with the existing external DC meter and the repo's mlx-lm static-batch path (B in {1,2,4,8}). Mac replay repeatability of about 2 J means a deterministic co-tenant set keeps device-level noise near the floor, so the experiment cleanly isolates the marginal-token amortization effect. Pilot residuals of 19-58 J on 2-4 kJ episodes set the batch-1 baseline. Static batching is not continuous batching, and that caveat must be stated.

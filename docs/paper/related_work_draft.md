# Related Work

JouleWise lies at the intersection of inference-energy benchmarking, software power measurement, experimental metrology, and split inference. Its central difference is one of emphasis: the measurement instrument is the product, while model and deployment comparisons are demonstration studies. The instrument reports an energy verdict for a complete, declared measurement window; records the raw observations and their chain of custody; freezes the comparison and acceptance rules before seeing the result; and declines to make a claim when an effect falls below its detection floor, the smallest effect it can distinguish under the stated conditions. This framing makes uncertainty, timing attribution, drift, and evidence integrity part of the result rather than implementation details behind a leaderboard value.

## LLM inference energy measurement and benchmarks

Recent systems have made large language model energy visible at useful levels of detail. TokenPowerBench separates total energy both by inference phase and by hardware component, reports prompt-processing and token-generation energy with phase-appropriate token denominators, and groups results by context length [TokenPowerBench]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: TokenPowerBench decomposes energy by phase and component, uses input/output-token denominators for the two phases, and bins by context length. --> That reporting structure is valuable, but its disclosed method says only that each power sample is tagged with the active stage; it does not specify boundary events, alignment rules, sampling rate, repetitions, variance, an idle baseline, or external validation, and it does not explain phase overlap under continuous batching [TokenPowerBench]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: the TokenPowerBench deep read found unspecified phase tagging, no sampling rate, no repetitions or error bars, no idle baseline or external validation, and unaddressed continuous-batching overlap. --> JouleWise adopts phase-specific reporting while restricting its primary scope to one sequential request at a time, where prompt processing and token generation have unambiguous boundaries that can be calibrated.

The Illusion of Power Capping in LLM Decode provides a stronger measurement template: it samples graphics processing unit (GPU) power, integrates the samples, repeats configurations, and checks the integral against a separate hardware energy counter, while explicitly limiting that check to operations long enough for the counter to be meaningful [IllusionPowerCapping]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: the Illusion study uses sampled-power integration, repetitions, an independent NVML energy-counter cross-check, and a duration-conditional validity domain. --> However, its counter agreement, run-to-run variation, snapshot fallback, and timing alignment are not composed into a bound on the reported savings, and its long sweeps do not include a drift-control design [IllusionPowerCapping]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: the sweep identifies uncomposed error terms and no drift control across long sweeps as the study's open lane. --> JouleWise treats those terms as inputs to a single claim decision rather than as separate caveats.

Benchmark standards reinforce that measurement validity belongs in each run. MLPerf Power and the associated Standard Performance Evaluation Corporation (SPEC) methodology require a qualified external analyzer, uncertainty computed at the actual load, fixed measurement ranges, synchronized clocks, sufficiently long windows, invalid-sample accounting, and special handling for battery-backed systems [MLPerfPower]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: MLPerf Power/SPEC requires load-specific analyzer uncertainty, fixed ranges, clock synchronization, minimum windows, per-window invalid-sample accounting, and neutralized battery charge flow. --> These rules are designed for direct power analyzers and data-center-style benchmarks; JouleWise translates their reject-on-missing-evidence discipline to a free software counter on consumer hardware. Apple-focused comparisons show why that translation matters: Silicon Showdown compares deployed ecosystems using a GPU-board boundary on one platform and a whole-system-on-chip software-counter boundary on Apple hardware, with unmatched runtimes and precision stacks and no comparison of model-output accuracy [SiliconShowdown]. <!-- 2026-07-11-rpt002-related-work-refresh.md: Silicon Showdown compares unmatched runtime/artifact/precision stacks across PyNVML GPU-board and powermetrics whole-SoC boundaries and includes no accuracy evaluation. --> JouleWise therefore names the measured boundary and prioritizes within-stack claims instead of treating unlike telemetry boundaries as interchangeable.

## Software power measurement on commodity hardware

Software-visible counters such as Intel's Running Average Power Limit (RAPL) counters and Apple's `powermetrics` make repeated energy experiments practical without a laboratory meter, but their readings remain outputs of a measurement system rather than ground truth. RAPL in Action shows that validation should model the relationship between software channels and wall power, search for lag before comparing streams, account for temporal correlation, warm the machine to reduce thermal drift, audit the sampler's overhead, and inspect counter mechanics such as update granularity, overflow, jitter, and timestamp behavior [RAPLInAction]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: RAPL in Action contributes model-based wall validation, lag search, autocorrelation-aware analysis, warm-up, sampler-overhead measurement, and a counter-mechanics checklist. --> Jay and Ostapenco likewise compare software meters against wall power with controlled workloads and regression, finding that the gap changes with load rather than behaving as a constant offset; they also refuse component-level rankings where no reference exists [JayOstapenco]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: Jay/Ostapenco regress wall power on software readings, find a load-dependent rather than constant gap, and decline claims where no reference validates subtotals. --> A wall meter can therefore test whole-machine totals, but it cannot by itself validate how a software trace divides that total between prompt processing and token generation [JayOstapenco]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: the sweep states that wall metering validates totals only, while phase splits require a separate phase-attribution method. -->

JouleWise applies this lineage to `powermetrics` on Apple silicon. It integrates named system-on-chip power channels only inside runtime-emitted phase boundaries, retains the raw counter stream, and calibrates timing attribution inside the same experimental window. Its current validated characterization finds that assigning power samples to the correct phase contributes about one joule of uncertainty and dominates ordinary repeat-to-repeat variation. In plain terms, the limiting question is not whether the machine repeats the workload consistently, but whether samples near a phase boundary belong just inside or just outside it. <!-- CLAIMS_STATUS.md section 1: the VALID standing measurement fact is that the instrument is attribution-limited at approximately one joule, rather than noise-limited. --> This limitation is published as part of the detection floor. It is not hidden by a smooth average or treated as a constant calibration offset.

The choice of energy basis also affects interpretation. A practical guide to power measurement recommends reporting both absolute energy and energy above idle, propagating idle variability, distinguishing quiet idle from idle during an active workload, and reporting runtime and peak power alongside energy [BeginnersGuidePowerMeasurement]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: the Beginner's Guide recommends absolute and marginal reporting, idle-variance propagation, active-idle awareness, and runtime/peak-power context. --> JouleWise follows the dual-reporting idea but uses gross energy inside the declared boundary for headline comparisons, with idle-subtracted energy as a separately labeled within-device view.

## Metrology and experimental discipline

For JouleWise, the detection floor combines repeatability, uncertainty about phase-boundary placement, and measured drift across the collection window. The project freezes the proposed contrast, workload, sample plan, and acceptance rules before collection; brackets measurements with references; counterbalances comparison order so gradual drift tends to cancel; and raises or refuses the floor when the observed conditions are worse than the preregistered assumptions. Run bundles are bound by cryptographic hashes, which make later changes detectable, and preserve raw power samples, runtime events, configuration, and derived summaries so a result can be recomputed rather than trusted from a table.

Paired minimum-detectable-effect methodology supports prospective sample sizing from paired differences and a one-way “prior ratchet”: if observed variation exceeds the preregistered assumption, the detection threshold may move upward but not downward [PairedMDE]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: Paired-MDE supplies powered sample sizing over paired differences and a preregistered prior-ratchet that can only increase the floor. --> That statistical treatment does not justify combining a worst-case timing-attribution bound as though it were independent random noise [PairedMDE]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: Paired-MDE's root-sum-square composition applies only to independent random components and would be anti-conservative for JouleWise's systematic attribution bound. --> MLPerf's per-window uncertainty and invalid-sample accounting offer a complementary rule-based model: validity is an outcome of the recorded run, not a one-time statement about the instrument's data sheet [MLPerfPower]. <!-- 2026-07-30-sweep-techniques.md: MLPerf/SPEC reports uncertainty and invalid samples per interval and rejects windows that do not meet the run-level rules. -->

JouleWise extends these ideas from benchmark compliance to instrument validation. Its main evaluation asks whether the counter response is linear, reads zero for a zero-effect comparison, resolves known effects around the declared floor, preserves energy when phases are summed, remains stable across time, and reconciles internal channels. Model-size, quantization, or split-inference comparisons then serve as test signals: they demonstrate what the validated instrument can and cannot resolve. This ordering also makes refusals informative. A rejected window, failed admission check, or below-floor contrast documents the instrument's operating limits instead of disappearing from the final analysis.

## Split and disaggregated inference as a demonstration context

Split, or disaggregated, inference places prompt processing and token generation on different devices and transfers the intermediate state between them. Existing work establishes that this deployment choice has an energy question, but it also shows why boundary discipline matters. Revisiting Disaggregation Energy reports higher energy for disaggregation on its evaluated two-GPU node and shows that performance depends on load, baseline, and transfer conditions; its energy boundary combines several software telemetry sources into whole-run energy per token [RevisitingDisaggregationEnergy]. <!-- 2026-07-11-rpt002-related-work-refresh.md: the study reports higher disaggregation energy on one two-A100 PCIe node, with load/baseline/transfer-sensitive performance and a pynvml+RAPL+IPMI whole-run J/token boundary. --> DualScale studies phase placement and per-phase power control, but reports GPU-only energy from homogeneous server hardware [DualScale]. <!-- 2026-07-11-rpt002-related-work-refresh.md: DualScale covers phase placement and per-phase DVFS on homogeneous H100 servers with GPU-only sampled energy. --> Prima.cpp includes whole-run energy and communication accounting for split inference, but does not report wall power or a per-stage energy split [PrimaCPP]. <!-- 2026-07-11-rpt002-related-work-refresh.md: Prima.cpp reports whole-run energy per output-token unit and device-side communication accounting, without wall power or per-stage energy. --> SplitZip demonstrates lossless transfer compression and reports where online time is spent, but makes no energy claim [SplitZip]. <!-- 2026-07-11-rpt002-related-work-refresh.md: SplitZip establishes bitwise-lossless compression and online performance shares but provides no energy implication. -->

JouleWise does not present a new split scheduler as its primary contribution. Instead, split inference is a demanding demonstration of the instrument: it introduces two measurement boundaries, a transfer interval, phase attribution, and possible drift across devices. A valid result must include both ends of the transfer, state the physical boundary on each device, and clear a preregistered detection floor. Whether splitting saves energy is therefore a study outcome; the reusable contribution is the method that can issue, qualify, or refuse that outcome.

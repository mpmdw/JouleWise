# JouleWise: Attribution-Limited Detection Floors for Phase-Resolved LLM Inference Energy on Apple Silicon

*Draft: demonstration values, instrument-characterization results, and archive locators are pending; bracketed markers show where they will land.*

## Abstract

Large language model (LLM) inference energy figures rarely include an error bar for the smallest trustworthy difference. This matters when dividing a request into prompt processing, which converts input to model state, and token generation, which produces discrete output units called tokens. Power falls steeply exactly where that boundary is drawn because prompt processing saturates compute while generation waits on memory; a boundary tens of milliseconds late across a tens-of-watts step moves on the order of a joule; the total is unchanged, so repetition cannot fix it. JouleWise treats Apple's *powermetrics* counter as an instrument, using runtime boundaries, in-session calibration, and a *detection floor*: a condition-scoped practical false-effect guard formed from the larger of absolute-repeatability and null-comparison guards, widened for calibrated attribution uncertainty and drift. A comparison must exceed that floor, and its whole uncertainty range must point one way; neither requirement is waived. Missing, stale, or inconsistent evidence produces a refusal rather than an optimistic result. The retained bracket-calibration corpus, comprising the a9 and a10 windows, provides the non-claim characterization: an approximately ±31 ms boundary shift across an approximately 33 W power swing can mis-attribute about one joule between phases. The method applies to one Apple-silicon machine, software stack (the complete hardware-and-software configuration), and measurement boundary (the physical energy domain included by the counter). The demonstration applies this discipline to phase-resolved measurements of two model sizes and will report whether each registered contrast is resolvable or must be refused.

## 1. Introduction

Energy numbers for large language model (LLM) inference now appear in leaderboards, systems papers, product comparisons, and policy debates. Published estimates for apparently similar queries can vary widely, yet the numbers are rarely accompanied by an error bar that says how small a difference the measurement method can actually distinguish. This omission is especially consequential on consumer hardware. Apple's *powermetrics* utility makes processor power estimates available without a laboratory meter and is therefore attractive for repeated experiments, but, to our knowledge, it has not been validated in published work as an instrument for separating the energy of LLM inference phases. Apple describes its outputs as estimates; treating them as exact readings can turn timing uncertainty at a phase boundary into a spurious energy difference.

The core difficulty is physical as much as statistical. Energy is the integral of power over time. An experiment may repeat consistently and still assign energy to the wrong phase if a power sample near the boundary between prompt processing and token generation is placed on the wrong side. Averaging more repetitions reduces random scatter but does not remove that attribution error. Slow changes in thermal or background state can add a second false difference over a long collection session.

Energy is obtained by integrating power over a declared interval, so the validity of an energy value depends on both the power reading and the interval boundaries. Formal benchmark methods make this a run-level question: instrument uncertainty, clock synchronization, measurement duration, invalid samples, fixed ranges, and battery behavior must be checked for the interval that produced the result [1] [2]. The full-system energy-efficiency benchmark lineage beginning with JouleSort established that an energy claim is incomplete without all of a declared workload, a metric of comparison, and rules for running the workload and measuring energy — and that those rules must fix the measurement boundary explicitly [3]. The central lesson across this lineage is that a meter specification alone does not validate a measurement.

Software-visible counters make repeated experiments practical, but they remain measurement systems rather than ground truth. Early full-system power-modeling work such as Mantis already validated model outputs against a measured reference before trusting them [4], and later validation work on Intel's Running Average Power Limit counters compares software readings with wall power, searches for lag, measures overhead and update behavior, controls thermal state, and limits claims when no reference instrument observes the quantity of interest [5] [6]. A wall meter can test whole-machine totals. It cannot by itself determine whether a software sample belongs to prompt processing or token generation, so phase attribution requires a separate timing experiment.

Recent LLM studies report energy across models, hardware, and workloads, sometimes separating prompt processing from token generation [7] [8]. This work establishes the value of phase-resolved reporting, but a phase label is easy to read as exact when the method does not publish its boundary events, alignment uncertainty, or minimum resolvable effect. The conceptual gap is therefore not another energy table; it is a method that connects phase boundaries, counter behavior, drift, uncertainty, and refusal in one claim decision.

We have found no published work that combines phase-resolved LLM energy on consumer silicon, a per-measurement error budget, and validation of *powermetrics* timing attribution under a named measurement boundary. Nearby results sharpen rather than close that gap: Apple vs. Oranges uses *powermetrics* for Apple-silicon efficiency measurements without calibrating phase attribution [9]; Prima.cpp's appendix records energy with *powermetrics* but provides no calibration or uncertainty treatment [10]; AgentStop, a 2026 agentic-LLM study at a peer-reviewed venue, uses the same instrument at the same 100 ms cadence, integrates by trapezoid with an idle subtraction, and claims a directional 15–20% energy saving without calibration or uncertainty treatment — exactly the situation a detection floor is meant to gate [11]; DualScale acknowledges that NVML's averaged coarse-interval samples “do not align exactly with individual decode iterations” [12]; and Benazir and Lin characterize latency, throughput, and microarchitecture on the same Apple-silicon hardware but report no energy [13]. Energy-aware neural-architecture-search guidelines further show that software counter choice can materially disagree with an external meter [14]. JouleWise makes instrument characterization and refusal behavior the primary result; model comparisons are demonstrations of what the characterized implementation can and cannot resolve.

We argue that a software power counter should be treated as a scientific instrument rather than as a logging convenience. The instrument must be calibrated in the same session in which it is used; each reported result must carry a detection floor; and the analysis must decline a directional claim when the observed effect cannot pass both the floor and direction gates. JouleWise implements this discipline for phase-resolved, single-request LLM inference on one named Apple-silicon machine and software stack. The runtime emits the phase boundaries because it drives the workload, while *powermetrics* supplies the power samples integrated between those boundaries.

This scope is deliberately narrow. A measurement characterizes one physical unit, operating-system build, runtime and library stack, model artifact, quantization (the numerical representation used for model weights), tokenizer (the mapping between text and token IDs), token-selection policy, single-request execution policy, telemetry backend, and measurement boundary. It does not establish that an Apple hardware class or vendor is more efficient than another platform. Without an external power meter, absolute values remain internal to the named *powermetrics* system-on-chip boundary; same-boundary contrasts can still be scientifically useful when they pass the calibration and floor gates. Gross joules per request are the primary energy metric. Joules per prompt or output token are tokenizer-scoped companion metrics and are never treated as tokenizer-independent work units.

This paper makes the following contributions:

1. An in-window calibration method that measures timing-attribution error for phase-resolved energy integration.
2. Detection floors composed from measured repeatability, worst-case attribution, and measured drift, published with every eligible result.
3. A fail-closed collection protocol with admission gates, counterbalanced ordering, single-authority hash-bound custody, pre-registration, and a refusal log.
4. A planned characterization program for within-stack workload-response, null response across magnitudes, empirical floor behavior, phase-attribution consistency, drift, and settling; the micro-delta slots, exact settling comparison, and authoritative result schema remain unfinished.
5. A pre-registered demonstration design for phase-resolved measurements of two model sizes, with a registered token-generation contrast and a registered prompt-processing contrast using a fixed 256-token prompt.
6. An open tool and a planned release of hash-bound evidence artifacts that support re-reduction and detection of departures from the published bytes.

## 2. In-window calibration method

Terminology used below is fixed as follows. A *window* is one uninterrupted collection session governed by one power state, instrument identity, calibration bracket, drift record, and final verdict. A *calibration bracket* is the authenticated pre-window and post-window calibration evidence that surrounds a science window. A *stage* is one ordered collection step within a window. An *arm* is one pre-registered workload or comparison track within a campaign. A *campaign* is the frozen plan that groups related windows, stages, and arms. A *cell* is one condition family that shares the declared telemetry backend, metric, interval type, workload profile, and stack identity. A *member* is one recorded workload run, and its *bundle* is the self-contained record of configuration, raw power trace, runtime events, environment observations, and derived summary. *Claim-bearing* evidence is eligible to support a reported claim, whereas *diagnostic* evidence can inform design or interpretation but cannot authorize one. A *verdict* is the final governed decision to admit or refuse the declared evidence. *Reduction* is the governed transformation of preserved raw evidence into derived results; *re-reduction* repeats that transformation under updated authorized inputs without altering the source evidence.

### Measurement model and boundary

JouleWise measures one sequential request at a time. The two phases are also called *prefill* and *decode*, respectively. Because the experiment controls the runtime, it records the start and stop of these phases directly in the runtime event stream rather than inferring them from shapes in the power trace. The reducer then integrates the named *powermetrics* system-on-chip power channels between each pair of phase events. Each sampled value is treated as the average power over its reported sampling interval, so the integral is a sum of interval-average power multiplied by interval duration, with the boundary intervals clipped at the phase events.

Bundles and ABBA blocks are the analysis units; samples and sub-items within them are not additional replicates.

The runtime and the telemetry sampler do not share a perfect clock. Operating-system launch latency, timestamp anchoring, and the sampler's averaging behavior can shift an apparent edge. A phase-energy estimate must therefore be an interval, not only a point. The calibration asks a concrete question: if software commands a load pulse at a known time, how far can the rising and falling edges of that pulse appear displaced in the sampled power trace?

### Bracketed pulse-train calibration

The runbook places calibration stages adjacent to each claim-bearing science window. Executable admission requires authenticated causal pre/post endpoints whose horizons cover the complete window and are no more than 24 hours from the opposite window endpoint. The released calibration protocol configuration commands 59 graphics-processor matrix-multiplication pulses and specifies quiet baselines of at least 4.5 seconds between fitted regions. Pulse durations are fixed in advance, and the gaps follow a deterministic low-discrepancy schedule, an ordering designed to spread gap positions evenly rather than repeat one period. The varied schedule reduces the risk that calibration accidentally locks to the telemetry sampler's cadence. Each capture reports the maximum of 59 pulse-level worst-edge residual bounds, plus the capture-local trace-anchor bound. If those 59 pulse-level statistics are independent, identically distributed draws from a specified stationary calibration-pulse population, the sample maximum exceeds that population's 95th percentile with probability \(1-0.95^{59}=0.9515\). The current protocol does not establish that independence or exchangeability, so absent a separate dependence justification the quantity is a conservative 59-pulse empirical maximum; transfer to science edges remains an additional assumption.

For each pulse, the estimator compares the commanded interval with the observed power plateau and fits the start-edge and stop-edge lags jointly. The accepted joint evidence is conservatively reduced to one symmetric worst-edge scalar. A common shift of both edges may leave pulse energy nearly unchanged, while an early start combined with a late stop can add energy at both boundaries. The calibration therefore retains a bound for the instrument's edge-placement error rather than relying on one best-fit lag.

Each member also carries a local bound on how its runtime clock is anchored to the trace, including the observed span between wall time and a monotonic clock that is not adjusted by network synchronization. For a reported phase, the analysis combines the member-local common shift, the calibration edge bound, and the clock-span term. It evaluates the energy integral at all four combinations of early and late start and stop edges, while scanning the allowed common shift without approximation. Together these possibilities are the *admissible set*, the energy values permitted by the calibrated timing bounds; their minimum and maximum form the reported interval. The physical intuition is simple: the interval asks how much energy could move into or out of the phase if both boundaries were placed at their most adverse calibrated positions.

The pre- and post-window calibrations form a bracket around the measurements. Both must be authenticated, fresh, and causally outside the science interval. The operative timing bound is

\[
B_{\mathrm{op}}=\max(B_{\mathrm{pre}},B_{\mathrm{post}})+\max(|B_{\mathrm{pre}}-B_{\mathrm{post}}|,10.818\ \mathrm{ms}).
\]

The timing allowance is the full \(\max(|B_{\mathrm{pre}}-B_{\mathrm{post}}|,10.818\ \mathrm{ms})\), not merely excess above the screen, and is added once to the larger endpoint bound. The 10.818 ms value is the genesis lower bound used by the currently selected issued artifact: a conservative policy floor derived from the observed range of 19 historical same-epoch bounds, not a probabilistic drift confidence limit. Successor artifacts use a lineage-monotone envelope that may only strengthen it. Drift above 12.093166 ms refuses the window. The full allowance is propagated into every floor and claim for ordinary repeatability scatter, while a pre-flight systematic failure is never budgeted. If the post-window bound is larger than the one used when a member was first reduced, the member must be reduced again through the pre-specified path with the wider bound or it cannot support a claim; metadata is never patched to make the bounds agree. A *pre-flight screen*, an admission check run before the first science member, rejects a calibration whose composite timing bound lies outside the previously characterized condition family, such as when a graphics-processor frequency ramp is absorbed by the pulse model as apparent edge displacement. A retry is allowed only after a specific cause is identified and removed, within the retry count frozen before collection. Repeating merely until a favorable calibration appears would be selection on the outcome.

## 3. Detection-floor composition

No two runs are physically identical: temperature history, hardware frequency decisions, background work, and power-sample window placement can each produce joule differences when no real effect is present. The floor is the largest difference the measurement system can manufacture on its own under the admitted conditions. A smaller measured difference is therefore not a small effect; it is a number the instrument could produce with no effect at all.

The *detection floor* is a practical guard against false observed effects under a cell's condition family, not a claim that a population percentile has been estimated exactly. JouleWise computes separate absolute and comparative floors and takes their maximum for the cell. An absolute floor measures how far repeated measurements of the same condition wander from their mean. A comparative floor measures the apparent difference between labels that are deliberately made identical and collected in A/B/B/A order.

### Repeatability and false-comparison guards

For an absolute cell, \(E_i\) is the energy in joules of the \(i\)th valid bundle, \(\bar E\) is the mean energy, \(r_i=E_i-\bar E\) is its residual, \(s_r\) is the sample standard deviation of those residuals, and \(n\) is the number of valid bundles. The point repeatability guard is

\[
F_{\mathrm{abs,point}}=\max\left(\max_i |r_i|,
t_{0.975,n-1}s_r\sqrt{1+1/n}\right).
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{abs,point}}\) is the absolute point guard in joules, and \(t_{0.975,n-1}\) is the 97.5th-percentile critical value of Student's \(t\) distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom.

For comparison, one A/B/B/A (ABBA) block contains runs \(A_1,B_1,B_2,A_2\). Its signed difference is

\[
\delta_i=(B_1+B_2-A_1-A_2)/2,
\]

so a positive \(\delta_i\) means condition B used more energy than condition A. For \(n\) valid blocks, \(\bar\delta\) is the mean block difference and \(s_\delta\) is its sample standard deviation. The comparative point guard is

\[
F_{\mathrm{cmp,point}}=\max\left(\max_i |\delta_i|,
|\bar\delta|+t_{0.975,n-1}s_\delta\sqrt{1+1/n}\right).
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{cmp,point}}\) is the comparative point guard in joules, and the Student-\(t\) subscript has the same meaning as above. The observed maxima protect against a false effect already seen; the prediction terms protect against one additional bundle or block. That Student-\(t\) prediction component is conditional on approximately independent, stationary, Gaussian bundle residuals or block contrasts after cooldown; the admission gates do not establish that model. Shared calibration and drift terms are propagated separately as deterministic bounds. Small samples receive a pre-registered *guard factor*, a multiplicative safety margin, and fewer than five valid bundles or blocks are treated only as development evidence, not as a claim gate.

### Worst-case timing attribution

Point repeatability is not the full floor. Each energy value is an interval from the timing calibration in Section 2. The floor computation evaluates the complete point-floor estimator over the joint *corners* of all admitted member intervals—the combinations of their extreme endpoints—and then takes the largest value. For a null ABBA block, the block interval is first formed from the four signed member intervals. Onset and offset, and at floor level the member intervals, are allowed to take independently adverse endpoints. This Cartesian construction deliberately ignores favorable covariance or common-mode cancellation: it is a deterministic adversarial decision bound, not a confidence interval or a root-sum-square combination, where root-sum-square means quadrature addition premised on independent terms. It may admit combinations excluded by the joint fit or common-mode physics. Exact floor enumeration currently supports at most 16 admitted bundles or blocks and otherwise refuses. The published component is no smaller than the largest value attained by the complete residual or contrast floor estimator over the admitted Cartesian product. For absolute cells, mean-centering means that value is not guaranteed to equal or exceed the largest raw member energy half-width.

This calculation revealed a stable and important limitation of the present implementation. Prompt processing saturates compute while token generation waits more heavily on memory, so power can fall steeply exactly where their boundary is drawn. Because energy is power multiplied by time, placing that boundary roughly 30 ms late across a 33 W step assigns \(0.030\ \mathrm{s}\times33\ \mathrm{W}\approx1\ \mathrm{J}\) to the wrong phase. The request total is unchanged, which is why averaging cannot repair the attribution, and the retained bracket-calibration corpus—the a9 and a10 windows—shows the same phenomenon at corpus precision: an approximately ±31 ms boundary shift across an approximately 33 W power change mis-attributed about one joule between phases, while ordinary repeatability was smaller than the uncertainty caused by placing samples at phase edges. The instrument is therefore *attribution-limited*, not *noise-limited*. More repetitions can refine the repeatability term, but they cannot average away this boundary-placement limit. ### Measured, never-zero drift allowance

Drift is a slow change in the machine or measurement response over the collection window. It is measured rather than assumed away. The reference schedule is pre-registered per campaign: the two floor campaigns use three fixed references at the start, one at the midpoint, and three at the end, whereas the contrast campaign uses nine references — three at the start, singles after the 20th, 40th, and 60th science members, and three at the end. Gross energy and idle-subtracted energy are separate whole-window claim families. For a request, idle-subtracted energy is gross request energy minus the measured idle mean power multiplied by the measured request-window duration; phase energy remains gross-only because no phase-idle model is defined. For each family, the protocol derives a repeatability bound from a settled reference corpus and measures the largest *excursion*, or departure, among the reference means. The allowance is

\[
A_{\mathrm{drift}}=\max(\text{observed start/mid/end excursion},
\text{derived reference-repeatability bound}).
\]

The subscript identifies this as the drift allowance. Consequently, a passing drift screen never means zero drift. The allowance remains positive even in an exceptionally stable window, and the midpoint protects against an interior excursion that similar endpoints would miss. The start, midpoint, and end points used to construct the maximum document the allowance but cannot validate it; containment must be evaluated on held-out probes or later sessions. No duration-scaling law is applied because the available evidence does not identify a physical law relating drift to elapsed time.

For each absolute or comparative component, the guarded, *corner-widened* value—the value maximized over those endpoint combinations—is increased once by its matching drift allowance. Let \(F_{\mathrm{abs}}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{cmp}}\) denote those final absolute and comparative energy magnitudes in joules. The operative floor for a cell is

\[
F_{\mathrm{cell}}=\max(F_{\mathrm{abs}},F_{\mathrm{cmp}}),
\]

where \(F_{\mathrm{cell}}\) is also an energy magnitude in joules. The components are not summed. Cross-window components keep their own calibration basis and allowance; an allowance is never added again at the cell or reporting level. Operative floor values and their full decomposition for each demonstration stack are withheld until issued artifacts are available: **[RESULT PENDING ISSUED ARTIFACTS]**. An earlier reduction was superseded by a corrected one; the release notes identify it.

### Publication label and the two claim gates

When timing attribution dominates, a floor may be published with an explicit attribution-limited label only if that dominance is the sole otherwise-refusing condition and an exact corner-widened floor exists. The published result identifies the dominant source as energy uncertainty caused by shifting the phase edges within the calibrated clock-anchor bound. It publishes the corner-widened value rather than the smaller point diagnostic and retains the point-only repeatability value as a diagnostic that cannot support a claim. The label never rescues any other refusal: missing, stale, contaminated, or inconsistent evidence remains terminal.

A claim then faces two separate gates, enforced separately. First, the magnitude of its point estimate must exceed \(F_{\mathrm{cell}}\), the cell floor. Second, both endpoints of its *fully composed decision interval*—the interval after all registered statistical, attribution, and drift terms have been included—must support the pre-registered direction. Let \(B_{\mathrm{claim}}\) be the *claim-side bound*, the uncertainty carried by this particular comparison as distinct from the cell's floor. The project also discloses

\[
\text{practical joint-clearance size}=F_{\mathrm{cell}}+B_{\mathrm{claim}}.
\]

For the measured phase-contrast regime, this practical sizing quantity is approximately five joules. It explains how large an effect will generally need to be to clear both gates together and ensures that neither physical uncertainty term is hidden as an apparent double count. It is not a single summed acceptance threshold, and the decision interval is not compared with the sum. Effects that fail the floor gate are reported as *not resolvable*, not as zero, equal, or evidence of no difference. Effects that pass the floor gate but whose interval does not support direction remain unresolved and receive no directional claim.

## 4. Fail-closed collection protocol

*Fail-closed* means that missing, inconsistent, stale, or contaminated evidence produces a refusal rather than an optimistic default. The collection unit is the measurement window defined in Section 2. Work that does not fit within two to four hours with at least a 20% failure margin is split prospectively into another independently calibrated window.

### Pre-registration and admission

Before quiet time, the operator freezes the run identifiers, membership, stage order, comparison definitions, the registered estimator's name and parameter hash, calibration retry count, acceptance thresholds, extraction specification, code revision, and an initially empty waiver list. Every configuration is validated and dry-run against a fresh collection directory. These choices are made before outcomes are visible. Pre-registration here is executable: the launcher and final verifier check the frozen values rather than relying on a narrative promise.

Prospective sizing is part of that freeze. Before collection, the planned effect, workload, and number of independent bundles or ABBA blocks are compared with the cell floor and the claim-side interval width. If the design has too little expected clearance, the operator must prospectively increase independent evidence, change the workload, or narrow the claim; the choice cannot be made after seeing claim data. A workload change is not merely a sensitivity adjustment. For example, lengthening the prompt changes the population of requests being estimated and therefore changes the *estimand*, the quantity the design aims to estimate.

Each stage then passes admission gates immediately before measurement. The approved power supply and power policy must be unchanged; displays must be asleep; the screensaver and low-power mode must be off; thermal pressure must be nominal; and central- and graphics-processor activity must meet the quiet-state criteria. Known background work is allowed to finish before the window, followed by a settling interval, rather than being ignored during analysis. Runtime and telemetry identities, clock anchoring, sample cadence, calibration freshness, and post-run environment observations are also checked. No environment override can produce a claim-bearing member.

### Counterbalanced order

Comparisons use the ABBA blocks defined in Section 3. Conditions A and B are measured in the order A, B, B, A, with ordinary cooldown between members. A machine measured over an hour warms and shifts; ABBA counterbalances order and gives the A and B runs the same average position in time only when the A and B observation-center times have equal sums. When member durations and gaps are balanced, steady drift therefore largely subtracts. The present protocol does not enforce exact time symmetry because member runtimes and recovery-gated cooldowns are unequal and variable, so ABBA is expected to reduce, not guarantee cancellation of, smooth within-block drift, and curvature survives. Actual time imbalance should be reported or incorporated as a covariate; the midpoint reference and separate whole-window drift allowance remain required. For null-floor calibration, A and B are aliases of the same configuration and payload, so any nonzero block difference is a false comparative effect. For scientific contrasts, the A and B definitions are frozen in advance, and the contrast is computed within each block before blocks are aggregated.

### Evidence custody and refusals

The bundle defined in Section 2 is the basic custody object. Cryptographic hashes bind the claim-bearing files of each bundle to the campaign record; the binding covers the files the analysis consumes, and the final verdict then fixes the exact member set, so substituting or omitting evidence after collection is detectable against the retained campaign record. Failed or interrupted artifacts are never deleted or overwritten. Each preserved attempt to collect a declared member is a *recorded occurrence*. Moving an occupied retry slot to quarantine outside the active collection directory is an operator action that the recording tools validate rather than an automatic one; the member is recollected only under an allowed retry; and an append-only supersession record states which recorded occurrence governs. Two present bundles claiming to be the same recorded occurrence cause a refusal. The final whole-window verdict binds the complete declared member set, its preserved attempts and supersessions, the calibration bracket, policy, and drift evidence by hash, so a later consumer cannot silently select a more favorable subset.

Collection does not continue indefinitely through environmental interruptions. Each stage attempt stops at its first member failure, and after the same cause fails a window stage for the third time the window itself closes rather than being retried. In actual operation, nights with three environmental member interruptions were closed and preserved as salvage rather than repeatedly retried. The surviving data may remain diagnostic, but it becomes claim-bearing only if the pre-registered verdict and extraction rules accept the complete resulting member set. This abandon-after-three rule limits outcome-dependent retrying and makes the cost of a noisy environment visible.

Custody — the requirement that every claim trace back to evidence whose alteration would be detectable against retained records — continues after collection. The corpus is written once by the tools, bound by hashes, and backed up before extraction. The extractor checks member hashes, required files and cross-file consistency, calibration identity, environment admission, complete campaign membership, cooldown evidence, phase metric identity, and the whole-window drift allowance. It excludes or refuses a cell according to the frozen rules; missing uncertainty never becomes zero. The result is single-authority, hash-bound custody: the tools rederive hashes and refuse omissions, substitutions, stale inputs, and rollback relative to the retained commitments. These controls do not prove that the experimenter who controls the laptop, repository, and archive did not fabricate the records or rewrite the repository and archive together before publication; the present system has no independent signer, external timestamp, or write-once log. The hashes bind the evidence consumed by the analysis and make post-hoc substitution detectable relative to the disclosed digests, but the root of the chain is the experimenters' own committed repository. Specifically, the floor-issuing tool (the *mint*) compares its inputs against the frozen list of expected input digests (the *pin set*), whose checksum the operator supplies on the command line, and authenticates the recorded head commit of the calibration record against the operator's own local repository copy. For v2 issuance, the issuing tool derives the project commit and tree state from git, refuses a dirty tree, and validates supplied labels against the derived state. It also records whether `origin/main` contains the project head and whether it contains the commit that last changed the recorded head commit of the calibration record; either containment fact may be recorded as unknown rather than causing refusal. This is a deliberate scope for a single-operator instrument: it creates a hard gate with specific recorded reasons against honest operator error, while fabrication would require forging a self-consistent committed history that survives review of the pushed public repository. A claim-bearing floor must be produced by the specified extraction path and consumed in the controlled analysis run; a standalone floor or result artifact cannot authorize a claim.

The refusal log is part of the evaluation, not an embarrassment to omit. It records contaminated members, calibration outside the admitted condition family, stale drift evidence, unresolved clock anchors, duplicate recorded occurrences, and below-floor effects. In one real end-of-night case, governed re-evaluation refused a window because a member's internal clock alignment could not be resolved. Independent adjudication found that refusal correct. The result remained non-claim-bearing: an example of the fail-closed design working as intended, not a software defect to be bypassed.

The workload will serve as a test signal rather than as the scientific finding. Each objective will retain the calibration, admission, custody, and floor rules used for the later demonstration. ## 6. Demonstration results

The planned demonstration will ask a deliberately modest scientific question: on one named consumer stack, how does phase-resolved energy per request differ between two sizes of the same model family, and can each registered difference pass its own floor and direction gates? The two conditions will be instruction-tuned models of the same family at roughly 1.5 and 7 billion parameters, both 4-bit quantized and served by the same runtime. The models share frozen prompt bytes and output policy within each arm: the token-generation arm uses the frozen 128-token prompt (p128), while the separate prompt-processing arm uses the fixed synthetic 256-token prompt (p256). Demonstration measurements will be collected in dedicated quiet windows under Section 4, with the floors of Section 3 derived from the protocol-controlled floor windows.

### Pre-registered design

Each model size will receive its own token-generation floor cell, and the prompt-processing contrast will receive dedicated 256-token floor cells on both stacks. Each floor design includes an absolute-repeatability arm and a null ABBA arm and is frozen before collection. The registered demonstration contains two model-size directional contrasts: one for token generation and one for prompt processing under a fixed synthetic 256-token prompt. They form one Holm multiple-testing family of two tests at an overall significance level of 0.05. Each contrast will be computed within its own ABBA blocks whose A and B are the two model sizes, on the frozen phase-energy metric. Its floor gate will compare the point-estimate magnitude with the cell floor; its separate direction gate will ask whether both endpoints of the composed interval support the registered direction.

### Prospective workload sizing

A non-claim diagnostic analysis of earlier captures informed prospective workload sizing. For the natural short prompt, its projected clearance was marginal against the sizing disclosure. This was a prospective design warning, not an issued refusal: no fresh demonstration contrast has been measured or refused. The token-generation contrast showed substantially more prospective clearance.

The registered prompt-processing arm adopts a fixed 256-token prompt whose projected contrast is roughly twice the sizing disclosure. The prompt is frozen before collection and produces identical token IDs under both model tokenizers. The projection extrapolates from prompt-length scaling observed on the smaller model; no long-prompt evidence exists for the larger model, so the fresh arm will be its first direct test. Lengthening the prompt also changes the estimand, as Section 4 explains. If the collected contrast does not clear its gates, the protocol will report the refusal rather than omit the arm.

## 7. Discussion and limitations

The attribution-limited resolution item below explains why boundary placement, rather than scatter, sets the experimental priority for this implementation.

A published refusal is scientifically useful because it maps the operating domain of the measurement method. A contaminated window, an unresolved clock anchor, or a contrast that fails a gate is not evidence that two systems are equal. It is evidence that the named implementation cannot support that claim under the recorded conditions. Preserving the failed evidence and its reason prevents selective reporting and helps later work distinguish a small physical effect from an inadequate measurement.

Prompt length illustrates why feasibility is tied to the estimand. A longer prompt can increase prompt-processing energy and make a contrast easier to resolve, but it answers a different question about a different request population. Feasibility therefore means that a pre-registered design can adjudicate the effect it actually names, not that the workload can be enlarged until any difference becomes visible. Beyond this Apple-silicon stack, the transferable contribution is the method—calibrate boundary attribution, measure drift, separate floor and direction gates, and preserve refusals—not the numerical floor, which must be established again for each instrument, boundary, and stack.

**Attribution-limited resolution.** The dominant uncertainty on the studied implementation is boundary placement, not scatter. In the retained bracket-calibration corpus—the a9 and a10 windows—an approximately ±31 ms boundary shift across an approximately 33 W power swing mis-attributed about one joule between phases, and repetitions do not reduce it. When attribution dominance is the sole otherwise-refusing condition and an exact corner-widened floor exists, the result may be published with an explicit attribution-limited label; that label never rescues any other refusal. The floor plus claim-side bound remains an approximately five-joule practical sizing disclosure, not one summed acceptance threshold. For a historical non-claim diagnostic of the short-prompt model-size contrast, its projected clearance was marginal against the sizing disclosure. That diagnostic motivated the registered fixed-256-token prompt-processing arm and the funded dedicated floor cells for both stacks; it was not a fresh measurement or an issued refusal. The fresh contrast can still be refused if it does not clear the registered gates. The resulting limitation concerns what this implementation, on the named stack and boundary, can support.

**One unit, one stack, one boundary.** Every result is scoped to one physical machine, operating-system build, runtime and library stack, model artifact, quantization, tokenizer, token-selection and output policy, telemetry backend, and measurement boundary. Nothing here ranks hardware classes or vendors, and the whole-system-on-chip boundary of *powermetrics* is not comparable to a graphics-board or wall boundary without a calibrated bridge.

**No external meter.** The calibration validates timing attribution—whether samples near a commanded edge land inside or outside a phase integral. It does not validate the counter's absolute gain or whole-system scale; absolute joule values remain internal to the named software boundary. A wall-power instrument could test totals but would still not adjudicate the division of a total between phases, which is the question the pulse-train experiment answers.

**Operator-attested provenance.** The custody chain is deliberately scoped to a single-operator instrument: its hashes and git bindings detect post-hoc substitution relative to disclosed or independently retained digests and guard against honest operator error, but its root remains under the experimenters' control. For v2 issuance, the issuing tool derives the project commit and tree state from git, refuses a dirty tree, and validates supplied labels against the derived state. It records whether `origin/main` contains the project head and whether it contains the commit that last changed the recorded head commit of the calibration record; either containment fact may be recorded as unknown rather than causing refusal. It therefore provides machine-checked internal consistency and tamper evidence relative to those retained digests, not machine-verified or third-party-verifiable provenance. Fabrication would still require a self-consistent committed history able to survive review of the pushed public repository.

**Load-regime transfer.** The calibration pulses are graphics-processor matrix multiplications under light central-processor load; transferring their timing bound to sustained mixed-load inference is an explicit assumption, constrained by the in-window bracket and the planned empirical floor probes but not eliminated. The drift allowance is measured per window with no duration-scaling law because the evidence does not identify one; longer windows than those characterized would need fresh drift evidence.

**Estimator applicability and stationarity.** The registered common-mode contrast estimator assumes that the two calibration-edge errors remain shared over the timescale of one ABBA block and transfer to the science contrast. The retained bracket-calibration corpus bounds onset and offset spans; it does not record the realized boundary error of each member, so it cannot verify that shared-error assumption member by member. The estimator is exact only when every measured phase window is strictly longer than twice its timing bound, using outward floating-point rounding. Geometry outside that domain is refused with a typed reason and is never estimated: a short or ambiguous window produces no contrast floor rather than an unsound one. If the estimator was not registered for a cell, the analysis instead uses the worst-case composition and says so.

**Sampling resolution.** The power sampler's approximately 112 ms cadence is comparable to the shortest measured prompt-processing windows on the smaller model: 0.121–0.147 seconds for the retained 128-token prompts, with many windows overlapping only two power intervals. The pipeline labels those windows not resolvable at the sampler's cadence rather than claiming resolution the instrument lacks, and the same pressure is expected for the 256-token cells on the smaller model. This is a limit on resolution within a correctly identified phase window, not phase mislabeling. The phase-recording proof establishes exact boundary identification for the inspected corpus: raw-trace reconstruction and independent reintegration reproduced every stored prompt-processing energy with 0.0 J discrepancy, every prompt-processing interval ended before token generation began, and phase overlap was zero.

**Sequential requests only.** Phase boundaries are well posed because one request runs at a time. Continuous batching, speculative decoding, and multi-request serving overlap phases by design and are out of scope; the method would need a defensible boundary definition before it could be extended there.

**Operational constraints as evidence.** Claim-bearing windows require a machine quiescent to the admission gates—no interactive agent sessions, displays asleep, and background work settled. Characterization captures of the idle environment, kept permanently non-claim-bearing, show a low but bursty floor whose excursions, not its mean, threaten a several-joule practical clearance size; idle subtraction cancels only the steady part. The zero-agent window rule is therefore load-bearing, and results collected outside it would not be admitted.

## 8. Related work

### Energy-benchmarking lineage

Energy benchmarking has a two-decade methodological lineage that JouleWise inherits rather than replaces. JouleSort argued that an energy-efficiency benchmark is incomplete unless it fixes a workload, a metric of comparison, and rules for running the workload and measuring energy, and it made the measurement boundary a first-class part of those rules: all power taken at the wall including conversion losses, every participating component counted even when idle, and any net change in stored battery energy shown to be no greater than zero with 95% confidence or included in the total [3]. It also named, without quantifying, the failure mode this paper measures: it rejected a fixed-energy-budget metric partly because of “inaccuracies in synchronizing readings from a power meter to the actual runs,” alongside the meter's own ±1.5% specification. Industry benchmarks then codified those run rules: SPECpower_ssj2008 fixed a graduated-load server workload with an accepted-analyzer and reporting discipline [15]; the SPEC methodology bounds analyzer uncertainty at 1% AC over the ranges actually measured, forbids automatic range transitions because uncertainty is undefined during them, and requires unknown or over-uncertainty samples to be detected and reported rather than silently averaged [2]; TPC-Energy added an audited energy metric to transaction-processing benchmarks [16]; and the Green500 ranked supercomputers by performance per watt [17]. Full-system power modeling took the complementary route of validating an estimate against a measured reference: Mantis calibrated once per system against measured AC power and was validated on two very different servers to within roughly 10% [4], and later work compared several such high-level models across machines from laptop to server [18]. JouleWise sits inside this tradition but differs on three axes that change what must be published: its boundary is a software counter internal to the system-on-chip rather than a wall meter, its unit of claim is a sub-request phase rather than a whole run, and the uncertainty it must disclose is not an analyzer specification but a measured bound on where a phase edge can fall. That is why the detection floor, rather than the joule value, is the primary result — and why the timing-synchronization concern JouleSort raised in passing becomes, at phase granularity on an uncalibrated software counter, the dominant term.

### LLM inference energy measurement

TokenPowerBench provides one of the closest reporting structures: it reports prefill energy and decode energy-per-token and groups results by context length [7]. Its disclosed method, however, does not specify the boundary events, alignment rule, repetition and variance protocol, decode-token denominator, or external validation; no idle-baseline subtraction is defined; and batch sizes are swept from 32 to 1024 under vLLM and TensorRT-LLM, so requests overlap by construction. JouleWise adopts phase-specific reporting but restricts its primary scope to one sequential request, where runtime-emitted phase boundaries are well posed and can be calibrated.

Ruf and Detyniecki isolate prefill by generating exactly one token and derive decode by subtraction, using a single run per context length with no error bars [19] — a fresh prefill-focused instance of phase separation by workload construction rather than calibrated boundary events.

The Illusion of Power Capping in LLM Decode samples graphics-processor power, integrates those samples, repeats configurations, and cross-checks sufficiently long operations against a separate hardware energy counter [20]. This is a stronger measurement template than an unqualified software reading. Its counter agreement, run-to-run variation, and sub-100 ms snapshot-fallback regime nevertheless remain separate diagnostics rather than one bound on the reported effect. It monitors die temperature (42–45 °C under its clamp), but does not counterbalance run order. JouleWise composes those classes of uncertainty into a decision rule for each contrast.

Apple-focused system comparisons reinforce the need for boundary labels. Silicon Showdown compares a graphics-board boundary on one platform with a whole-system-on-chip *powermetrics* boundary on Apple hardware, while runtimes and precision stacks also differ [21]. Intelligence per Watt and ML.ENERGY similarly emphasize breadth across deployed inference configurations [22] [8]. Such studies answer valuable systems questions, but because their measurement boundaries and software stacks differ, they cannot by themselves support a hardware-class causal ranking. JouleWise therefore makes the named stack and measurement boundary part of every result and prioritizes within-boundary effects.

### Software power counters and measurement standards

RAPL in Action established a validation agenda for commodity software counters: align lag before comparing streams, model wall power rather than assuming identity, account for temporal correlation, warm the system, measure sampler overhead, and inspect update granularity, overflow, jitter, and timestamp behavior [5]. Jay and Ostapenco likewise regress software readings against wall power and show that disagreement varies with load; they avoid claims about subtotals that the reference meter cannot observe [6]. Dauner et al. characterize RAPL and NVML counter self-consistency against sampling cadence with a wall-meter reference: NVML cumulative counters update no faster than approximately 100 ms, and a flagship GPU's counter underestimates integrated power by up to 95% at 0.5 ms sampling, recovering only at 1 s [23] — independently establishing on other counters that a software counter's update cadence can silently bias energy readings and corroborating this paper's treatment of the approximately 112 ms *powermetrics* cadence as a first-order instrument limit; they characterize whole-workload counter behavior without phase boundaries, whereas JouleWise characterizes placement of a runtime-defined boundary within a request and turns it into a claim gate. Cao et al. provide an NLP-side empirical prior: software counters carry 2–47% error against a hardware meter, with software variance twice the meter's [24]; like [5] and [6], this is total-energy evidence only, so the wall-meter-cannot-adjudicate-phases hinge remains. Panigrahy and Tyagi supply the sibling audit of a platform where per-process attribution is impossible because no counter exists [25]; JouleWise shows that a counter's presence is not sufficient — its timing behavior must be characterized before it licenses phase attribution, and absent that characterization the correct output is refusal. JouleWise follows this epistemic boundary. A future external meter could test total gain and load dependence, but it would not validate how a total is divided between prompt processing and token generation. The in-window pulse train addresses that distinct question.

MLPerf Power supplies the broad benchmark setting and public-run discipline, including NTP clock synchronization between the system under test and the Director [1]. The associated SPEC Power and Performance Methodology supplies the load-specific analyzer uncertainty, fixed ranges, minimum intervals, invalid-sample accounting, and controlled battery behavior attributed here [2]. JouleWise translates their per-run reject-on-missing-evidence principle to a consumer software counter. Its principal uncertainty is not only an analyzer specification; it includes whether samples at a software-defined phase edge belong inside or outside the integral.

### Metrology and experimental discipline

Prospective paired minimum-detectable-effect methods estimate the smallest effect a planned paired study has adequate statistical power to find. They size a study from paired variation and can impose a one-way prior ratchet: observed variability may raise the planned threshold but may not lower it after outcomes are seen [26]. JouleWise uses that logic for prospective design, but it does not treat the worst-case attribution term as independent random noise. Root-sum-square composition would be anti-conservative for a systematic edge-placement bound. Instead, the analysis maximizes over calibrated edge positions, measures drift with bracketed references, and publishes the resulting floor with the effect.

This emphasis changes the role of failed runs. A rejected admission, stale calibration, contaminated member, or below-floor contrast is evidence about the instrument's operating domain. Hash-bound raw traces, runtime events, configurations, supersessions, verdicts, and pre-registration records make that evidence capable of being reduced again under the governed process. The refusal is retained rather than disappearing behind a final average.

### Split and disaggregated inference

Revisiting Disaggregated Large Language Model Serving reports essentially unconditionally higher energy for disaggregation on its evaluated two-graphics-processor node, while its performance benefits depend on load, baseline, and transfer conditions [27]. DualScale models graphics-processor-only phase energy by regression [12]; Prima.cpp reports whole-run but not stage energy [10]; and SplitZip makes no energy claim [28].

JouleWise claims no scheduler; split inference is a future instrument demonstration requiring both endpoints, named boundaries, cross-device clock alignment, and a pre-registered floor or calibrated bridge.

## 10. Conclusion

JouleWise treats a software power counter as a scientific instrument whose operating domain must be measured. The present evidence shows why: on the studied Apple-silicon stack, phase-boundary attribution can dominate repeatability, so more runs alone do not make a small contrast trustworthy. The final paper will report the planned characterization and two-model demonstration with separate floor and direction gates, including any refusal the recorded evidence requires.

The reusable contribution is the combination of in-window calibration, detection floors built from measured terms, prospective sizing, counterbalanced collection, fail-closed decisions, and single-authority hash-bound custody. Those methods may transfer to other software counters, but the numerical floor will not; every new instrument, boundary, and stack must establish its own. Future work can add independent stacks, model families, an external meter for total-energy scale, and richer phase structures such as speculative decoding, mixture-of-experts routing, and split inference.

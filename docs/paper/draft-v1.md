# JouleWise: Measuring the Smallest Phase-Energy Difference It Can Distinguish in LLM Inference on Apple Silicon

*Draft: demonstration values, instrument-characterization results, and archive locators are pending; bracketed markers show where they will land.*

## Abstract

Published energy figures for large language model (LLM) inference rarely say how small a difference the measurement could actually distinguish from error. That question becomes sharp when a request is split into prompt processing, which reads the input and builds the model's internal state, and token generation, which produces the output one token at a time. Power drops steeply at their boundary because prompt processing keeps the compute units busy while generation spends much of its time waiting on memory. Draw that boundary late and energy is charged to the wrong phase. The request total is unchanged, so repetition cannot repair the assignment. JouleWise therefore treats Apple's *powermetrics* counter as a scientific instrument. The runtime reports each phase boundary; the counter is calibrated in the same uninterrupted collection session; and every result allowed to support a claim carries a *detection floor*: the largest apparent difference this measurement system is allowed to produce when the two things compared have not physically changed. “Allowed” here means that every required power-state, calibration, timing, and file-integrity check passed. The floor is the larger of two measured guards, one from repeated runs of one condition and one from comparisons whose A and B labels deliberately name the same condition; it is widened for calibrated timing uncertainty and measured slow change during the session. A comparison must be larger than that floor, and its entire uncertainty interval must point in the direction fixed before collection. Missing, out-of-date, or inconsistent evidence produces a *refusal*: a recorded decision not to publish a result from that evidence. The retained characterization is diagnostic, meaning it explains instrument behavior but cannot support a scientific comparison. It comprises 30 phase-repeatability runs collected on 25 July 2026; the immediately preceding calibration and reference collection of 24–25 July supplies context. The combined phase-edge timing bounds span 25.6–31.1 ms, leaving phase-energy attribution uncertainty on the order of a joule. Everything reported holds for one Apple-silicon machine, one complete hardware-and-software configuration, and one measurement boundary, the processor power channels the counter reports. The planned demonstration applies the same rules to two model sizes and will report whether each comparison is distinguishable or refused. The comparison definitions, numeric pass limits, and analysis are fixed before data collection.

## 1. Introduction

Energy numbers for large language model (LLM) inference now appear in leaderboards, systems papers, product comparisons, and policy debates. Published estimates for apparently similar queries can vary widely, yet the numbers are rarely accompanied by an error bar that says how small a difference the measurement method can actually distinguish. This omission is especially consequential on consumer hardware. Apple's *powermetrics* utility makes processor power estimates available without a laboratory meter and is therefore attractive for repeated experiments, but, to our knowledge, it has not been validated in published work as an instrument for separating the energy of LLM inference phases. Apple describes its outputs as estimates; treating them as exact readings can turn timing uncertainty at a phase boundary into a spurious energy difference.

The core difficulty is physical as much as statistical. Energy is the integral of power over time. An experiment may repeat consistently and still assign energy to the wrong phase if a power sample near the boundary between prompt processing and token generation is placed on the wrong side. Averaging more repetitions reduces random scatter but does not remove that attribution error. Slow changes in thermal or background state can add a second false difference over a long collection session.

Energy is obtained by integrating power over a declared interval, so the validity of an energy value depends on both the power reading and the interval boundaries. Formal benchmark methods make this a run-level question: instrument uncertainty, clock synchronization, measurement duration, invalid samples, fixed ranges, and battery behavior must be checked for the interval that produced the result [1] [2]. The full-system energy-efficiency benchmark lineage beginning with JouleSort established that an energy claim is incomplete without all of a declared workload, a metric of comparison, and rules for running the workload and measuring energy — and that those rules must fix the measurement boundary explicitly [3]. The central lesson across this lineage is that a meter specification alone does not validate a measurement.

Software-visible counters make repeated experiments practical, but they remain measurement systems rather than ground truth. Early full-system power-modeling work such as Mantis already validated model outputs against a measured reference before trusting them [4], and later validation work on Intel's Running Average Power Limit (RAPL) counters compares software readings with wall power, searches for lag, measures overhead and update behavior, controls thermal state, and limits claims when no reference instrument observes the quantity of interest [5] [6]. A wall meter can test whole-machine totals. It cannot by itself determine whether a software sample belongs to prompt processing or token generation, so phase attribution requires a separate timing experiment.

Recent LLM studies report energy across models, hardware, and workloads, sometimes separating prompt processing from token generation [7] [8]. This work establishes the value of phase-resolved reporting, but a phase label is easy to read as exact when the method does not publish its boundary events, alignment uncertainty, or minimum resolvable effect. The conceptual gap is therefore not another energy table; it is a method that connects phase boundaries, counter behavior, drift, uncertainty, and refusal in one claim decision.

We have found no published work that does all three of these at once: report LLM energy separately for each phase on consumer hardware, attach an error budget to every measurement, and check how accurately *powermetrics* places events in time — all under a stated measurement boundary. Nearby results sharpen rather than close that gap. Apple vs. Oranges uses *powermetrics* for Apple-silicon efficiency measurements without calibrating phase attribution [9]. Prima.cpp's appendix records energy with *powermetrics* but provides no calibration or uncertainty treatment [10]. AgentStop, a 2026 agentic-LLM study at a peer-reviewed venue, uses the same instrument at the same 100 ms sampling interval and integrates by trapezoid with an idle subtraction. It claims a directional 15–20% energy saving without calibration or uncertainty treatment, exactly the situation a detection floor is meant to gate [11]. DualScale acknowledges that samples from the NVIDIA Management Library (NVML), NVIDIA's software interface to graphics-processor counters, “do not align exactly with individual decode iterations” [12]. Benazir and Lin characterize latency, throughput, and microarchitecture on the same Apple-silicon hardware but report no energy [13]. Energy-aware neural-architecture-search guidelines further show that software counter choice can materially disagree with an external meter [14]. JouleWise makes instrument characterization and refusal behavior the primary result. Model comparisons demonstrate what the characterized implementation can and cannot resolve.

We argue that a software power counter should be treated as a scientific instrument rather than as a logging convenience. The instrument must be calibrated in the same session in which it is used; each reported result must carry a detection floor; and the analysis must decline a directional claim when the observed effect cannot pass both the floor and direction gates. JouleWise implements this discipline for phase-resolved, single-request LLM inference on one named Apple-silicon machine and runtime-and-library configuration. The runtime emits the phase boundaries because it drives the workload, while *powermetrics* supplies the power samples integrated between those boundaries.

This scope is deliberately narrow. Each measurement describes exactly one configuration: one physical machine, one operating-system build, one runtime and declared set of libraries, one model file, one quantization (the numerical representation used for the model weights), one tokenizer (the rule that turns text into token IDs and back), one token-selection policy, one single-request execution policy, one power-data source, and one measurement boundary. It does not establish that an Apple hardware class or vendor is more efficient than another platform. Without an external power meter, the absolute joule values mean something only inside the boundary the counter itself reports: the central-processor (CPU), graphics-processor (GPU), and neural-engine power rails of the processor package, and not display, memory, storage, or peripheral power. Comparisons made inside that same boundary can still be scientifically useful, provided they pass the calibration and floor gates. Total joules per request is the primary energy metric. Joules per prompt token and per output token are reported alongside it, but only ever within one tokenizer: two tokenizers cut the same text into different numbers of tokens, so a per-token figure is not a unit of work that can be carried across them.

This paper makes the following contributions:

1. A calibration that runs inside the same uninterrupted collection session as the measurements it supports — we call such a session a *measurement window* — and that measures how far the line between two phases can be misplaced in time when energy is added up between them.
2. Detection floors built only from things we measured: how much repeated runs of one condition vary, how much energy the worst allowed misplacement of a phase edge could move, and how much the machine drifts over the session. A floor is published with every result that passes every listed check.
3. A collection protocol that refuses rather than guesses: every run must pass its entry checks before it counts; run order alternates so slow drift cannot pass for a real difference; every file is tied to the record by cryptographic hash — a short fingerprint that changes if any byte of the file changes — with a single tool allowed to write that record; the analysis plan is fixed before any data exist; and everything the protocol declines to use is logged rather than discarded.
4. A planned program for characterizing the instrument. It asks how measured energy responds when the workload is scaled up under this hardware-and-software configuration; whether comparing two conditions that are secretly identical still comes out near zero at short, medium, and long run lengths; how the floors behave in practice; whether the energy assigned to each phase adds up correctly and stays free of leakage from the other phase; how much the machine drifts; and how long it needs to settle after being disturbed. Three pieces are still unfinished: the small deliberate differences used to probe the decision path, the exact comparison that will fix the settling time, and the authoritative format for recording results.
5. A demonstration whose design is written down and fixed before any data are collected: phase-by-phase measurements of two model sizes, with one fixed comparison for token generation and one for prompt processing using a 256-token prompt.
6. An open tool, and a planned release of the evidence files together with their cryptographic fingerprints, so that readers can redo the analysis from the raw records and detect any file that differs from the bytes we published.

## 2. In-window calibration method

Terms used below name physical or reproducibility units. A *window* is one uninterrupted collection session with one declared power state and software configuration, surrounded by calibration runs and ending in one accept-or-refuse decision. A *calibration bracket* is the calibration immediately before and after that window. A *stage* is one ordered collection step. An *arm* is one workload or comparison track fixed before collection. A *campaign* is the complete fixed plan that groups its windows, stages, and arms. A plan is *fixed* only when its exact bytes and cryptographic fingerprint are recorded before outcomes exist. A file is *authenticated* when its recomputed fingerprint matches that recorded value. A *cell* is a set of runs that use the same power-data source, energy definition, measured interval, workload, hardware, and software. A *member* is one workload run; its *bundle* contains the configuration, raw power trace, runtime events, environment observations, and derived summary for that run. A *capture* is one uninterrupted collection by the *powermetrics* sampler — one continuous power trace, placed as a whole onto ordinary clock time by a single timing calculation, together with, when the collection is a calibration stage, the one bound it yields on how far a phase edge could be misplaced in time. *Capture* names the act of collecting rather than the thing collected on, so a capture is neither a *member* nor a *bundle*: the bundle of a workload run holds the power trace that the capture covering that run produced. A *corpus* is a fingerprinted collection of those bundles used together for one calculation. Evidence is *claim-bearing* only when every specified check passes; *diagnostic* evidence may explain behavior but cannot authorize a scientific claim. A *verdict* is the final accept-or-refuse decision for the complete declared evidence. *Reduction* means computing derived quantities from preserved raw files; repeating that computation under an authorized method change is *re-reduction* and never alters the raw files. A comparison is *registered* when its conditions, metric, expected direction, and pass rules appear in that fixed plan. A file or value is *issued* only after the specified program has authenticated every input, reproduced the calculation, and recorded the final fingerprint; until then it is pending.

### Measurement model and boundary

JouleWise measures one sequential request at a time. The two phases are also called *prefill* and *decode*, respectively. Because the experiment controls the runtime, it records the start and stop of these phases directly in the runtime event stream rather than inferring them from shapes in the power trace. The analysis program then integrates the three named *powermetrics* processor power channels — CPU, GPU, and neural engine — between each pair of phase events; no other channel enters the integral. Each sampled value is treated as the average power over its reported sampling interval, so the integral is a sum of interval-average power multiplied by interval duration, with the boundary intervals clipped at the phase events.

The analysis counts whole runs, and whole four-run A/B/B/A blocks (condition A, condition B, condition B, condition A), as its independent units. The individual power samples inside a run, and the items inside a workload, are not extra repeats.

The runtime and the power sampler do not share a perfect clock. Operating-system launch latency, timestamp anchoring, and the sampler's averaging behavior can shift an apparent edge. A phase-energy estimate must therefore be an interval, not only a point. The calibration asks a concrete question: if software commands a load pulse at a known time, how far can the rising and falling edges of that pulse appear displaced in the sampled power trace?

### Bracketed pulse-train calibration

The collection instructions place a calibration stage immediately before and immediately after every science window whose data may support a claim. The collection program checks this rather than trusting the operator to have done it: each of the two calibrations must be authenticated, must genuinely fall before (or after) the science runs it brackets, and each calibration capture time must sit no more than 24 hours from the far end of that window. The released calibration protocol configuration commands 59 graphics-processor matrix-multiplication pulses and specifies low-power baseline intervals of at least 4.5 seconds between fitted regions. Pulse durations are fixed in advance, and the gaps follow a deterministic low-discrepancy schedule, an ordering designed to spread gap positions evenly rather than repeat one period. The varied schedule reduces the risk that calibration accidentally locks to the power sampler's repeat interval. Each capture reports the maximum of 59 pulse-level worst allowed edge displacements, plus the capture-local trace-anchor bound. If those 59 pulse-level statistics are independent and interchangeable draws from a specified calibration-pulse population—the outcome of one pulse gives no information about another, and every pulse follows the same unchanging distribution—the sample maximum exceeds that population's 95th percentile with probability \(1-0.95^{59}=0.9515\). The current protocol does not establish that the pulses are independent or interchangeable in that sense, so unless that is justified separately the number should be read as exactly what it is: the largest of 59 measured pulse bounds. Carrying it over to the phase edges of a real workload is a further assumption.

The pulse estimator can be rebuilt as follows. For each sampler interval \(I_i\), it observes average graphics-processor power \(y_i\). Outside every commanded pulse plus a local margin it sets the baseline \(b\) to the median power and defines the median absolute deviation as the median of |y_i-b|; it sets the noise scale \(\sigma\) to the larger of \(1.4826\) times the median absolute deviation and \(0.001\) W. For a pulse commanded on at \(t_{\mathrm{on}}\) and off at \(t_{\mathrm{off}}\), it sets amplitude \(A\) to the median of intervals at least \(0.25\) s inside the plateau minus \(b\). A candidate pair \((d_{\mathrm{on}},d_{\mathrm{off}})\) predicts

\[
\hat y_i=b+A\frac{|I_i\cap[t_{\mathrm{on}}+d_{\mathrm{on}},t_{\mathrm{off}}+d_{\mathrm{off}}]|}{|I_i|}.
\]

The objective is to minimize \(L=\sum_i H_{1.345}((y_i-\hat y_i)/\sigma)\), where \(H_{1.345}\) is quadratic for a normalized error of magnitude at most \(1.345\) and linear beyond it so one power spike cannot dominate the fit. Coordinate search examines each lag from \(-0.75\) to \(+0.75\) s, first in \(0.005\)-s steps and then in \(0.0005\)-s steps. A pulse is accepted only if its plateau is at least \(10\) W above baseline, its amplitude-to-noise ratio is at least \(10\), its fitted loss is less than half the loss of a flat baseline, both fitted lags have magnitude below \(0.50\) s, and the trace covers the full search region. The accepted lag set contains every pair whose loss is no more than \(L_{\min}+\max(1,0.05L_{\min})\). A rectangle is *ruled out* only when an analytic lower bound proves that every pair inside it exceeds that limit; remaining rectangles are bisected until both dimensions are at most \(0.0001\) s. Command-event timestamp uncertainty widens the surviving onset and offset extremes. For one accepted pulse, the retained *residual bound* is the largest absolute onset or offset lag still allowed after that widening. The search *converges*, meaning completes with a usable bound, only when every commanded pulse passes, no extra plateau is detected, the whole accepted two-dimensional set has been enclosed, and the shared capture budget is not exhausted. Thus “converge” names a mechanical completion predicate, not visual agreement with the trace.

Here is the complete allowed-set construction. Let monotonic time \(M\) map to wall time \(w(M)=\alpha+\beta(M-m_0)\), where \(m_0\) is the first monotonic reading, \(\alpha\) is the wall-time intercept, and \(\beta\) is the relative clock rate. A paired stamp \(s\) records wall value \(W_s\), monotonic readings just before and after it, \(M_s^-\) and \(M_s^+\), and resolution \(R_s\). It is allowed exactly when

\[
W_s-R_s-\beta(M_s^++R_s-m_0)\leq\alpha\leq
W_s+R_s-\beta(M_s^--R_s-m_0).
\]

The five required stamps are `pre_spawn` immediately before launching the sampler, `first_parse` when its first complete record is parsed, `sampling_started` when the measurement window opens, `sampling_stopped` when it closes, and `post_parse` after the final record is parsed. For native sampler record \(i\), let \(N_i\) be its whole-second wall label and \(q_i\) the cumulative reported elapsed time from the first record. If \(A\) is the wall time at which the first averaging interval ends and \(D=0.000250\) s is the model-departure allowance, every record imposes

\[
N_i-D\leq A+\beta q_i\leq N_i+1\ \mathrm{s}+D.
\]

The first interval must also end after the pre-launch wall time plus its own elapsed duration and no later than the first-parse wall time, with the paired-stamp resolutions included. A candidate \((\alpha,\beta,A)\) survives only if it satisfies every stamp inequality, every native-record inequality, and this causal interval. The capture refuses if the set is empty, if the fitted rate interval extends strictly beyond \(1\pm50\) parts per million (equality at either limit is still allowed), or if the first-parse lag or numeric-precision checks fail. Otherwise minimizing and maximizing \(A\) over the same set produces \([A_{\min},A_{\max}]\). “Allowed” and “ruled out” therefore mean, respectively, satisfying or violating these explicit inequalities.

Each member therefore carries its own anchor bound, derived by that estimator from the member's own recorded bytes. For a reported phase, the analysis combines the member-local common shift, the calibration edge bound, and this clock-anchor term. It evaluates the energy integral at all four combinations of early and late start and stop edges, while scanning the allowed common shift without approximation. Together these possibilities are the *admissible set*, the energy values permitted by the calibrated timing bounds; their minimum and maximum form the reported interval. The physical intuition is simple: the interval asks how much energy could move into or out of the phase if both boundaries were placed at their most adverse calibrated positions.

The pre- and post-window calibrations form a bracket around the measurements. Both must be authenticated, within the 24-hour limit stated above, and causally outside the science interval. The operative timing bound is

\[
B_{\mathrm{op}}=\max(B_{\mathrm{pre}},B_{\mathrm{post}})+\max(|B_{\mathrm{pre}}-B_{\mathrm{post}}|,9.724\ \mathrm{ms}).

## 3. Detection-floor composition

No two runs are physically identical: temperature history, hardware frequency decisions, background work, and power-sample window placement can each produce joule differences when no real effect is present. The floor is the largest difference the measurement system can manufacture on its own under the explicitly allowed conditions listed below. A smaller measured difference is therefore not a small effect; it is a number the instrument could produce with no effect at all.

The *detection floor* is a practical guard against seeing a difference that is not there, under the conditions that define a cell. It is not a claim to have estimated some percentile of a population exactly. JouleWise computes separate absolute and comparative floors and takes their maximum for the cell. An absolute floor measures how far repeated measurements of the same condition wander from their mean. A comparative floor measures the apparent difference between labels that are deliberately made identical and collected in A/B/B/A order.

### Repeatability and false-comparison guards

For an absolute cell, \(E_i\) is the energy in joules of the \(i\)th bundle that passed every cell entry and verification check, \(\bar E\) is the mean energy, \(r_i=E_i-\bar E\) is its residual, \(s_r\) is the sample standard deviation of those residuals, and \(n\) is the number of such bundles. The point repeatability guard is

\[
F_{\mathrm{abs,point}}=\max\left(\max_i |r_i|,
t_{0.975,n-1}s_r\sqrt{1+1/n}\right).
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{abs,point}}\) is the absolute point guard in joules, and \(t_{0.975,n-1}\) is the 97.5th-percentile critical value of Student's \(t\) distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom.

For comparison, one A/B/B/A (ABBA) block contains runs \(A_1,B_1,B_2,A_2\). Its signed difference is

\[
\delta_i=(B_1+B_2-A_1-A_2)/2,
\]

so a positive \(\delta_i\) means condition B used more energy than condition A. For \(n\) blocks whose four runs passed every required check, \(\bar\delta\) is the mean block difference and \(s_\delta\) is its sample standard deviation. The comparative point guard is

\[
F_{\mathrm{cmp,point}}=\max\left(\max_i |\delta_i|,
|\bar\delta|+t_{0.975,n-1}s_\delta\sqrt{1+1/n}\right).
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{cmp,point}}\) is the comparative point guard in joules, and the Student-\(t\) subscript has the same meaning as above. The observed maxima protect against a false effect already seen; the prediction terms protect against one additional bundle or block. The Student-\(t\) prediction term is only as good as its assumptions: that after cooldown the run-to-run residuals, or the block differences, are roughly independent of one another, do not change their behaviour as the session goes on, and follow a normal distribution. The admission gates do not establish any of that. Shared calibration and drift terms are propagated separately as deterministic bounds. Small samples receive a *guard factor*, a multiplicative safety margin fixed before collection, and fewer than five bundles or blocks that passed every required check are treated only as development evidence, not as a claim gate.

The guard factor is mechanical: \(g(n)=\sqrt{9/(n-1)}\) for \(5\leq n<10\), and \(g(n)=1\) for \(n\geq10\). With fewer than five independent bundles or blocks the software emits the unguarded diagnostic components but no publishable guarded floor.

### Worst-case timing attribution

Point repeatability is not the full floor. Each energy value is an interval from the timing calibration in Section 2. The floor computation evaluates the complete point-floor estimator over the joint *corners* of all member intervals that passed the entry checks—the combinations of their extreme endpoints—and then takes the largest value. For a null ABBA block, the block interval is first formed from the four signed member intervals. Onset and offset, and at floor level the member intervals, are allowed to take independently adverse endpoints. Taking every combination this way deliberately gives up any credit for errors that might cancel — because two runs' timing errors move together, or because both edges shift the same way. The result is a bound fixed by rule and chosen to be as unfavourable as the calibration allows. It is not a confidence interval, and it is not a root-sum-square combination, that is, adding the terms in quadrature, which would assume they are independent and vary at random. It may therefore include combinations that the joint pulse fit, or the physics of a shared shift, would rule out. Working through every combination exactly is currently supported for at most 16 runs or blocks that passed the entry checks; beyond that the calculation refuses. The published component is never smaller than the largest value the full floor calculation reaches over any of those combinations. For repeatability cells, because residuals are measured about the mean, that value is not guaranteed to be as large as the widest single run's own energy interval.

This calculation revealed a stable and important limitation of the present implementation. Prompt processing saturates compute while token generation waits more heavily on memory, so power can fall steeply exactly where their boundary is drawn. Because energy is power multiplied by time, moving that boundary by tens of milliseconds across a tens-of-watts change can assign roughly a joule to the wrong phase. The request total is unchanged, which is why averaging cannot repair the attribution. Across the 30 diagnostic phase-repeatability runs collected on 25 July 2026, the combined bound on where a phase edge may fall spans 25.6–31.1 ms. Dividing each run's phase-energy envelope — the width of the energy interval that timing bound leaves open — by that run's own bound gives 21–58 W; these are quotients we computed, not power steps anyone measured. The ten ordinary prompt-processing runs have envelopes of 0.98–1.47 J. In one retained ordinary prompt-processing run, the 31.07-ms bound and 1.016-J envelope give a quotient of 32.7 W. The immediately preceding calibration and reference collection supplies context but contains no phase-repeatability runs. Ordinary repeatability was smaller than the uncertainty caused by placing samples at phase edges, so the instrument is *attribution-limited*, not *noise-limited*. More repetitions can refine the repeatability term, but they cannot average away this boundary-placement limit. Figure 2 illustrates the mechanism.

### Measured, never-zero drift allowance

Drift is a slow change in the machine or measurement response over the collection window. It is measured rather than assumed away. The reference schedule is fixed before collection: the two floor campaigns use three fixed references at the start, one at the midpoint, and three at the end, whereas the contrast campaign uses nine references — three at the start, singles after the 20th, 40th, and 60th science members, and three at the end. Total energy and idle-subtracted energy are treated as separate claim families. Idle-subtracted request energy is total request energy minus mean idle power times request duration; phase energy is reported as a total because no rule assigns idle power to only one phase.

A *settled reference corpus* begins with an exact member list whose recorded status is `settled_reference`. Settlement requires at least ten distinct bundle paths. For every listed bundle, the stored summary, metadata, and raw-evidence fingerprints must agree; the reduction method must be current; the scientific configuration must be identical; both gross and idle-subtracted request energies must be present; and the operating-system build, power-supply fingerprint, and calibration fingerprint must match across all members. A missing, duplicate, unsafe, or mismatched member refuses derivation.

For one claim family, let the corpus contain \(n_c\) point energies ordered as \(C_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq C_{(n_c)}\), with sample standard deviation \(s_c\). The current windows use three start and three end references, so their matching reference-repeatability bound is

\[
R_c=\max\!\left(
\frac{C_{(n_c)}+C_{(n_c-1)}+C_{(n_c-2)}}{3}
-\frac{C_{(1)}+C_{(2)}+C_{(3)}}{3},
\ t_{0.975,n_c-1}s_c\sqrt{2/3}
\right).
\]

The calculation is performed separately for gross request energy and idle-subtracted request energy; a window must use the family that matches its claim. The resulting bound must be no more than 24 hours old and must match the window's operating-system build, power-supply fingerprint, and calibration fingerprint, or the window refuses.

A claim then faces two separate gates, enforced separately. First, the magnitude of its point estimate must exceed \(F_{\mathrm{cell}}\), the cell floor. Second, both endpoints of its *fully composed decision interval*—the interval after all statistical, attribution, and drift terms fixed before collection have been included—must support the direction fixed before collection. Let \(B_{\mathrm{claim}}\) be the *claim-side bound*, the uncertainty carried by this particular comparison as distinct from the cell's floor. The project also discloses

\[
\text{practical joint-clearance size}=F_{\mathrm{cell}}+B_{\mathrm{claim}}.
\]

It is not a single summed pass limit, and the decision interval is not compared with the sum. Effects that pass the floor gate but whose interval does not support direction remain unresolved and receive no directional claim.

## 4. Collection stops whenever required evidence fails

*Fail-closed* means that missing, inconsistent, stale, or contaminated evidence produces a refusal rather than an optimistic default. The collection unit is the measurement window defined in Section 2. Work that does not fit within two to four hours with at least a 20% failure margin is split prospectively into another independently calibrated window.

### Fixing the design and admitting runs

Before measured collection begins, the operator records exact bytes for the run identifiers, membership, stage order, comparison definitions, calibration retry count, numeric acceptance limits, extraction specification, source revision, and an initially empty list of permitted exceptions. The extraction specification also fixes the calculation: comparative cells in this cycle use the conservative worst-case rule. Any future alternative must be named with all parameters in those fixed files; a result file cannot select its own calculation. Every configuration is validated and trial-run against a fresh collection directory. The collection program and final verification program compare the executed values with the fixed bytes rather than relying on a narrative promise.

The same fixed-before-collection rule covers prospective sizing. Before collection, the planned effect, workload, and number of independent bundles or ABBA blocks are compared with the cell floor and the claim-side interval width. If the design has too little expected clearance, the operator must prospectively increase independent evidence, change the workload, or narrow the claim; the choice cannot be made after seeing claim data. A workload change is not merely a sensitivity adjustment. For example, lengthening the prompt changes the population of requests being estimated and therefore changes the *estimand*, the quantity the design aims to estimate.

Each stage passes the following admission predicate immediately before measurement. The environment record must have `power_source = "AC Power"` (alternating current), `power.external_connected = true`, `low_power_mode = false`, `display_power_state = "all_asleep"`, `screensaver_engaged = false`, and `thermal_pressure = "nominal"`. `power.adapter_watts` must be a known number and must equal every other admission observation in the window; any recorded adapter descriptions or power-source labels must also remain unchanged. Thus “quiet,” “settled,” and “approved power” are shorthand for the explicit observations and inequalities above, not operator judgment.

### Counterbalanced order

Comparisons use the ABBA blocks defined before Figure 1. Conditions A and B are measured in the order A, B, B, A, and every intervening cooldown must satisfy the same exit criterion. A machine measured over an hour warms and shifts. If run midpoints are \(t_{A1},t_{B1},t_{B2},t_{A2}\), exact first-order time balance means \(t_{A1}+t_{A2}=t_{B1}+t_{B2}\). Under a linear drift, that equality makes the average drift contribution to A equal the average contribution to B. Unequal runtimes and recovery waits mean exact balance cannot be assumed. The time imbalance \(q=(t_{B1}+t_{B2}-t_{A1}-t_{A2})/2\) is computable from the retained per-run records, but the current extraction computes and reports only the block difference; recording \(q\) alongside each contrast is a planned reporting addition, not a present output of the pipeline. Curvature and the separate whole-window drift allowance remain. For a null-floor block, A and B are the same configuration and payload, so any nonzero block difference is false. For scientific contrasts, A and B are fixed before collection and each block difference is computed before blocks are aggregated.

**Worked ABBA block, awaiting fresh energies.** Substitute the four fresh run energies into \(\delta=(B_1+B_2-A_1-A_2)/2\) (the quantity the extraction reports); compute \(q\) from the retained midpoint times as a check on time balance; then record the four cooldown outcomes and confirm each met the exit criterion. `TODO-EVIDENCE[four energies, four run midpoint times, and three cooldown records from one fresh ABBA block]`.

### Preserving every input and every refusal

The bundle defined in Section 2 is the basic custody object. Every file in a bundle that could support a claim is recorded in the campaign record by its cryptographic hash, the fingerprint that changes if any byte changes. The fingerprints cover exactly the files the analysis reads, and the final verdict fixes which runs belong to the window, so swapping or omitting a file is detectable. Failed or interrupted artifacts are never deleted or overwritten. Each preserved attempt to collect a declared member is a *recorded occurrence*. *Quarantine* is a separate retained directory outside the active collection path: moving an occupied retry slot there prevents it from being mistaken for the active member but does not delete it. The operator performs that move, the recording tools validate it, an allowed retry writes a new slot, and an append-only replacement record says which occurrence is current and which it replaces. Two present bundles claiming the same occurrence cause a refusal. The final window verdict fingerprints the complete declared member set, preserved attempts, replacements, calibration bracket, policy, and drift evidence, preventing a later analysis from selecting a more favorable subset.

Collection does not continue indefinitely through environmental interruptions. Each stage attempt stops at its first member failure, and after the same cause fails a window stage for the third time the window itself closes rather than being retried. In actual operation, nights with three environmental member interruptions were closed and preserved as salvage rather than repeatedly retried. The surviving data may remain diagnostic, but it becomes claim-bearing only if the fixed verdict and extraction rules accept the complete resulting member set. This abandon-after-three rule limits outcome-dependent retrying and makes the cost of a noisy environment visible.

Custody continues after collection. The collection tools write each corpus once and it is backed up before extraction. The analysis program recomputes every fingerprint and checks required files, agreement between files, calibration identity, environment admission, complete planned membership, cooldown records, phase-energy definition, and drift allowance. A missing uncertainty never becomes zero. Exactly one specified program may write the final record; it derives its own fingerprints rather than trusting supplied ones. A missing, substituted, stale, duplicated, or unexpected input produces a named refusal.

The analysis method is also fixed in the recorded plan rather than accepted from the result being tested. Result files reject any field that tries to select their own estimator, even if nested or escaped, and reject duplicate keys. ## 5. Instrument characterization

The instrument characterization uses workloads as known test signals, not as scientific findings. Each row below asks one question about the measuring apparatus — does measured energy grow with the amount of work done, does a comparison of a condition against itself come out at zero, does the apparatus notice an effect it was deliberately given, do the two phase energies account for the request, does the machine stay put and recover, and does any of this repeat on another day — and answers it against a limit fixed before the first member is measured. Existing fragments cannot be promoted when their complete window verdict failed.

What was missing until now was not the measurements but the acceptance rules. The repository fixed *which* runs would be measured — the plan names every member, its order, and its configuration, and records its exact bytes and fingerprint before any outcome exists — and left *what counts as passing* to analysis code and working notes, both of which can be edited after the data are in hand. Two artifacts now close that gap. A normative contract fixes the outcome vocabulary, the definitions, the refusal codes, and the exact key structure of the result, and deliberately carries no number at all, so that no editing pass can move a limit. A frozen specification carries every limit, every *estimator* — the named formula together with the exact authenticated input fields it reads — every sample count, and the field path that produces every published value. *Frozen* here means fixed in the sense of Section 2: exact bytes and a cryptographic fingerprint recorded before any outcome exists. The program that issues the result recomputes each fingerprint itself, refuses any input field that tries to select its own estimator or its own limit, and refuses to issue at all unless those acceptance rules were fixed strictly before the first admitted member was captured and every borrowed limit came from an artifact frozen strictly earlier still.
<!-- evidence: docs/contracts/characterization_result_schema_v1.md; configs/campaigns/metrology_v1/characterization_result_schema_v1.json -->

Because those rules now exist before collection, "protocol incomplete" is retired as an outcome. Every row will report exactly one of four fixed outcomes, and a report that cannot reach one of them for a row is not issued at all.

Six terms carry the weight of this section, and each means one specific thing. A *criterion* is the unit that decides: one named quantity, one formula, one sample unit, one minimum evidence count, one comparison, and one limit. A *row outcome* is what the reader is told about a row, and it has exactly four values: the row **supported** the registered behavior, it **did not support a conclusion** under the registered criterion, it **showed that the registered expected behavior did not hold**, or — for between-session stability only — it **remains pending** because too few sessions meet that row's written eligibility rule, which is stated with the row below. A *held-out probe* is a measurement deliberately excluded from building the allowance it will later be tested against; a reference that helped construct an allowance cannot then demonstrate that the allowance contains it, because that comparison is satisfied by construction and measures nothing. *Containment* means the whole composed interval lies inside the limit on both sides, which is a stricter and more useful thing than failing to exclude zero: a wide, uninformative interval fails containment and passes a significance test, which is exactly backwards for an instrument check. A *resolution-qualified* conclusion is bounded by what one admitted measurement could resolve rather than by physics — "linear to no worse than one admitted bundle's timing half-width over the tested range" is resolution-qualified; "linear" is not. A *claim-anchored limit* is one taken from a separately issued artifact rather than from the data being judged, which matters because a limit computed from a collection's own scatter grows when the collection is sloppier, so on its own it can never fail for the reason we care about.

Rows report; criteria decide. A row carries one or more criteria and supports its registered behavior only when every one of them does, so a pass on one half of a row can never make the printed row look like a pass overall. Within a row, a criterion that was evaluated and failed outranks one that could not be decided, so a known failure never hides behind an unreached decision. A row that fails on complete, authenticated evidence is published as a result, together with the sentence — frozen before collection — naming what the paper gives up because of it; a failed row that does not say what it narrows would be decorative. A published refusal never asserts equality, absence of effect, or instrument validity outside the named stack and conditions. A report in which every row supported its registered behavior still publishes its refusal log. And a row that failed inside a window whose whole-window verdict passed does not license re-collecting to reverse it: any successor window names its predecessor and both results publish.

### How each row is decided

Each row below states the problem that forces it, what it measures physically, the exact statistic, where its limit comes from, what a failure costs, and a worked substitution. Where retained evidence exists, the substitution uses it; every such number is diagnostic instrument evidence and supports no claim. Where it does not, the substitution names exactly which fresh values complete it.

**Workload response.** The forcing problem: if measured energy does not grow in a fixed straight-line way with the amount of work done, then any energy difference between two conditions can be an artifact of the conditions doing different amounts of work rather than of the thing being compared, and the joules-per-token conversion that sizes every later probe is meaningless. Physically, the row drives the same model through five output lengths and asks whether the energy it reports tracks the tokens it actually produced. The statistic is ordinary least squares of \(E=a+bT\) over admitted bundles, one bundle one point, with \(T\) the runtime-observed realized output count, computed once for whole-request energy and once for token-generation energy; \(R_{\max}\) is the largest departure of any level mean from that fitted line, taken over the authenticated energy intervals so that a level whose measurements are individually uncertain is not credited with a precision it does not have. The two limits come from different places on purpose. The resolution limit is the largest timing half-width any single admitted bundle carries, so passing it means "no curvature bigger than one measurement could resolve"; it is always computable, and it is also the limit a degraded collection can widen for itself, which is why it is never used alone. The claim-anchored limit is the operative floor already issued for the matching cell in an earlier window — the largest difference the instrument can manufacture with nothing changed — so passing it means the departure is smaller than a difference the instrument invents on its own. Failure costs the per-token conversion: the token-generation slope stops being usable to size an effect, the small-difference row cannot be built from it, and no comparison may rely on a single conversion across levels. Worked substitution: with the five level means \([\bar E_{128},\ldots,\bar E_{2048}]\) and their half-widths, fit \(a\) and \(b\), evaluate the fitted line at each registered \(T\), take the largest absolute residual over the interval endpoints to get \(R_{\max}\), take \(H\) as the largest half-width in the set, and compare \(R_{\max}\) with \(H\) and with the issued floor. The forty admitted bundle energies that complete this substitution have not been collected.

**Identical-condition null response.** The forcing problem: an A/B/B/A comparison whose A and B are the same condition and the same payload must come out at zero, so any nonzero block difference is false by construction — and that makes this row the one place where the paper's own detection floor can be falsified rather than assumed. Physically, the row measures the same workload against itself, five blocks at each of three output sizes, and asks how large a difference the apparatus manufactures when nothing differs. The statistic is the signed block difference \(\delta_i=(B_1+B_2-A_1-A_2)/2\), reported as the mean with its complete composed interval and as the largest single absolute block difference. The limit is the operative floor already issued for the matching cell in an independent window: the floor is *defined* as the largest difference the instrument can manufacture with nothing changed, and a null block is nothing changed, so a null block exceeding it falsifies the floor rather than the null. Independence matters here more than anywhere else — the floor campaigns' own A/B/B/A stages construct the comparative floor, so testing those same blocks against that floor would be circular, and the null ladder's blocks are held outside that derivation for exactly this reason. If no such floor has been issued when the plan is frozen, the pre-registered alternative activates: ten blocks per magnitude, five building the comparator and five held out to test it. That branch is the fallback rather than the default because the guard factor the project applies to small samples, \(g(n)=\sqrt{9/(n-1)}\), equals \(1.5\) at five blocks and \(1\) at ten, so an unconditional split would impose a weaker comparator even where a stronger issued one exists. Failure costs the floor: the affected cell's issued floor is withdrawn from claim use and every contrast consuming it returns "not resolvable" until a floor is re-derived. Worked substitution: for one block, substitute the four run energies into \(\delta=(B_1+B_2-A_1-A_2)/2\), compose the block interval from the four signed member intervals, then check that the interval contains zero, that the mean interval lies inside plus-or-minus the comparator, and that the largest \(|\delta_i|\) does not exceed it. The fifteen blocks' energies that complete this substitution have not been collected.

**Deliberate small-difference challenge.** The forcing problem: a gate that has never been shown a near-threshold case is untested, and a floor that has never been challenged from just below and just above is a number nobody has tried to break. Physically, the row manufactures a known energy difference by lengthening the output by a computed number of tokens, places it deliberately at fractions and multiples of the published floor, and checks that the ordinary two-gate path reaches the outcome written down in advance. The statistic is the realized signed contrast divided by the applicable operative floor. The registered ratios \(0.5\), \(1.0\), \(1.5\), and \(3.0\) are not chosen here: they are already recorded in the micro-delta plan, and the super-floor end of that range is independently corroborated by the retained finding that comparative differences at roughly three times the widened floor were the ones the conservative composition could resolve. The token increment is \(\lfloor rF/b\rfloor\) below the boundary and \(\lceil rF/b\rceil\) above it, so integer rounding always pushes the probe further from the gate rather than across it. Two guards keep the row honest. The comparator for each slot is the larger of the two arms' own exact-configuration floors, and the evidence behind those floors must be disjoint from the blocks under test. And because the probe is sized in tokens through a fitted slope, a wrong slope aims the probe at the wrong place: the specification records the predicted joule difference at freeze and the realized one at report, and a slot that misses by more than the registered tolerance is returned as undecided, never as a gate failure. Failing to build the test is not the test failing. Failure costs in whichever direction it occurs: a sub-floor probe that resolves means the floor understates how often the instrument invents an effect, and the floor construction is reported as falsified for that cell; a super-floor probe that fails to resolve means the two-gate path is less sensitive than the floor predicts, and prospective clearance sizing is re-derived upward. Worked substitution: with the issued decode slope \(b\) in joules per token and the already-issued floor \(F\) that the ratios are measured against, compute \(k_{1.5}=\lceil 1.5F/b\rceil\), build the pair A = \(512\) output tokens and B = \(512+k_{1.5}\), collect five blocks in each direction, and compare \(|\hat\delta|\) with \(\max(F_A,F_B)\) and the composed interval with the registered sign. The slope and floor that complete this substitution are not yet issued, and the tolerance that decides a mis-sized probe is not yet ratified.

**Phase accounting.** The forcing problem is the property this paper is named for. Prompt processing saturates compute while token generation waits more heavily on memory, so power falls steeply exactly where their boundary is drawn, and moving that boundary by tens of milliseconds across a tens-of-watts change assigns roughly a joule to the wrong phase while leaving the request total untouched. Everything in Sections 3 and 7 that rests on phase-boundary placement being the limiting uncertainty currently rests on retained diagnostics that were collected under no criterion at all. This row makes that property a criterioned, printed outcome. Physically it does seven things at once, and all seven must hold. Closure asks whether the two phase energies account for the request: \(D=E_{\mathrm{prompt}}+E_{\mathrm{generation}}-E_{\mathrm{request}}\) per bundle, split by sign because the two signs mean opposite things. A positive \(D\) means both phases counted energy from the same sampling interval at their shared boundary, which is a hard instrument defect, so the only allowance it gets is the rounding error of the arithmetic itself. A negative \(D\) is expected and legitimate, because energy genuinely falls in the un-phased gap between the two phase windows, and its permitted magnitude is that bundle's own gap duration times the largest power recorded across that gap — derived per bundle from retained trace data, never a chosen constant. Invariance asks whether energy leaks across the boundary: prompt processing finishes before generation starts, so with the prompt held fixed, how many tokens are generated afterwards cannot change the energy prompt processing used, and a nonzero fitted slope means it did. That slope faces the same two limits as the workload-response residual, a resolution band and a claim-anchored band, both formed as an energy scale divided by the registered output-token span. The remaining five subtests realize the timing-attribution property itself, and each consumes a quantity the harness already writes or a condition already committed in code, so none of them introduces a new number. Decomposition requires the one timing term shared by the whole session to be reported separately from each bundle's own term. Bracket requires both of the window's calibration captures to fall inside the range of bounds recorded by the issued calibration-acceptance edition. Two flag subtests require that no claim-bearing member carries the flag raised when its timing bound exceeds a quarter of its own window duration, nor either flag raised when a phase is covered by too few power records or sampled too coarsely for its duration. Dominance evaluates the committed condition that compares the largest residual the member timing envelopes admit against the guarded point-only floor — the floor computed from run-to-run scatter alone, with the small-sample guard factor applied and no timing term. Failure costs are pre-committed and severe: a positive closure residual withdraws every phase-specific energy claim until the boundary treatment is re-derived; a nonzero prompt-processing slope under a fixed prompt would narrow the phase-specific claim rather than be explained away; and a dominance result of "no" means this window's floors do not receive the attribution-limited label at all, and Section 7's attribution-limited paragraph is re-scoped to the retired-era corpus only. Worked substitutions, from retained diagnostic evidence that supports no claim: for closure, raw-trace reconstruction and independent reintegration of a retained smaller-model population reproduced every stored phase energy with \(0.0\) J discrepancy, every prompt-processing interval ended before generation began, and overlap between the two phases was zero — the substitution that a fresh window must reproduce with its own bundles. For decomposition, one retained prompt-processing run's composed bound of \(31.07\) ms is not one quantity but two: \(24.879\) ms of it is the session's single shared calibration term, identical for every member of that session, and only \(6.195\) ms is that run's own local and edge-span contribution. Quoting a corpus range over the composed bound, as Sections 3 and 7 currently do for the retained corpus, therefore presents one shared constant as thirty independent draws, and this subtest exists to stop that. For the bracket subtest, the substitution is the one already reported below: a re-derived capture bound of \(0.0309\) s inside a then-issued corpus range of \([0.0227, 0.0336]\) s. **Drift and recovery.** The forcing problem has two halves that fail differently. The first: the drift allowance is built as the maximum of the observed start-to-end excursion and a corpus repeatability bound, so testing the same start, midpoint, and end points against the allowance they constructed is satisfied by construction and establishes nothing — Section 3 says so in its own words. The second: the collection instructions wait a fixed \(180\) s after any intervention and only then attempt admission, so a blind wait can never tell you when recovery actually happened. Physically, the drift half measures reference probes placed in the window but excluded from building the allowance, and asks whether the allowance contains them. The recovery half measures how long the machine actually takes to satisfy the complete cooldown exit criterion after a sustained load. The statistics are, for drift, the largest absolute deviation of any held-out probe from the window's allowance-constructing reference mean, computed separately for gross and idle-subtracted request energy; and for recovery, the elapsed time recorded on the first cooldown evaluation that passes. That second statistic is observable today and needs no new instrument: the controller already polls in five-second sub-windows during every inter-member cooldown, and the elapsed time at release is written into the experiment manifest with the release outcome. The limits: for drift, the window's own published allowance; for recovery, \(180\) s, which is the settling convention this row is asked to adjudicate. The \(180\) s and the cooldown gate's \(300\)-s refusal cap are two different mechanisms and must not be conflated — the cap decides when the gate gives up, and the criterion asks whether recovery in fact completed inside the convention. Failure costs: a held-out probe outside the allowance means the allowance understates within-window drift, and every floor carrying it is re-derived with a larger one; a recovery time above the convention raises the settling interval to the observed maximum plus the registered margin in a successor policy edition, and every window collected under the present convention is re-examined for admission validity. The claim is bounded to workload-induced disturbance; it says nothing about recovery from operator intervention. Worked substitution, from retained diagnostic evidence that supports no claim: for one retained collection the re-derived largest excursion among the start mean, midpoint, and end mean is \(0.5094\) J for both energy families, against allowances of \(0.652\) and \(0.658\) J — the arithmetic of the comparison, computed on exactly the three points that built those allowances. That is the substitution the criterion forbids as a test, and showing it here is the point: replacing those three constructed points with held-out probes measured under the same allowance is the entire content of the drift criterion. The held-out probe energies and the three recovery times that complete a real substitution have not been collected.

**Between-session stability.** The forcing problem: every number in this paper belongs to one session, and a reader has no way to know whether a floor issued on Tuesday describes the instrument on Thursday. Physically, the row compares the same quantities — calibration bound, each operative floor, and the null result — across independently calibrated sessions running the identical hardware and software stack. Which sessions count is fixed by a written rule and never by a list, because a list can be chosen after the outcomes are visible and a rule cannot: a session is eligible when its whole-window verdict is issued and passed, when every field naming the hardware and software stack — the complete list at the end of this section — matches the reference session exactly, and when its capture-method era is a current claim-bearing anchor method. Every session considered and excluded is published with its reason. Two rules then apply. The corridor rule compares the largest and smallest operative floor for each repeated floor role and requires the ratio to be no more than \(1.25\); that ratio is the project's existing one-directional test for deciding when a stored floor has gone stale, generalized here to both directions, and it decides only whether a stored floor may still be reused, not whether the sessions are statistically indistinguishable — the strongest permitted wording is that the sessions are stable enough for the stated reuse corridor. The spread rule requires, for each repeated quantity, that the range across eligible sessions be no larger than the largest bound a single session already declares for it; if session-to-session variation exceeds the uncertainty a single session already admits to, then the single-session bound understates what the instrument does between sessions. Fewer than three eligible sessions leaves the row pending; that is a stated outcome, not a gap. Failure costs floor reuse: a corridor breach marks the affected floors stale rather than reusable, and a spread breach means no single-session bound may be quoted as though it covered reuse. ## 6. Demonstration results

The planned demonstration will ask a deliberately modest scientific question: on one named consumer-hardware configuration, how does phase-resolved energy per request differ between two sizes of the same model family, and can each registered difference pass its own floor and direction gates? The two conditions will be instruction-tuned models of the same family at roughly 1.5 and 7 billion parameters, both 4-bit quantized and served by the same runtime. The models share fixed prompt bytes and output policy within each arm: the token-generation arm uses the fixed 128-token prompt (p128), while the separate prompt-processing arm uses the fixed synthetic 256-token prompt (p256). Demonstration measurements will be collected in dedicated windows that pass every Section 4 entry check, with the floors of Section 3 derived from the protocol-controlled floor windows.

### Design fixed before collection

Each model size will receive its own token-generation floor cell. The floor for 256-token prompt processing is measured in its own right rather than borrowed: its floor cells are registered in advance in the campaign plans fixed before collection, and no value is carried over from a different prompt length. Each floor design includes an absolute-repeatability arm and a null ABBA arm and is fixed before collection. The registered demonstration contains two model-size directional contrasts: one for token generation and one for prompt processing under a fixed synthetic 256-token prompt. The two planned comparisons — token generation and 256-token prompt processing — are treated as a single registered family of two tests and corrected together by a fixed two-test multiplicity correction, which tightens each test's numeric cutoff so that running two tests does not raise the overall chance of a false positive (\(\alpha=0.05\), \(m=2\)). Both tests are two-sided, and the direction each is expected to go was registered in advance. Each contrast will be computed within its own ABBA blocks whose A and B are the two model sizes, on the phase-energy metric fixed before collection. Its floor gate will compare the point-estimate magnitude with the cell floor; its separate direction gate will ask whether both endpoints of the composed interval support the registered direction.

The *Holm procedure* controls the chance of any false rejection across the two tests. Order their two-sided p-values as \(p_{(1)}\leq p_{(2)}\). Compare \(p_{(1)}\) with \(0.05/2=0.025\). If it is larger, neither test passes the multiplicity check. If it is no larger, compare \(p_{(2)}\) with \(0.05\); the second ordered test passes only when that comparison also passes. ## 7. Discussion and limitations

The attribution-limited resolution item below quotes the full range of what was retained, rather than presenting one run's largest bound and its derived quotient as though they described the whole corpus. It explains why boundary placement, rather than run-to-run scatter, is what this implementation has to attack first.

A published refusal is scientifically useful because it maps the operating domain of the measurement method. A contaminated window, an unresolved clock anchor, or a contrast that fails a gate is not evidence that two systems are equal. It is evidence that the named implementation cannot support that claim under the recorded conditions. Preserving the failed evidence and its reason prevents selective reporting and helps later work distinguish a small physical effect from an inadequate measurement.

Prompt length illustrates why feasibility is tied to the estimand. A longer prompt can increase prompt-processing energy and make a contrast easier to resolve, but it answers a different question about a different request population. Feasibility therefore means that a design fixed before collection can adjudicate the effect it names, not that the workload can be enlarged until any difference becomes visible. Beyond this Apple-silicon configuration, the transferable contribution is the method—calibrate boundary attribution, measure drift, separate floor and direction gates, and preserve refusals—not the numerical floor, which must be established again for each instrument, boundary, and configuration.

**Attribution-limited resolution.** On the implementation studied here, the largest source of uncertainty is phase-boundary placement, not run scatter. Across the 30 diagnostic phase-repeatability runs collected on 25 July 2026, the combined bound spans 25.6–31.1 ms; each phase-energy envelope divided by its own bound gives 21–58 W; and the ordinary prompt-processing envelopes span 0.98–1.47 J. The immediately preceding calibration/reference collection supplies context but no phase runs. Those watt figures are computed quotients, not measured power steps, and repetition does not shrink the boundary-placement uncertainty. When that dominance is the only otherwise-blocking condition and an exact corner-widened floor exists, the result may carry the attribution-limited label; the label never rescues any other refusal. A floor is a property of one cell under one calculation and cannot be quoted from another. An older short-prompt model-size diagnostic suggested marginal clearance under the conservative calculation and motivated the fixed-256-token prompt-processing arm; it was neither a fresh measurement nor an issued refusal. The fresh contrast can still be refused. Section 3 additionally requires every published attribution-limited floor to disclose the sizing quantity that accompanies it — that cell's floor added to the claim-side bound of the comparison being tested — so that neither of those two uncertainty terms is later dropped as though it were double counting the other.

**A corrected clock anchor retires every corpus collected before it.** Section 2 explains the replacement. Every measurement window collected before that correction used the retired capture method, so none can support a current claim. Those corpora were already outside the claim path because only evidence collected after its analysis plan was fixed may authorize a claim;<!-- docs/paper/results-fill-registry.md: "No historical result is a supplier for this registry. **The 748 bundles collected under the retired clock-anchor calculation stay auditable but can never support a claim.** Section 4 explains that every stored bundle carries a label naming the clock-anchor method that produced it, and that admission refuses any bundle not labeled with the current method. This repository holds 748 bundles carrying the earlier label. They remain auditable in full: each can still be recomputed byte for byte under the method named on it. What they cannot become is claim evidence, and the reason lies in the calculation rather than in the files. Their power samples were placed on the wall-clock timeline by the calculation that assumed the wall clock advances at exactly the rate of the monotonic counter, the counter that only ever advances and is never adjusted — the assumption Section 2 reports as measurably false on this machine — and recomputing them under the current calculation would be choosing an analysis after seeing the data, which the fixed-plan rule of Section 4 forbids. **One unit, one configuration, one boundary.** Every result is scoped to one physical machine, operating-system build, runtime and libraries, model file, quantization, tokenizer, token-selection and output policy, power-data source, and measurement boundary. Nothing here ranks hardware classes or vendors, and the *powermetrics* processor-package boundary (CPU, GPU, and neural-engine rails only) is not comparable to a graphics-board or wall boundary without a calibrated bridge.

**No external meter.** The calibration validates timing attribution—whether samples near a commanded edge land inside or outside a phase integral. It does not validate the counter's absolute gain or whole-system scale; absolute joule values remain internal to the named software boundary. A wall-power instrument could test totals but would still not adjudicate the division of a total between phases, which is the question the pulse-train experiment answers.

**Where the captures came from rests on trusting the operator.** JouleWise checks its files, custody record, and derivations end to end, but it cannot prove who created a capture or what unobserved process ran on the machine. File fingerprints and a named source-code revision detect later substitution relative to the published evidence archive and protect against ordinary operator error. Issuance also refuses unrecorded source changes. The system has no independent signer, external timestamp, or write-once log. Two gaps in this trust are worth naming explicitly, because the design places them outside what it defends against rather than defeating them. The first is a program running on this same machine under the same user account as the measurement: the operating system's permission model gives such a program exactly the access to the evidence files that the measurement tools themselves have, and JouleWise has no way to tell the two apart, so a locally running program written to interfere with the measurement is assumed not to be present. The second is that when one program starts another, JouleWise records the launch but cannot prove which program did the starting; a recorded launch is evidence of a launch, not proof of which parent performed it. The stated assumption is therefore a single-operator machine carrying no program written to attack the measurement, and results collected on a shared or multi-user machine would fall outside it. The system establishes machine-checked internal consistency and tamper evidence relative to disclosed bytes, not third-party-verified provenance.

**A known timing gap between checking a file and using it, accepted rather than repaired.** One program in the pipeline must establish which floor specification file it is permitted to read before it reads any value out of that file. Both the check that selects the file and the step that grants the permission identify the file by its path name, and they do so at different moments; in the interval between them the name can be made to point at a different file. A second program running at the same time on this machine, holding permission to write in the repository, can use that interval to have the wrong floor specification recorded as the authorized one, and in repeated trials it succeeded on a small fraction of attempts. It does not falsify a number: every value the program reads after that point is checked against a fingerprint fixed beforehand, so a permission obtained this way cannot alter any published result. The harm is confined to the record of which specification was authorized — the program would break its own written rule never to authorize the other specification, and a refusal caused by the swap would be filed under a less specific reason than the true one. Because a locally running hostile program lies outside the assumption stated just above, this is carried as a disclosed limitation with a procedural rule attached, namely that the program is run by a single operator with no other program writing to the repository at the same time, rather than closed in code.

**The evidence that authorizes a collection expires, and the longest expiry is a disclosed instrument setting.** Before a window whose data may support a claim is allowed to start, the instrument requires a set of recorded facts — about the fixed plan, about the machine's power and clock state, and about the software present — each written into its own file that carries the time it was made. Each such file is valid only for a stated span after that time; once the span has passed, the pre-collection checks refuse to proceed and the fact must be recorded again. Ten kinds of this evidence — the ones describing the fixed plan itself, the analysis programs it names, and the tests demonstrating that those programs behave as specified — carry a span of 168 hours, or seven days. Four kinds carry 24 hours, and the two recorded at the moment a collection begins carry 6 hours. Evidence about the machine's immediate state, such as which processes are running and how its power settings and clock are configured, expires far sooner, in minutes rather than days. These spans are chosen rather than derived from any physical property: they trade the effort of regenerating evidence against the risk that the machine has changed since the evidence was written, and the 168-hour span was set so that a campaign of successive nightly windows can complete without regenerating its slowest-moving evidence partway through. A shorter span would make the instrument stricter and a longer one would make it looser, which is why the value belongs in this section — a reader judging how fresh the evidence behind a result is needs the number rather than the word “fresh.” One condition does not scale with the span at all: every piece of this evidence is tied to the particular start-up of the machine that produced it, so restarting the machine invalidates it however much of its span remains.

**Load-regime transfer.** The calibration pulses are graphics-processor matrix multiplications under light central-processor load; transferring their timing bound to sustained mixed-load inference is an explicit assumption, constrained by the in-window bracket and the planned empirical floor probes but not eliminated. The drift allowance is measured per window with no duration-scaling law because the evidence does not identify one; longer windows than those characterized would need fresh drift evidence.

**When timing errors may be shared within a block.** The conservative calculation in Section 3 lets every member take an independently adverse timing endpoint. An alternative calculation uses one onset error and one offset error shared by all four runs in an ABBA block, while still letting each run's remaining timing residual take its adverse value. This can be used only when the four runs share the same phase geometry and the collection plan fixed that calculation before data existed. It refuses a block with nonseparable windows or any geometry outside that domain. Exact-arithmetic tests found no understatement in \(4{,}096\) generated cases, but that validates the calculation, not the physical premise: calibration bounds possible errors and does not observe each run's realized error. Every result using shared errors must therefore disclose the block-timescale sharing assumption. No current comparative result is issued, so no magnitude from the older replay appears here.

**Rule for enough overlapping power records.** Each *powermetrics* record is an average over a support interval. Count a record for a phase only when its support has positive overlap with the phase: \(\min(t_{\mathrm{phase,end}},t_{\mathrm{sample,end}})>\max(t_{\mathrm{phase,start}},t_{\mathrm{sample,start}})\). A phase is labeled resolvable at the sampler rate only when at least three records overlap; fewer than three produces `not_resolvable_sample_count`. Partially overlapping boundary records count because their energy contribution is clipped to the exact overlap.

**Sequential requests only.** Phase boundaries are well posed because one request runs at a time. *Continuous batching* means that a server inserts new requests into a batch while earlier requests are still generating; *speculative decoding* means that a smaller draft model proposes several tokens that a target model then accepts or rejects. Both mechanisms, and multi-request serving generally, overlap work from different requests or models. They are out of scope until a new protocol names the power-allocation boundary and calibrates it.

**Operational constraints as evidence.** Claim-bearing windows require a machine quiescent to the admission gates—no interactive agent sessions, displays asleep, and background work settled. Captures of the idle machine, kept permanently outside the claim path, show an idle power level that is low but bursty. It is the bursts, not the average, that threaten a clearance of a few joules, because subtracting an idle average removes only the steady part. The zero-agent window rule is therefore load-bearing, and results collected outside it would not pass the entry checks.

## 8. Related work

### Energy-benchmarking lineage

Energy benchmarking has a two-decade methodological lineage that JouleWise inherits rather than replaces. JouleSort argued that an energy-efficiency benchmark is incomplete unless it fixes a workload, a metric of comparison, and rules for running the workload and measuring energy, and it made the measurement boundary a first-class part of those rules: all power taken at the wall including conversion losses, every participating component counted even when idle, and any net change in stored battery energy shown to be no greater than zero with 95% confidence or included in the total [3]. It also named, without quantifying, the failure mode this paper measures: it rejected a fixed-energy-budget metric partly because of “inaccuracies in synchronizing readings from a power meter to the actual runs,” alongside the meter's own ±1.5% specification. Industry benchmarks then codified those run rules: SPECpower_ssj2008 fixed a graduated-load server workload with an accepted-analyzer and reporting discipline [15]; the SPEC methodology bounds analyzer uncertainty at 1% AC over the ranges actually measured, forbids automatic range transitions because uncertainty is undefined during them, and requires unknown or over-uncertainty samples to be detected and reported rather than silently averaged [2]; TPC-Energy added an audited energy metric to transaction-processing benchmarks [16]; and the Green500 ranked supercomputers by performance per watt [17]. Full-system power modeling took the complementary route of validating an estimate against a measured reference: Mantis calibrated once per system against measured AC power and was validated on two very different servers to within roughly 10% [4], and later work compared several such high-level models across machines from laptop to server [18]. JouleWise sits inside this tradition but differs on three axes that change what must be published: its boundary is a software counter over the processor package's CPU, GPU, and neural-engine rails rather than a wall meter, its unit of claim is a sub-request phase rather than a whole run, and the uncertainty it must disclose is not an analyzer specification but a measured bound on where a phase edge can fall. That is why the detection floor, rather than the joule value, is the primary result — and why the timing-synchronization concern JouleSort raised in passing becomes, at phase granularity on an uncalibrated software counter, the dominant term.

### LLM inference energy measurement

TokenPowerBench provides one of the closest reporting structures: it reports prefill energy and decode energy-per-token and groups results by context length [7]. Its disclosed method, however, does not specify the boundary events, alignment rule, repetition and variance protocol, decode-token denominator, or external validation; no idle-baseline subtraction is defined; and batch sizes are swept from 32 to 1024 under vLLM, an open-source model-serving runtime, and NVIDIA TensorRT-LLM, an optimized NVIDIA serving runtime, so requests overlap by construction. JouleWise adopts phase-specific reporting but restricts its primary scope to one sequential request, where runtime-emitted phase boundaries are well posed and can be calibrated.

Ruf and Detyniecki isolate prefill by generating exactly one token and derive decode by subtraction, using a single run per context length with no error bars [19] — a fresh prefill-focused instance of phase separation by workload construction rather than calibrated boundary events.

The Illusion of Power Capping in LLM Decode samples graphics-processor power, integrates those samples, repeats configurations, and cross-checks sufficiently long operations against a separate hardware energy counter [20]. This is a stronger measurement template than an unqualified software reading. Its counter agreement, run-to-run variation, and sub-100 ms snapshot-fallback regime nevertheless remain separate diagnostics rather than one bound on the reported effect. It monitors die temperature (42–45 °C under its clamp), but does not counterbalance run order. JouleWise composes those classes of uncertainty into a decision rule for each contrast.

Apple-focused system comparisons reinforce the need for boundary labels. Silicon Showdown compares a graphics-board boundary on one platform with an on-chip *powermetrics* boundary on Apple hardware, while runtimes and precision-related software configurations also differ [21]. Intelligence per Watt and ML.ENERGY similarly emphasize breadth across deployed inference configurations [22] [8]. Such studies answer valuable systems questions, but because their measurement boundaries and software stacks differ, they cannot by themselves support a hardware-class causal ranking. JouleWise therefore makes the named configuration and measurement boundary part of every result and prioritizes within-boundary effects.

### Software power counters and measurement standards

RAPL in Action established a validation agenda for commodity software counters: align lag before comparing streams, model wall power rather than assuming identity, account for temporal correlation, warm the system, measure sampler overhead, and inspect update granularity, overflow, jitter, and timestamp behavior [5]. Jay and Ostapenco likewise regress software readings against wall power and show that disagreement varies with load; they avoid claims about subtotals that the reference meter cannot observe [6]. Dauner et al. ask how consistent the RAPL and NVML counters are with themselves as the sampling rate changes, checked against a wall meter. They find that NVML's running totals update no faster than about every 100 ms, and that a flagship GPU's counter underestimates integrated power by up to 95% when sampled every 0.5 ms, recovering only at 1 s [23]. On different counters, that independently establishes the point this paper depends on: how often a software counter updates can quietly bias an energy reading, which is why the roughly 112 ms *powermetrics* sampling interval is treated here as a first-order limit of the instrument. Their work characterizes counter behaviour over a whole workload, with no phase boundaries in it; JouleWise instead characterizes where a boundary defined by the runtime falls inside a request, and turns that into a gate a claim must pass. Cao et al. provide an empirical prior from natural-language-processing workloads: software counters carry 2–47% error against a hardware meter, with software variance twice the meter's [24]; like [5] and [6], this is total-energy evidence only, so the wall-meter-cannot-adjudicate-phases hinge remains. Panigrahy and Tyagi supply the sibling audit of a platform where per-process attribution is impossible because no counter exists [25]; JouleWise shows that a counter's presence is not sufficient — its timing behavior must be characterized before it licenses phase attribution, and absent that characterization the correct output is refusal. JouleWise follows this epistemic boundary. A future external meter could test total gain and load dependence, but it would not validate how a total is divided between prompt processing and token generation. The in-window pulse train addresses that distinct question.

MLPerf Power supplies the broad benchmark setting and public-run discipline, including Network Time Protocol (NTP) synchronization between the system under test and the control machine [1]. The associated SPEC Power and Performance Methodology supplies the load-specific analyzer uncertainty, fixed ranges, minimum intervals, invalid-sample accounting, and controlled battery behavior attributed here [2]. JouleWise translates their per-run reject-on-missing-evidence principle to a consumer software counter. Its principal uncertainty is not only an analyzer specification; it includes whether samples at a software-defined phase edge belong inside or outside the integral.

### Metrology and experimental discipline

Prospective paired minimum-detectable-effect methods estimate the smallest effect a planned paired study has adequate statistical power to find. They size a study from paired variation and can impose a one-way prior ratchet: observed variability may raise the planned detection limit but may not lower it after outcomes are seen [26]. JouleWise uses that logic for prospective design, but it does not treat the worst-case attribution term as independent random noise. Root-sum-square composition would be anti-conservative for a systematic edge-placement bound. Instead, the analysis maximizes over calibrated edge positions, measures drift with bracketed references, and publishes the resulting floor with the effect.

This emphasis changes the role of failed runs. A rejected admission, stale calibration, contaminated member, or below-floor contrast is evidence about the instrument's operating domain. Fingerprinted raw traces, runtime events, configurations, replacement records, verdicts, and fixed-before-collection plans allow that evidence to be reduced again under the specified process. The refusal is retained rather than disappearing behind a final average.

### Split and disaggregated inference

Revisiting Disaggregated Large Language Model Serving reports essentially unconditionally higher energy for disaggregation on its evaluated two-graphics-processor node, while its performance benefits depend on load, baseline, and transfer conditions [27]. DualScale models graphics-processor-only phase energy by regression [12]; Prima.cpp reports whole-run but not stage energy [10]; and SplitZip makes no energy claim [28].

JouleWise claims no scheduler; split inference is a future instrument demonstration requiring both endpoints, named boundaries, cross-device clock alignment, and a floor fixed before collection or a calibrated bridge.

## 10. Conclusion

JouleWise treats a software power counter as a scientific instrument whose operating domain must be measured. The present evidence shows why: on the studied Apple-silicon configuration, phase-boundary attribution can dominate repeatability, so more runs alone do not make a small contrast trustworthy. The final paper will report the planned characterization and two-model demonstration with separate floor and direction gates, including any refusal the recorded evidence requires.

The reusable contribution is the combination of six habits: calibrate the instrument inside the same session it measures; build the detection floor only out of quantities you have measured; work out before collecting whether the planned experiment can resolve the effect it names; alternate the run order so slow drift cannot pass for a real difference; refuse rather than guess when the evidence is missing or inconsistent; and let a single tool write the record, tying every file to it by cryptographic fingerprint. Those habits may transfer to other software counters, but the floor's numerical value will not; every new instrument, boundary, and hardware-and-software configuration must establish its own. Future work can add independent hardware-and-software configurations, model families, an external meter for total-energy scale, and richer phase structures such as speculative decoding; mixture-of-experts routing, which sends each token through selected expert subnetworks; and split inference across devices.

## Appendix A. Reproducing this work

This appendix is written for two different readers, and it keeps their jobs apart throughout because they need different things. **Re-derivation** means recomputing the paper's published quantities from the preserved raw files. It needs no Apple hardware, no elevated privileges, and no software beyond Python itself. **Fresh collection** means producing new measurements, and it needs the specific machine, operating system, model files, and quiet conditions named below. A reviewer who wants to know whether the published numbers follow from the published bytes is doing the first job only.

Three words recur below and are fixed here, because the rest of the appendix rests on them. A *fingerprint* is a SHA-256 digest: a string of sixty-four lowercase hexadecimal characters computed from a file's exact bytes, which changes if any byte changes. A *refusal* is a recorded decision not to produce a result from the evidence offered, carrying a named reason code; Section A.4 explains what a replicator should conclude when one appears. A *revision* is one recorded state of the source code, named by the identifier its version-control system assigns, so that "the program that produced this file" is a checkable statement rather than a description.

### A.1 What a reader needs

**To re-derive the published numbers: Python 3.11 or later, and nothing else.** The `joulewise` package declares no third-party dependencies. Every module the analysis path imports ships with Python itself — the "standard library," hence *stdlib-only*. This is a fixed constraint on the project rather than a property the current code happens to have, and it is what makes the verification path checkable by a reviewer who declines to install anything. The environment that produced the retained reductions is recorded at `env/analysis-lock.txt` as a *constraints file*: a list of exact package versions used to pin an installation, never a list of packages to install. That file is nearly empty, and its emptiness is the evidence for the stdlib-only claim rather than an assertion about it.

**Where the stdlib-only claim stops.** Two optional dependency groups exist. In Python packaging these are called *extras*: named groups of dependencies installed only when a reader asks for them by name, and absent otherwise.

- The `mac` extra installs the inference runtime and tokenizer libraries. It is required to *run* a model, and therefore to collect new measurements. It cannot be installed on non-Apple hardware. It is not needed to re-derive anything in this paper.
- The `analysis` extra installs a plotting library used for one optional report figure. No published number depends on it. It was deliberately left uninstalled in the recorded analysis environment, so that the lock file records the environment that actually produced the retained reductions rather than a tidier one.

Both extras are imported lazily — the import happens inside the function that needs the library, not when the package loads — so a command that needs one and cannot find it stops with a named failure rather than an import error at start-up. The absence is visible rather than silent. The measurement environment is pinned separately at `env/mac-measurement-lock.txt`, thirty-seven pinned packages, whose header records the host it was frozen from: macOS 15.7.7 build 24G720 on Apple silicon, under Python 3.13.1.

**To collect fresh measurements: an Apple-silicon Mac, plus four things that are checked rather than assumed.** The retained corpus was collected on an Apple M3 Max. 1. *Permission to read the power counter.* The *powermetrics* utility runs with administrator privilege. JouleWise never prompts for a password, because a prompt during a measurement would itself disturb the machine; it invokes the counter non-interactively and refuses with an explicit instruction when that is not configured. 2. *A quiet machine.* Every entry condition in Section 4 — mains power connected, low-power mode off, displays asleep, screensaver off, nominal thermal pressure, and an idle baseline meeting the stated percentile limits — is evaluated by the program immediately before each stage. 3. *A working copy dedicated to measurement.* Collection runs from a checkout held at a fixed absolute path, recorded in the window's own environment record, and never from the copy a person is editing. The reason is custody — the unbroken record of which bytes produced which result — rather than tidiness: a working copy carrying uncommitted edits cannot be named by a single revision, so its evidence could not identify the code that produced it. The retained plans record that path inside themselves, so a reader can see which checkout produced a plan.
4. *The model files themselves.* Two instruction-tuned models of one family at roughly 1.5 and 7 billion parameters, both 4-bit quantized, are named in the campaign plan. A reader who substitutes a re-quantized copy of the same nominal model is measuring a different condition, and the plan is what makes that substitution detectable rather than invisible.

**The code repository contains no measured evidence.** Every directory that holds run data is excluded from version control by design, so a reader who obtains the code has the programs and the plans fixed before collection, but none of the bytes those programs consumed. The measured evidence is released as a separate archive. The two halves are bound to each other in both directions: a plan names the source revision and the estimator source fingerprints it was frozen against, and a result names the plan and every input file it read.

**Why the release is shaped this way: freezing, and the two ordering gates.** The pattern above exists to answer one question mechanically — *was this pass criterion fixed before the data it judges existed?* — and the project adopted it because the answer was once no. A condition that had refused three cells was converted from a hard refusal into a labelled claim path on the same day the data behind those cells were collected. Nothing in the pipeline could refuse that edit, because nothing had fixed the criterion in the first place. The repair is not a rule against editing; it is putting every limit inside a fingerprinted specification rather than in prose or in analysis code, so that changing one requires a new predecessor-linked freeze and leaves a trace. A limit written in prose can be changed by an editing pass with no receipt. A limit written into the frozen specification cannot.

Two checks then make "fixed before collection" decidable without trusting anyone's account of the order. One program issues the report; it recomputes every fingerprint itself, and rejects any input field that tries to nominate its own estimator or its own limit. Before it evaluates anything, it applies both gates:

- *The criteria must predate the data.* The writer refuses unless the freeze record's issue time strictly precedes the capture time of every admitted member.
- *A borrowed limit must predate the freeze that borrows it.* Where a criterion's limit comes from a separately issued artifact rather than from the data it judges, the writer refuses unless that supplier carries a strictly earlier *freeze ordinal* — a counter that increases by one with each freeze, so that "earlier" is decided by ordering rather than by comparing clocks.

A refusal from either gate is protocol failure rather than a result: the writer emits no report at all. That distinction matters to a replicator, because it means a violation of collection order cannot be laundered into a published row that merely reads as failed.

### A.3 Verifying everything, in order

The checks below run in the order the custody chain was built, because each step's output is the next step's input: the code, then the plans fixed before collection, then the collected bundles, then the calibration bound, then the window decision, then the floors, then the claim verdicts. A reader may stop at any point. Each step states what it proves and — just as importantly — what it does not.

Every command reports a *return code* when it finishes: the number a program hands back to the shell, conventionally zero when everything it checked passed. Where a program uses more than two return codes, the meanings are given, because in this pipeline a nonzero return does not always mean that nothing was produced.

**Steps 1–6 need only the code and a Python interpreter.** They were executed as printed, in a checkout holding no measured evidence, and each returned zero.

*Step 1 — obtain the code at the revision the release names.* Clone the repository normally and check out exactly that revision. Two cautions are load-bearing rather than stylistic. A checkout carrying local edits cannot be identified by a revision, and every later step binds its result to one. And the clone must carry full history: the receipt chain of Step 6 replays archived source trees against the commits they were pinned to, so a depth-limited clone lacks the objects it needs and will fail for that reason rather than for a custody reason.

*Step 2 — confirm the verification path needs nothing but Python.* This checks the stdlib-only claim of Section A.1 rather than asserting it. The command imports every module the analysis path uses and prints the set of imported top-level modules that are not part of Python's own standard library. The `-S` flag suppresses the interpreter's site-configuration hooks, so that a package installed system-wide cannot be mistaken for a dependency of this one.

```sh
python3 -S -c "
import sys, importlib
for m in ('joulewise.cli','joulewise.reduce','joulewise.floor_extraction','joulewise.whole_window',
          'joulewise.uncertainty_evidence','joulewise.powermetrics_fiducial','joulewise.detection_floor'):
    importlib.import_module(m)
extra = sorted({n.split('.')[0] for n in sys.modules}
               - set(sys.stdlib_module_names) - {'joulewise', '__main__'})
print('third-party modules imported:', extra)
"
```

Proves: the reduction, floor-extraction, uncertainty, calibration-detector, and command-line modules import no third-party package, so nothing in the steps below can depend on one. It prints an empty list. Does not prove anything about the collection path, which does require the `mac` extra of Section A.1.

*Step 3 — confirm the plans fixed before collection carry the bytes the paper describes.* Each fixed plan file has a sidecar in the format the system checksum tool reads, so this check needs no JouleWise code at all — which is the point: it can be run by a reader who does not yet trust the programs under review.

```sh

shasum -a 256 -c plan_tree.sha256 calibration_plan.sha256
```

Proves: the plan tree and calibration plan carry the bytes their sidecars record, so the analysis plan has not been edited since it was fixed. Does not prove that the sidecar is itself the published one; that binding comes from the freeze receipt, which names the plan by fingerprint, and ultimately from the release manifest.

*Step 4 — confirm the project's generated documents agree with their source of record.* Parts of the project's running-state and task documents are rendered from one structured source file and enclosed by markers; a hand edit inside a marked region would silently desynchronize the document from its source.

```sh
python3 scripts/gen_state.py --check
```

Proves: the structured source is itself in canonical form, and every generated region matches what that source renders right now. Return codes: `0` exact agreement, `1` drift between source and document, `2` an invalid source or missing markers. It prints nothing on success; silence is the pass.

*Step 5 — confirm the assembled report matches the artifacts it was assembled from.* The report assembler recomputes the fingerprint of every artifact its manifest lists, regenerates its results page from those artifacts, re-resolves every file inclusion, and compares the result with the committed output.

```sh
python3 scripts/build_capstone.py --profile rpt001 --offline --check
```

Proves: no part of the assembled report was edited away from what its sources produce, and no listed artifact has changed bytes. `--offline` forbids network access during the check, so the outcome depends only on files present locally. `--check` writes nothing. Return codes: `0` no drift, `2` drift.

*Step 6 — verify the receipt chain from version-control objects.* This is the strongest check available without the evidence archive. It walks the retained campaign plans and re-verifies every freeze and evidence receipt against the source trees they were pinned to.

```sh
python3 scripts/verify_receipt_histsem.py --repository-root . --require-published
```

Proves: across the nine retained campaign plans, ninety-nine receipts verify, each linked to its predecessor, with no gap and no rewritten link. `--require-published` refuses to accept a receipt that has not been published rather than passing it as provisional. This is the step that fails on a depth-limited clone, for the reason given in Step 1.

**Steps 7–11 need the evidence archive.** They consume collected bundles, which are excluded from the code repository by design (Section A.2), so they cannot run in a code-only checkout. Each program's interface can still be inspected anywhere by asking it for `--help`; only the runs need the archive. Below, `<runs root>` is the directory the release archive unpacks to. Two of these steps write files or append to a log, and each says so — they are re-derivations, not read-only inspections, and a replicator should run them against a copy of the archive.

It is read-only: it does not rewrite the bundle it validates. Does not prove that this bundle belongs to the window — that is Step 9.

*Step 8 — re-derive the calibration bound from primary bytes, ignoring the stored answer.* This check runs through the detector's own verification entry point rather than a command-line flag. ### A.4 What a refusal means to a replicator

A *refusal* is not an error, a crash, or a defect report. It is an outcome of the measurement method: a recorded decision that the evidence offered does not authorize the result requested, together with the name of the condition that failed. Four consequences follow for anyone reproducing this work.

**A refusal is reproducible, and that is what the reason name is for.** Given the same bytes and the same fixed plan, the programs return the same refusal under the same name. A replicator who runs the walkthrough of Section A.3 over the released archive and obtains a refusal has not failed to reproduce the paper; they have reproduced a published outcome, provided the name matches the one the release records. A refusal whose *name* differs from the published one is the genuine discrepancy and is worth reporting, because it means two different conditions are being reached from the same evidence.

**The names form a closed vocabulary, and an unrecognized name stops rendering rather than passing through.** The floor-extraction and whole-window programs each carry an explicit registry of the reasons they may emit — among them `clock_anchor_unresolved`; `capture_pipeline_absent` and `capture_pipeline_superseded`, which separate evidence that names no current capture method from evidence that names a retired one, deliberately kept as distinct events; `whole_window_verdict_conflict`; `instrument_calibration_stale`; `negative_power_sample`; and `cooldown_cap_hit`. The characterization vocabulary is kept disjoint from the others, so that no coincidental refusal raised elsewhere in the pipeline can stand in for a required characterization refusal, and no characterization name is ever emitted by another component. Where an upstream reason must be carried along, it is preserved verbatim in its own field rather than translated into a nearby name.
<!-- evidence: joulewise/floor_extraction.py:190 ff reason registry; joulewise/whole_window.py:199 ff; docs/contracts/characterization_result_schema_v1.md:239-245 disjoint vocabulary, :296 unlisted code stops rendering, :674 raw_source_code preserved verbatim -->

**A refusal is not evidence that the two things compared are equal.** This is the misreading the paper most wants to prevent. A refused contrast says that the named implementation, under the recorded conditions, cannot adjudicate the difference. The effect may be real and below what this instrument resolves, or it may be absent, and the evidence does not separate those two cases. Section 7 states the scientific use of a refusal: it maps the operating domain of the measurement method rather than the world.

**Refusals are preserved, and preserved in place.** Failed or interrupted artifacts are never deleted or overwritten. A retry does not reuse the slot of the attempt it replaces: the occupied slot is moved to a retained quarantine directory outside the active collection path, the retry writes a new slot, and an append-only replacement record states which occurrence is current and which one it supersedes. Two present bundles claiming the same occurrence — the same declared slot in the fixed plan — cause a refusal rather than a silent choice between them. For a replicator this has a concrete consequence: the archive contains more members than the analysis admits, by design, and a member's presence in the archive is never evidence that it was used. The verdict of Section A.2 is what says which members were.

**One worked case.** At the end of one collection night, re-evaluation under the recorded rules refused an entire window because one member's internal clock alignment could not be resolved. Independent adjudication found the refusal correct. That window's data remain in the archive, remain fully auditable, and remain incapable of supporting a claim. That is the fail-closed design of Section 4 working as designed, not a software defect that some later run repaired.

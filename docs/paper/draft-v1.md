# JouleWise: Attribution-Limited Detection Floors for Phase-Resolved LLM Inference Energy on Apple Silicon

*Draft: demonstration values, instrument-characterization results, and archive locators are pending; bracketed markers show where they will land.*

## Abstract

Published energy figures for large language model (LLM) inference rarely say how small a difference the measurement could actually detect. That question becomes sharp when a request is split into its two phases: prompt processing, which reads the input and builds the model's internal state, and token generation, which produces the output one token at a time. Power drops steeply right where the line between them falls, because prompt processing keeps the compute units busy while generation spends much of its time waiting on memory. Draw that line tens of milliseconds late across a step of tens of watts and about a joule of energy is charged to the wrong phase. The total for the request is unchanged, so repeating the run cannot fix which phase the energy went to. JouleWise therefore treats Apple's *powermetrics* counter as a scientific instrument. The runtime reports where each phase begins and ends; the counter is calibrated inside the same session in which it is used; and every eligible result carries a *detection floor* — the largest difference the measurement system can produce on its own, under the conditions admitted for that comparison, when nothing has really changed. The floor is the larger of two measured guards, one from repeating a single condition and one from comparing two conditions deliberately made identical, and it is then widened for the calibrated timing uncertainty and for measured drift. A comparison must be larger than that floor, and its entire uncertainty range must point the same way; neither requirement is ever waived. Missing, out-of-date, or inconsistent evidence produces a *refusal*: a recorded decision not to publish an optimistic result. The characterization retained here supports no claim of its own. It comes from 30 runs in window a10 that repeat a single phase, with window a9 supplying reference and bracketing-calibration context: the combined bound on where a phase edge may fall spans 25.6–31.1 ms, and the phase energy this leaves uncertain is on the order of a joule. Everything reported holds for one Apple-silicon machine, one software stack (the complete hardware-and-software configuration), and one measurement boundary (the part of the machine's power the counter actually sees). The demonstration applies the same discipline to phase-by-phase measurements of two model sizes, and will report for each pre-registered comparison whether it can be resolved or must be refused.

## 1. Introduction

Energy numbers for large language model (LLM) inference now appear in leaderboards, systems papers, product comparisons, and policy debates. Published estimates for apparently similar queries can vary widely, yet the numbers are rarely accompanied by an error bar that says how small a difference the measurement method can actually distinguish. This omission is especially consequential on consumer hardware. Apple's *powermetrics* utility makes processor power estimates available without a laboratory meter and is therefore attractive for repeated experiments, but, to our knowledge, it has not been validated in published work as an instrument for separating the energy of LLM inference phases. Apple describes its outputs as estimates; treating them as exact readings can turn timing uncertainty at a phase boundary into a spurious energy difference.

The core difficulty is physical as much as statistical. Energy is the integral of power over time. An experiment may repeat consistently and still assign energy to the wrong phase if a power sample near the boundary between prompt processing and token generation is placed on the wrong side. Averaging more repetitions reduces random scatter but does not remove that attribution error. Slow changes in thermal or background state can add a second false difference over a long collection session.

Energy is obtained by integrating power over a declared interval, so the validity of an energy value depends on both the power reading and the interval boundaries. Formal benchmark methods make this a run-level question: instrument uncertainty, clock synchronization, measurement duration, invalid samples, fixed ranges, and battery behavior must be checked for the interval that produced the result [1] [2]. The full-system energy-efficiency benchmark lineage beginning with JouleSort established that an energy claim is incomplete without all of a declared workload, a metric of comparison, and rules for running the workload and measuring energy — and that those rules must fix the measurement boundary explicitly [3]. The central lesson across this lineage is that a meter specification alone does not validate a measurement.

Software-visible counters make repeated experiments practical, but they remain measurement systems rather than ground truth. Early full-system power-modeling work such as Mantis already validated model outputs against a measured reference before trusting them [4], and later validation work on Intel's Running Average Power Limit counters compares software readings with wall power, searches for lag, measures overhead and update behavior, controls thermal state, and limits claims when no reference instrument observes the quantity of interest [5] [6]. A wall meter can test whole-machine totals. It cannot by itself determine whether a software sample belongs to prompt processing or token generation, so phase attribution requires a separate timing experiment.

Recent LLM studies report energy across models, hardware, and workloads, sometimes separating prompt processing from token generation [7] [8]. This work establishes the value of phase-resolved reporting, but a phase label is easy to read as exact when the method does not publish its boundary events, alignment uncertainty, or minimum resolvable effect. The conceptual gap is therefore not another energy table; it is a method that connects phase boundaries, counter behavior, drift, uncertainty, and refusal in one claim decision.

We have found no published work that does all three of these at once: report LLM energy separately for each phase on consumer hardware, attach an error budget to every measurement, and check how accurately *powermetrics* places events in time — all under a stated measurement boundary. Nearby results sharpen rather than close that gap. Apple vs. Oranges uses *powermetrics* for Apple-silicon efficiency measurements without calibrating phase attribution [9]. Prima.cpp's appendix records energy with *powermetrics* but provides no calibration or uncertainty treatment [10]. AgentStop, a 2026 agentic-LLM study at a peer-reviewed venue, uses the same instrument at the same 100 ms cadence and integrates by trapezoid with an idle subtraction. It claims a directional 15–20% energy saving without calibration or uncertainty treatment, exactly the situation a detection floor is meant to gate [11]. DualScale acknowledges that NVML's averaged coarse-interval samples “do not align exactly with individual decode iterations” [12]. Benazir and Lin characterize latency, throughput, and microarchitecture on the same Apple-silicon hardware but report no energy [13]. Energy-aware neural-architecture-search guidelines further show that software counter choice can materially disagree with an external meter [14]. JouleWise makes instrument characterization and refusal behavior the primary result. Model comparisons demonstrate what the characterized implementation can and cannot resolve.

We argue that a software power counter should be treated as a scientific instrument rather than as a logging convenience. The instrument must be calibrated in the same session in which it is used; each reported result must carry a detection floor; and the analysis must decline a directional claim when the observed effect cannot pass both the floor and direction gates. JouleWise implements this discipline for phase-resolved, single-request LLM inference on one named Apple-silicon machine and software stack. The runtime emits the phase boundaries because it drives the workload, while *powermetrics* supplies the power samples integrated between those boundaries.

This scope is deliberately narrow. Each measurement describes exactly one configuration: one physical machine, one operating-system build, one runtime and library stack, one model file, one quantization (the numerical representation used for the model weights), one tokenizer (the rule that turns text into token IDs and back), one token-selection policy, one single-request execution policy, one telemetry source, and one measurement boundary. It does not establish that an Apple hardware class or vendor is more efficient than another platform. Without an external power meter, the absolute joule values mean something only inside the boundary the counter itself reports: the CPU, GPU, and neural-engine power rails of the processor package, and not display, memory, storage, or peripheral power. Comparisons made inside that same boundary can still be scientifically useful, provided they pass the calibration and floor gates. Total joules per request is the primary energy metric. Joules per prompt token and per output token are reported alongside it, but only ever within one tokenizer: two tokenizers cut the same text into different numbers of tokens, so a per-token figure is not a unit of work that can be carried across them.

This paper makes the following contributions:

1. A calibration that runs inside the same uninterrupted collection session as the measurements it supports — we call such a session a *measurement window* — and that measures how far the line between two phases can be misplaced in time when energy is added up between them.
2. Detection floors built only from things we measured: how much repeated runs of one condition vary, how much energy the worst allowed misplacement of a phase edge could move, and how much the machine drifts over the session. A floor is published with every result eligible to carry one.
3. A collection protocol that refuses rather than guesses: every run must pass its entry checks before it counts; run order alternates so slow drift cannot pass for a real difference; every file is tied to the record by cryptographic hash — a short fingerprint that changes if any byte of the file changes — with a single tool allowed to write that record; the analysis plan is fixed before any data exist; and everything the protocol declines to use is logged rather than discarded.
4. A planned program for characterizing the instrument. It asks how measured energy responds when the workload is scaled up on this stack; whether comparing two conditions that are secretly identical still comes out near zero at short, medium, and long run lengths; how the floors behave in practice; whether the energy assigned to each phase adds up correctly and stays free of leakage from the other phase; how much the machine drifts; and how long it needs to settle after being disturbed. Three pieces are still unfinished: the small deliberate differences used to probe the decision path, the exact comparison that will fix the settling time, and the authoritative format for recording results.
5. A demonstration whose design is written down and frozen before any data are collected: phase-by-phase measurements of two model sizes, with one registered comparison for token generation and one for prompt processing using a fixed 256-token prompt.
6. An open tool, and a planned release of the evidence files together with their cryptographic fingerprints, so that readers can redo the analysis from the raw records and detect any file that differs from the bytes we published.

## 2. In-window calibration method

Terminology used below is fixed as follows. A *window* is one uninterrupted collection session governed by one power state, instrument identity, calibration bracket, drift record, and final verdict. A *calibration bracket* is the authenticated pre-window and post-window calibration evidence that surrounds a science window. A *stage* is one ordered collection step within a window. An *arm* is one pre-registered workload or comparison track within a campaign. A *campaign* is the frozen plan that groups related windows, stages, and arms. A *pack* is the frozen campaign-plan directory and authenticated sidecars that govern one planned window. A *cell* is one condition family that shares the declared telemetry backend, metric, interval type, workload profile, and stack identity. A *member* is one recorded workload run. Its *bundle* is the self-contained record of configuration, raw power trace, runtime events, environment observations, and derived summary. *Claim-bearing* evidence is eligible to support a reported claim, whereas *diagnostic* evidence can inform design or interpretation but cannot authorize one. A *verdict* is the final governed decision to admit or refuse the declared evidence. The *mint* is the governed issuing tool that produces a floor artifact from authenticated inputs. *Reduction* is the governed transformation of preserved raw evidence into derived results; *re-reduction* repeats that transformation under updated authorized inputs without altering the source evidence.

### Measurement model and boundary

JouleWise measures one sequential request at a time. The two phases are also called *prefill* and *decode*, respectively. Because the experiment controls the runtime, it records the start and stop of these phases directly in the runtime event stream rather than inferring them from shapes in the power trace. The reducer then integrates the three named *powermetrics* processor power channels — CPU, GPU, and neural engine — between each pair of phase events; no other channel enters the integral. Each sampled value is treated as the average power over its reported sampling interval, so the integral is a sum of interval-average power multiplied by interval duration, with the boundary intervals clipped at the phase events.

The analysis counts whole runs, and whole A/B/B/A blocks, as its independent units. The individual power samples inside a run, and the items inside a workload, are not extra repeats.

The runtime and the telemetry sampler do not share a perfect clock. Operating-system launch latency, timestamp anchoring, and the sampler's averaging behavior can shift an apparent edge. A phase-energy estimate must therefore be an interval, not only a point. The calibration asks a concrete question: if software commands a load pulse at a known time, how far can the rising and falling edges of that pulse appear displaced in the sampled power trace?

### Bracketed pulse-train calibration

The runbook places a calibration stage immediately before and immediately after every science window whose data may support a claim. The launcher checks this rather than trusting the operator to have done it: each of the two calibrations must be authenticated, must genuinely fall before (or after) the science runs it brackets, must still be valid across the whole of the window, and must sit no more than 24 hours from the far end of that window. The released calibration protocol configuration commands 59 graphics-processor matrix-multiplication pulses and specifies quiet baselines of at least 4.5 seconds between fitted regions. Pulse durations are fixed in advance, and the gaps follow a deterministic low-discrepancy schedule, an ordering designed to spread gap positions evenly rather than repeat one period. The varied schedule reduces the risk that calibration accidentally locks to the telemetry sampler's cadence. Each capture reports the maximum of 59 pulse-level worst-edge residual bounds, plus the capture-local trace-anchor bound. If those 59 pulse-level statistics are independent, identically distributed draws from a specified stationary calibration-pulse population, the sample maximum exceeds that population's 95th percentile with probability \(1-0.95^{59}=0.9515\). The current protocol does not establish that the pulses are independent or interchangeable in that sense, so unless that is justified separately the number should be read as exactly what it is: the largest of 59 measured pulse bounds. Carrying it over to the phase edges of a real workload is a further assumption.

For each pulse, the estimator compares the commanded interval with the observed power plateau and fits the start-edge and stop-edge lags jointly. The evidence that survives is then reduced, conservatively, to a single number: how far the worse of the two edges could be displaced, counted equally in either direction. A common shift of both edges may leave pulse energy nearly unchanged, while an early start combined with a late stop can add energy at both boundaries. The calibration therefore retains a bound for the instrument's edge-placement error rather than relying on one best-fit lag. The pulse-fit search itself is capped by a preregistered evaluation budget: if the detector cannot converge on all 59 pulses within a fixed number of candidate evaluations, the capture is refused as non-convergent rather than accepted with a partial fit. ## 3. Detection-floor composition

No two runs are physically identical: temperature history, hardware frequency decisions, background work, and power-sample window placement can each produce joule differences when no real effect is present. The floor is the largest difference the measurement system can manufacture on its own under the admitted conditions. A smaller measured difference is therefore not a small effect; it is a number the instrument could produce with no effect at all.

The *detection floor* is a practical guard against seeing a difference that is not there, under the conditions that define a cell. It is not a claim to have estimated some percentile of a population exactly. JouleWise computes separate absolute and comparative floors and takes their maximum for the cell. An absolute floor measures how far repeated measurements of the same condition wander from their mean. A comparative floor measures the apparent difference between labels that are deliberately made identical and collected in A/B/B/A order.

### Repeatability and false-comparison guards

For an absolute cell, \(E_i\) is the energy in joules of the \(i\)th valid bundle, \(\bar E\) is the mean energy, \(r_i=E_i-\bar E\) is its residual, \(s_r\) is the sample standard deviation of those residuals, and \(n\) is the number of valid bundles. The point repeatability guard is

\[
F_{\mathrm{abs,point}}=\max\left(\max_i |r_i|,
t_{0.975,n-1}s_r\sqrt{1+1/n}\right).
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{abs,point}}\) is the absolute point guard in joules, and \(t_{0.975,n-1}\) is the 97.5th-percentile critical value of Student's \(t\) distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom.

For comparison, one A/B/B/A (ABBA) block contains runs \(A_1,B_1,B_2,A_2\). Its signed difference is

\[
\delta_i=(B_1+B_2-A_1-A_2)/2,
\]

so a positive \(\delta_i\) means condition B used more energy than condition A. For \(n\) valid blocks, \(\bar\delta\) is the mean block difference and \(s_\delta\) is its sample standard deviation. The comparative point guard is

\[
F_{\mathrm{cmp,point}}=\max\left(\max_i |\delta_i|,
|\bar\delta|+t_{0.975,n-1}s_\delta\sqrt{1+1/n}\right).
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{cmp,point}}\) is the comparative point guard in joules, and the Student-\(t\) subscript has the same meaning as above. The observed maxima protect against a false effect already seen; the prediction terms protect against one additional bundle or block. The Student-\(t\) prediction term is only as good as its assumptions: that after cooldown the run-to-run residuals, or the block differences, are roughly independent of one another, do not change their behaviour as the session goes on, and follow a normal distribution. The admission gates do not establish any of that. Shared calibration and drift terms are propagated separately as deterministic bounds. Small samples receive a pre-registered *guard factor*, a multiplicative safety margin, and fewer than five valid bundles or blocks are treated only as development evidence, not as a claim gate.

### Worst-case timing attribution

Point repeatability is not the full floor. Each energy value is an interval from the timing calibration in Section 2. The floor computation evaluates the complete point-floor estimator over the joint *corners* of all admitted member intervals—the combinations of their extreme endpoints—and then takes the largest value. For a null ABBA block, the block interval is first formed from the four signed member intervals. Onset and offset, and at floor level the member intervals, are allowed to take independently adverse endpoints. Taking every combination this way deliberately gives up any credit for errors that might cancel — because two runs' timing errors move together, or because both edges shift the same way. The result is a bound fixed by rule and chosen to be as unfavourable as the calibration allows. It is not a confidence interval, and it is not a root-sum-square combination, that is, adding the terms in quadrature, which would assume they are independent and vary at random. It may therefore include combinations that the joint pulse fit, or the physics of a shared shift, would rule out. Working through every combination exactly is currently supported for at most 16 admitted runs or blocks; beyond that the calculation refuses. The published component is never smaller than the largest value the full floor calculation reaches over any of those combinations. For repeatability cells, because residuals are measured about the mean, that value is not guaranteed to be as large as the widest single run's own energy interval.

This calculation revealed a stable and important limitation of the present implementation. Prompt processing saturates compute while token generation waits more heavily on memory, so power can fall steeply exactly where their boundary is drawn. Because energy is power multiplied by time, moving that boundary by tens of milliseconds across a tens-of-watts change can assign roughly a joule to the wrong phase. The request total is unchanged, which is why averaging cannot repair the attribution. Across the 30 phase-repeatability runs of window a10, the combined bound on where a phase edge may fall spans 25.6–31.1 ms. ### Measured, never-zero drift allowance

Drift is a slow change in the machine or measurement response over the collection window. It is measured rather than assumed away. The reference schedule is pre-registered per campaign: the two floor campaigns use three fixed references at the start, one at the midpoint, and three at the end, whereas the contrast campaign uses nine references — three at the start, singles after the 20th, 40th, and 60th science members, and three at the end. Total energy and idle-subtracted energy are treated as two separate families of claim for the window as a whole. For a request, idle-subtracted energy is its total energy minus the measured average idle power multiplied by how long the request took; phase energy is reported as a total only, because there is no defined model of what idle power belongs to a single phase. For each family, the protocol derives a repeatability bound from a settled reference corpus and measures the largest *excursion*, or departure, among the reference means. The allowance is

\[
A_{\mathrm{drift}}=\max(\text{observed start/mid/end excursion},
\text{derived reference-repeatability bound}).
\]

The subscript identifies this as the drift allowance. Consequently, a passing drift screen never means zero drift. The allowance remains positive even in an exceptionally stable window, and the midpoint protects against an interior excursion that similar endpoints would miss. The start, midpoint, and end points used to construct the maximum document the allowance but cannot validate it; containment must be evaluated on held-out probes or later sessions. No duration-scaling law is applied because the available evidence does not identify a physical law relating drift to elapsed time.

The original whole-window verdict files for a9 and a10 were not kept. The reference runs that were kept do, however, re-derive the drift figures exactly: the largest excursion for a10 is 0.5094 J on total energy and 0.5094 J on idle-subtracted energy, against allowances of 0.652 and 0.658 J; for a9 the excursions are 0.310 and 0.305 J against allowances of 0.624 and 0.609 J. Any statement that those drift screens passed therefore rests on this re-derivation rather than on a preserved verdict file.

For each component, repeatability or comparative, the guarded value after it has been widened over those endpoint combinations — the *corner-widened* value — is increased once by its matching drift allowance. Let \(F_{\mathrm{abs}}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{cmp}}\) denote those final absolute and comparative energy magnitudes in joules. The operative floor for a cell is

\[
F_{\mathrm{cell}}=\max(F_{\mathrm{abs}},F_{\mathrm{cmp}}),
\]

where \(F_{\mathrm{cell}}\) is also an energy magnitude in joules. The components are not summed. Cross-window components keep their own calibration basis and allowance; an allowance is never added again at the cell or reporting level. Operative floor values and their full decomposition for each demonstration stack are withheld until issued artifacts are available: **[RESULT PENDING ISSUED ARTIFACTS]**. An earlier reduction was superseded by a corrected one; the release notes identify it.

### Publication label and the two claim gates

A floor may be published with an explicit *attribution-limited* label — meaning that where the phase edges fall, rather than run-to-run scatter, is what sets it — but only when two conditions hold: that dominance is the only thing that would otherwise force a refusal, and an exact corner-widened floor has actually been computed. The retained characterization behind this label comes from the 30 phase-repeatability runs of window a10; a9 contributes reference and bracketing-calibration context only, and the figure's ±30 ms / 33 W / 1 J values remain illustrative. The published result names its dominant source: the energy that moves when the phase edges are shifted anywhere the calibrated clock-anchor bound allows. It publishes the corner-widened value rather than the smaller point value, and keeps the point-only repeatability number as a diagnostic that cannot support a claim. The label never rescues any other refusal: missing, stale, contaminated, or inconsistent evidence remains terminal.

A claim then faces two separate gates, enforced separately. First, the magnitude of its point estimate must exceed \(F_{\mathrm{cell}}\), the cell floor. Second, both endpoints of its *fully composed decision interval*—the interval after all registered statistical, attribution, and drift terms have been included—must support the pre-registered direction. Let \(B_{\mathrm{claim}}\) be the *claim-side bound*, the uncertainty carried by this particular comparison as distinct from the cell's floor. The project also discloses

\[
\text{practical joint-clearance size}=F_{\mathrm{cell}}+B_{\mathrm{claim}}.
\]

This sizing quantity must be reported for the particular cell rather than as a universal five-joule rule. For the retained token-generation contrast cell, the conservative floor alone is 8.611855 J; the claim-side bound remains a separate additional quantity in prospective sizing. Their sum explains how large an effect will generally need to be to clear both gates together and ensures that neither physical uncertainty term is hidden as an apparent double count. It is not a single summed acceptance threshold, and the decision interval is not compared with the sum. Effects that fail the floor gate are reported as *not resolvable*, not as zero, equal, or evidence of no difference. Effects that pass the floor gate but whose interval does not support direction remain unresolved and receive no directional claim.

## 4. Fail-closed collection protocol

*Fail-closed* means that missing, inconsistent, stale, or contaminated evidence produces a refusal rather than an optimistic default. The collection unit is the measurement window defined in Section 2. Work that does not fit within two to four hours with at least a 20% failure margin is split prospectively into another independently calibrated window.

### Pre-registration and admission

Before quiet time, the operator freezes the run identifiers, membership, stage order, comparison definitions, calibration retry count, acceptance thresholds, extraction specification, code revision, and an initially empty waiver list. The extraction specification also fixes the estimator: comparative cells in this cycle use the conservative worst-case calculation. Any future alternative must be named and parameter-bound in the committed pre-registration and extraction specification; estimator identity is not accepted from result or floor-artifact data. Every configuration is validated and dry-run against a fresh collection directory. These choices are made before outcomes are visible. Pre-registration here is executable: the launcher and final verifier check the frozen values rather than relying on a narrative promise.

Prospective sizing is part of that freeze. Before collection, the planned effect, workload, and number of independent bundles or ABBA blocks are compared with the cell floor and the claim-side interval width. If the design has too little expected clearance, the operator must prospectively increase independent evidence, change the workload, or narrow the claim; the choice cannot be made after seeing claim data. A workload change is not merely a sensitivity adjustment. For example, lengthening the prompt changes the population of requests being estimated and therefore changes the *estimand*, the quantity the design aims to estimate.

Each stage then passes admission gates immediately before measurement. The approved power supply and power policy must be unchanged; displays must be asleep; the screensaver and low-power mode must be off; thermal pressure must be nominal; and central- and graphics-processor activity must meet the quiet-state criteria. Known background work is allowed to finish before the window, followed by a settling interval, rather than being ignored during analysis. Runtime and telemetry identities, clock anchoring, sample cadence, calibration freshness, and post-run environment observations are also checked. No environment override can produce a claim-bearing member.

### Counterbalanced order

Comparisons use the ABBA blocks defined in Section 3. Conditions A and B are measured in the order A, B, B, A, with ordinary cooldown between members. A machine measured over an hour warms up and shifts as it goes. Running A, B, B, A balances the order, and it puts the A runs and the B runs at the same average point in time — but only when the midpoints of the two A runs and of the two B runs add up to the same total. When member durations and gaps are balanced, steady drift therefore largely subtracts. The present protocol does not enforce exact time symmetry because member runtimes and recovery-gated cooldowns are unequal and variable, so ABBA is expected to reduce, not guarantee cancellation of, smooth within-block drift, and curvature survives. Actual time imbalance should be reported or incorporated as a covariate; the midpoint reference and separate whole-window drift allowance remain required. For null-floor calibration, A and B are aliases of the same configuration and payload, so any nonzero block difference is a false comparative effect. For scientific contrasts, the A and B definitions are frozen in advance, and the contrast is computed within each block before blocks are aggregated.

### Evidence custody and refusals

The bundle defined in Section 2 is the basic custody object. Every file in a bundle that could support a claim is recorded in the campaign record by its cryptographic hash, the fingerprint that changes if any byte of the file changes. The fingerprints cover exactly the files the analysis reads, and the final verdict then fixes which runs belong to the window, so swapping a file or leaving one out after collection can be detected against the retained record. Failed or interrupted artifacts are never deleted or overwritten. Each preserved attempt to collect a declared member is a *recorded occurrence*. Moving an occupied retry slot to quarantine outside the active collection directory is an operator action that the recording tools validate rather than an automatic one; the member is recollected only under an allowed retry; and an append-only supersession record states which recorded occurrence governs. Two present bundles claiming to be the same recorded occurrence cause a refusal. The final whole-window verdict binds the complete declared member set, its preserved attempts and supersessions, the calibration bracket, policy, and drift evidence by hash, so a later consumer cannot silently select a more favorable subset.

Collection does not continue indefinitely through environmental interruptions. Each stage attempt stops at its first member failure, and after the same cause fails a window stage for the third time the window itself closes rather than being retried. In actual operation, nights with three environmental member interruptions were closed and preserved as salvage rather than repeatedly retried. The surviving data may remain diagnostic, but it becomes claim-bearing only if the pre-registered verdict and extraction rules accept the complete resulting member set. This abandon-after-three rule limits outcome-dependent retrying and makes the cost of a noisy environment visible.

Custody — the requirement that every claim trace back to evidence whose alteration would be detectable against retained records — continues after collection. The corpus is written once by the tools, bound by hashes, and backed up before extraction. The extractor checks member hashes, required files and cross-file consistency, calibration identity, environment admission, complete campaign membership, cooldown evidence, phase metric identity, and the whole-window drift allowance. It excludes or refuses a cell according to the frozen rules; missing uncertainty never becomes zero. The result is what we call single-authority, hash-bound custody: one governed tool writes the record, and it recomputes every fingerprint itself, so a missing file, a swapped file, an out-of-date input, or a quiet return to an earlier version is refused rather than accepted. If a different estimator is used, its identity lives only in the committed pre-registration and is worked out again inside the governed analysis path; no field of an admitted result or floor file carries it. In that estimator's implementation, the admitted file formats reject any field naming an estimator, even when the name is buried inside a nested structure or written with escape characters, and the reader rejects a file that gives the same key twice. Removing the vocabulary altogether closed a forgery route that had already been demonstrated against the file format, which is stronger than adding one more check to a field an attacker controls. This does not make every admitted surface closed by construction: the guarantee still depends on the governed extraction and custody checks. The Discussion and limitations section states the trust boundary of this single-authority chain. The hashes bind the evidence consumed by the analysis and make post-hoc substitution detectable relative to the disclosed digests. Specifically, the mint compares its inputs against the frozen list of expected input digests (the *pin set*), whose checksum the operator supplies on the command line, and authenticates the recorded head commit of the calibration record against the operator's own local repository copy. For v2 issuance, the issuing tool derives the project commit and tree state from git, refuses a dirty tree, and validates supplied labels against the derived state. It also records whether `origin/main` contains the project head and whether it contains the commit that last changed the recorded head commit of the calibration record; either containment fact may be recorded as unknown rather than causing refusal. This mechanism creates a hard gate with specific recorded reasons against honest operator error and gives reviewers a public history against which to check the disclosed digests. A claim-bearing floor must be produced by the specified extraction path and consumed in the controlled analysis run; a standalone floor or result artifact cannot authorize a claim.

## 6. Demonstration results

The planned demonstration will ask a deliberately modest scientific question: on one named consumer stack, how does phase-resolved energy per request differ between two sizes of the same model family, and can each registered difference pass its own floor and direction gates? The two conditions will be instruction-tuned models of the same family at roughly 1.5 and 7 billion parameters, both 4-bit quantized and served by the same runtime. The models share frozen prompt bytes and output policy within each arm: the token-generation arm uses the frozen 128-token prompt (p128), while the separate prompt-processing arm uses the fixed synthetic 256-token prompt (p256). Demonstration measurements will be collected in dedicated quiet windows under Section 4, with the floors of Section 3 derived from the protocol-controlled floor windows.

### Pre-registered design

Each model size will receive its own token-generation floor cell. The floor for 256-token prompt processing is measured in its own right rather than borrowed: its floor cells are registered in advance in the frozen campaign plans, and no value is carried over from a different prompt length. Each floor design includes an absolute-repeatability arm and a null ABBA arm and is frozen before collection. The registered demonstration contains two model-size directional contrasts: one for token generation and one for prompt processing under a fixed synthetic 256-token prompt. The two planned comparisons — token generation and 256-token prompt processing — are treated as a single registered family of two tests and corrected together by the Holm procedure, which tightens each test's threshold so that running two tests does not raise the overall chance of a false positive (\(\alpha=0.05\), \(m=2\)). Both tests are two-sided, and the direction each is expected to go was registered in advance. Each contrast will be computed within its own ABBA blocks whose A and B are the two model sizes, on the frozen phase-energy metric. Its floor gate will compare the point-estimate magnitude with the cell floor; its separate direction gate will ask whether both endpoints of the composed interval support the registered direction.

### Prospective workload sizing

Earlier captures were analysed for diagnosis only — nothing in them can support a claim — and that analysis informed how the workloads were sized. For the natural short prompt, the difference the design expected to produce only barely exceeded the sizing figure. That was a warning about the design before collection, not a refusal of any measured result: no fresh demonstration comparison has yet been measured or refused. The token-generation comparison had considerably more room to spare.

The registered prompt-processing arm adopts a fixed 256-token prompt whose projected contrast is approximately 11.6 J. ## 7. Discussion and limitations

The attribution-limited resolution item below quotes the full range of what was retained, rather than presenting one run's largest bound and its derived quotient as though they described the whole corpus. It explains why boundary placement, rather than run-to-run scatter, is what this implementation has to attack first.

A published refusal is scientifically useful because it maps the operating domain of the measurement method. A contaminated window, an unresolved clock anchor, or a contrast that fails a gate is not evidence that two systems are equal. It is evidence that the named implementation cannot support that claim under the recorded conditions. Preserving the failed evidence and its reason prevents selective reporting and helps later work distinguish a small physical effect from an inadequate measurement.

Prompt length illustrates why feasibility is tied to the estimand. A longer prompt can increase prompt-processing energy and make a contrast easier to resolve, but it answers a different question about a different request population. Feasibility therefore means that a pre-registered design can adjudicate the effect it actually names, not that the workload can be enlarged until any difference becomes visible. Beyond this Apple-silicon stack, the transferable contribution is the method—calibrate boundary attribution, measure drift, separate floor and direction gates, and preserve refusals—not the numerical floor, which must be established again for each instrument, boundary, and stack.

**Attribution-limited resolution.** On the implementation studied here, the largest source of uncertainty is where the phase boundary is placed, not how much runs scatter. Across the 30 phase-repeatability runs of window a10, the combined bound on that placement spans 25.6–31.1 ms; each run's phase-energy envelope divided by its own bound gives 21–58 W; and the ordinary prompt-processing envelopes span 0.98–1.47 J. Window a9 supplies reference and bracketing-calibration context but no phase runs. Those watt figures are quotients we computed, not power steps anyone measured, and repeating runs does not shrink the boundary-placement uncertainty. When that dominance is the only condition that would otherwise force a refusal, and an exact corner-widened floor exists, the result may be published with an explicit attribution-limited label; the label never rescues any other refusal. There is no universal five-joule practical sizing disclosure under the estimator used this cycle: the retained token-generation contrast cell has an 8.611855 J floor before its separate claim-side bound is added for sizing. For a historical non-claim diagnostic of the short-prompt model-size contrast, its projected clearance was marginal against this conservative scale. That diagnostic motivated the registered fixed-256-token prompt-processing arm and the funded dedicated floor cells for both stacks; it was not a fresh measurement or an issued refusal. The fresh contrast can still be refused if it does not clear the registered gates. The resulting limitation concerns what this implementation, on the named stack and boundary, can support.

**The correction traded precision for validity.** The revised anchor pays for an uncertainty its predecessor simply assumed away, and doing so costs resolution: individual bounds widen — 3.09 µs on the shared fixture, 0.311 ms in the mean anchor term across the corpus — and two of nineteen calibration members are refused rather than accepted. **One unit, one stack, one boundary.** Every result is scoped to one physical machine, operating-system build, runtime and library stack, model artifact, quantization, tokenizer, token-selection and output policy, telemetry backend, and measurement boundary. Nothing here ranks hardware classes or vendors, and the *powermetrics* processor-package boundary (CPU, GPU, and neural-engine rails only) is not comparable to a graphics-board or wall boundary without a calibrated bridge.

**No external meter.** The calibration validates timing attribution—whether samples near a commanded edge land inside or outside a phase integral. It does not validate the counter's absolute gain or whole-system scale; absolute joule values remain internal to the named software boundary. A wall-power instrument could test totals but would still not adjudicate the division of a total between phases, which is the question the pulse-train experiment answers.

**Where the captures came from rests on trusting the operator.** JouleWise authenticates its files, its custody chain, and its derivations end to end, but the origin of a capture is another matter: nothing here defends against deliberate fabrication by the one person running the machine, and the design assumes no hostile program is running on that machine. The hashes and git bindings detect post-hoc substitution relative to disclosed or independently retained digests and guard against honest operator error, but the root remains under the experimenters' control. The present system has no independent signer, external timestamp, or write-once log. For v2 issuance, the issuing tool derives the project commit and tree state from git, refuses a dirty tree, and validates supplied labels against the derived state. It records whether `origin/main` contains the project head and whether it contains the commit that last changed the recorded head commit of the calibration record; either containment fact may be recorded as unknown rather than causing refusal. The chain therefore provides machine-checked internal consistency and tamper evidence relative to those retained digests, not machine-verified or third-party-verifiable provenance. A forged record would still need a self-consistent committed history able to survive review of the pushed public repository.

**Load-regime transfer.** The calibration pulses are graphics-processor matrix multiplications under light central-processor load; transferring their timing bound to sustained mixed-load inference is an explicit assumption, constrained by the in-window bracket and the planned empirical floor probes but not eliminated. The drift allowance is measured per window with no duration-scaling law because the evidence does not identify one; longer windows than those characterized would need fresh drift evidence.

**Validated tighter estimator, registered for the next mint.** A two-shared-edge calculation treats the onset error and offset error as common within an A/B/B/A block while keeping bundle-specific residuals adversarial. On the retained token-generation contrast cell it gives 1.869502 J, about 4.6 times tighter than the 8.611855 J worst-case default. After the arithmetic was moved behind the governed extraction boundary, a terminal audit found zero exact-arithmetic understatements in 4,096 independently generated cases. More than six successive adversarial review rounds had tested progressively smaller defects, from 0.25 J down to approximately \(5\times10^{-10}\) J, with exact rational arithmetic used as the acceptance bar; each discovered defect class was closed, including a serialization attack addressed by removing registration vocabulary from admitted artifacts. **Sampling resolution.** The power sampler's approximately 112 ms cadence is comparable to the shortest measured prompt-processing windows on the smaller model: 0.121–0.147 seconds for the retained 128-token prompts, with many windows overlapping only two power intervals. The pipeline labels those windows not resolvable at the sampler's cadence rather than claiming resolution the instrument lacks, and the same pressure is expected for the 256-token cells on the smaller model. This is a limit on resolution within a correctly identified phase window, not phase mislabeling. The phase-recording proof establishes exact boundary identification for the inspected corpus: raw-trace reconstruction and independent reintegration reproduced every stored prompt-processing energy with 0.0 J discrepancy, every prompt-processing interval ended before token generation began, and phase overlap was zero.

**Sequential requests only.** Phase boundaries are well posed because one request runs at a time. Continuous batching, speculative decoding, and multi-request serving overlap phases by design and are out of scope; the method would need a defensible boundary definition before it could be extended there.

**Operational constraints as evidence.** Claim-bearing windows require a machine quiescent to the admission gates—no interactive agent sessions, displays asleep, and background work settled. Captures of the idle machine, kept permanently outside the claim path, show an idle power level that is low but bursty. It is the bursts, not the average, that threaten a clearance of a few joules, because subtracting an idle average removes only the steady part. The zero-agent window rule is therefore load-bearing, and results collected outside it would not be admitted.

## 8. Related work

### Energy-benchmarking lineage

Energy benchmarking has a two-decade methodological lineage that JouleWise inherits rather than replaces. JouleSort argued that an energy-efficiency benchmark is incomplete unless it fixes a workload, a metric of comparison, and rules for running the workload and measuring energy, and it made the measurement boundary a first-class part of those rules: all power taken at the wall including conversion losses, every participating component counted even when idle, and any net change in stored battery energy shown to be no greater than zero with 95% confidence or included in the total [3]. It also named, without quantifying, the failure mode this paper measures: it rejected a fixed-energy-budget metric partly because of “inaccuracies in synchronizing readings from a power meter to the actual runs,” alongside the meter's own ±1.5% specification. Industry benchmarks then codified those run rules: SPECpower_ssj2008 fixed a graduated-load server workload with an accepted-analyzer and reporting discipline [15]; the SPEC methodology bounds analyzer uncertainty at 1% AC over the ranges actually measured, forbids automatic range transitions because uncertainty is undefined during them, and requires unknown or over-uncertainty samples to be detected and reported rather than silently averaged [2]; TPC-Energy added an audited energy metric to transaction-processing benchmarks [16]; and the Green500 ranked supercomputers by performance per watt [17]. Full-system power modeling took the complementary route of validating an estimate against a measured reference: Mantis calibrated once per system against measured AC power and was validated on two very different servers to within roughly 10% [4], and later work compared several such high-level models across machines from laptop to server [18]. JouleWise sits inside this tradition but differs on three axes that change what must be published: its boundary is a software counter over the processor package's CPU, GPU, and neural-engine rails rather than a wall meter, its unit of claim is a sub-request phase rather than a whole run, and the uncertainty it must disclose is not an analyzer specification but a measured bound on where a phase edge can fall. That is why the detection floor, rather than the joule value, is the primary result — and why the timing-synchronization concern JouleSort raised in passing becomes, at phase granularity on an uncalibrated software counter, the dominant term.

### LLM inference energy measurement

TokenPowerBench provides one of the closest reporting structures: it reports prefill energy and decode energy-per-token and groups results by context length [7]. Its disclosed method, however, does not specify the boundary events, alignment rule, repetition and variance protocol, decode-token denominator, or external validation; no idle-baseline subtraction is defined; and batch sizes are swept from 32 to 1024 under vLLM and TensorRT-LLM, so requests overlap by construction. JouleWise adopts phase-specific reporting but restricts its primary scope to one sequential request, where runtime-emitted phase boundaries are well posed and can be calibrated.

Ruf and Detyniecki isolate prefill by generating exactly one token and derive decode by subtraction, using a single run per context length with no error bars [19] — a fresh prefill-focused instance of phase separation by workload construction rather than calibrated boundary events.

The Illusion of Power Capping in LLM Decode samples graphics-processor power, integrates those samples, repeats configurations, and cross-checks sufficiently long operations against a separate hardware energy counter [20]. This is a stronger measurement template than an unqualified software reading. Its counter agreement, run-to-run variation, and sub-100 ms snapshot-fallback regime nevertheless remain separate diagnostics rather than one bound on the reported effect. It monitors die temperature (42–45 °C under its clamp), but does not counterbalance run order. JouleWise composes those classes of uncertainty into a decision rule for each contrast.

Apple-focused system comparisons reinforce the need for boundary labels. Silicon Showdown compares a graphics-board boundary on one platform with an on-chip *powermetrics* boundary on Apple hardware, while runtimes and precision stacks also differ [21]. Intelligence per Watt and ML.ENERGY similarly emphasize breadth across deployed inference configurations [22] [8]. Such studies answer valuable systems questions, but because their measurement boundaries and software stacks differ, they cannot by themselves support a hardware-class causal ranking. JouleWise therefore makes the named stack and measurement boundary part of every result and prioritizes within-boundary effects.

### Software power counters and measurement standards

RAPL in Action established a validation agenda for commodity software counters: align lag before comparing streams, model wall power rather than assuming identity, account for temporal correlation, warm the system, measure sampler overhead, and inspect update granularity, overflow, jitter, and timestamp behavior [5]. Jay and Ostapenco likewise regress software readings against wall power and show that disagreement varies with load; they avoid claims about subtotals that the reference meter cannot observe [6]. Dauner et al. ask how consistent the RAPL and NVML counters are with themselves as the sampling rate changes, checked against a wall meter. They find that NVML's running totals update no faster than about every 100 ms, and that a flagship GPU's counter underestimates integrated power by up to 95% when sampled every 0.5 ms, recovering only at 1 s [23]. On different counters, that independently establishes the point this paper depends on: how often a software counter updates can quietly bias an energy reading, which is why the roughly 112 ms *powermetrics* cadence is treated here as a first-order limit of the instrument. Their work characterizes counter behaviour over a whole workload, with no phase boundaries in it; JouleWise instead characterizes where a boundary defined by the runtime falls inside a request, and turns that into a gate a claim must pass. Cao et al. provide an NLP-side empirical prior: software counters carry 2–47% error against a hardware meter, with software variance twice the meter's [24]; like [5] and [6], this is total-energy evidence only, so the wall-meter-cannot-adjudicate-phases hinge remains. Panigrahy and Tyagi supply the sibling audit of a platform where per-process attribution is impossible because no counter exists [25]; JouleWise shows that a counter's presence is not sufficient — its timing behavior must be characterized before it licenses phase attribution, and absent that characterization the correct output is refusal. JouleWise follows this epistemic boundary. A future external meter could test total gain and load dependence, but it would not validate how a total is divided between prompt processing and token generation. The in-window pulse train addresses that distinct question.

MLPerf Power supplies the broad benchmark setting and public-run discipline, including NTP clock synchronization between the system under test and the Director [1]. The associated SPEC Power and Performance Methodology supplies the load-specific analyzer uncertainty, fixed ranges, minimum intervals, invalid-sample accounting, and controlled battery behavior attributed here [2]. JouleWise translates their per-run reject-on-missing-evidence principle to a consumer software counter. Its principal uncertainty is not only an analyzer specification; it includes whether samples at a software-defined phase edge belong inside or outside the integral.

### Metrology and experimental discipline

Prospective paired minimum-detectable-effect methods estimate the smallest effect a planned paired study has adequate statistical power to find. They size a study from paired variation and can impose a one-way prior ratchet: observed variability may raise the planned threshold but may not lower it after outcomes are seen [26]. JouleWise uses that logic for prospective design, but it does not treat the worst-case attribution term as independent random noise. Root-sum-square composition would be anti-conservative for a systematic edge-placement bound. Instead, the analysis maximizes over calibrated edge positions, measures drift with bracketed references, and publishes the resulting floor with the effect.

This emphasis changes the role of failed runs. A rejected admission, stale calibration, contaminated member, or below-floor contrast is evidence about the instrument's operating domain. Hash-bound raw traces, runtime events, configurations, supersessions, verdicts, and pre-registration records make that evidence capable of being reduced again under the governed process. The refusal is retained rather than disappearing behind a final average.

### Split and disaggregated inference

Revisiting Disaggregated Large Language Model Serving reports essentially unconditionally higher energy for disaggregation on its evaluated two-graphics-processor node, while its performance benefits depend on load, baseline, and transfer conditions [27]. DualScale models graphics-processor-only phase energy by regression [12]; Prima.cpp reports whole-run but not stage energy [10]; and SplitZip makes no energy claim [28].

JouleWise claims no scheduler; split inference is a future instrument demonstration requiring both endpoints, named boundaries, cross-device clock alignment, and a pre-registered floor or calibrated bridge.

## 10. Conclusion

JouleWise treats a software power counter as a scientific instrument whose operating domain must be measured. The present evidence shows why: on the studied Apple-silicon stack, phase-boundary attribution can dominate repeatability, so more runs alone do not make a small contrast trustworthy. The final paper will report the planned characterization and two-model demonstration with separate floor and direction gates, including any refusal the recorded evidence requires.

The reusable contribution is the combination of six habits: calibrate the instrument inside the same session it measures; build the detection floor only out of quantities you have measured; work out before collecting whether the planned experiment can resolve the effect it names; alternate the run order so slow drift cannot pass for a real difference; refuse rather than guess when the evidence is missing or inconsistent; and let a single tool write the record, tying every file to it by cryptographic fingerprint. Those habits may transfer to other software counters, but the floor's numerical value will not; every new instrument, boundary, and stack must establish its own. Future work can add independent stacks, model families, an external meter for total-energy scale, and richer phase structures such as speculative decoding, mixture-of-experts routing, and split inference.

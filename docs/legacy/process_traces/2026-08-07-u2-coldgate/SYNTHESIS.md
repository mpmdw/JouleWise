## OUTCOME: NOT RATIFIED — PACKET REMANDED, EXHIBIT TO REWORK

**The gate cannot ratify on this packet, and the divergence between the
two verdicts is itself the proof.** The refuter established — from the
exhibit's own bytes — that `SUCCESSOR_DECISION_IDS` declares D-102,
D-109, and D-117 as the exhibit's binding authorities, while the packet
quoted D-102 and D-116 in full and consigned D-109 and D-117 to its
unswept list. D-109 self-describes as supplying "the authority/universe
rulings D-102 left silent," and its R1.2 / R1.4 / R2.2 / R2.5 / R2.6 /
R2.8 are operative text for seven of the twelve questions (Q1, Q4, Q5,
Q6, Q8, Q9, Q11). The cold judge — correctly ruling from the quoted
binding text, as chartered — ratified several questions whose governing
clauses were never in front of him; his own Q2 caveat ("if the rule
traces to a ratified decision not quoted, that is a packet-completeness
problem") states the general principle. A ruling produced this way
cannot stand as ratification, and the defect is the ASSEMBLY's (it was
instructed to quote governing clauses in full including adverse ones; it
selected the wrong entries), not either judge's.

This is the third recorded instance of the selective-quotation packet
defect class, now in a new form: whole-entry omission rather than
truncation. **Packet rule adopted for all future gates: the packet MUST
quote, in full, every decision entry the exhibit itself declares as
authority (its decision-ID tuple or equivalent), and the assembly must
diff its quotation set against that declaration before convening.**

**Terms used below, each built once here because they recur.**

- **S-0** — the first step of the `_v4` measurement transaction: a
  clone-proof run, executed at the lead's bench, in which Ed approves each
  freeze command at a live permission prompt. Nothing downstream (windows,
  data, the paper's §6) exists until S-0 passes.
- **S-1, the candidate** — the code that S-0 executes against: the
  implementation of ruling D-151 (the versioned-successor pinset) and the
  marker co-design ruling. It is called a *candidate* because until it is
  reviewed and merged it is a proposal, not the instrument.
- **Gauntlet** — the standing shape for delegated work: implement under an
  exhaustively declared write scope → an independent **refuter** attacks it
  → fix round → **delta re-audit**, which re-attacks the fix itself, because
  fix rounds introduce their own defects.
- **Refuter, seat, and lens** — a *seat* is a reviewer instance; a *refuter*
  is a seat whose job is to break the work, not approve it. A **contract
  lens** asks whether the artifact does what the ruling said; an
  **execution lens** builds and runs attacks against it. **REFUTED** means
  an attack succeeded; **SURVIVES** means it failed.
- **Writer ≠ reviewer** — the rule that no seat grades its own round. A
  document written by the seat that did the work is a **self-report** and is
  marked as one, no matter how careful it is.
- **Preservation commit** — a commit made by the lead, on a branch, purely
  to stop uncommitted delegated work from evaporating when the agent holding
  it dies. It is explicitly *not reviewed and not gauntleted*, and its
  commit message carries the harvest order for whoever picks it up.
- **Rule-1 gate** — the standing rule that the lead never delegates final
  verification. For S-1 this meant reading the entire diff personally; a
  delegate's "tests green" is necessary and never sufficient.
- **Kernel, and live rows** — `docs/process/state_kernel.json`, the
  machine-readable list of open work orders. A *live row* is an open one; the
  count is quoted in commit messages and is checkable with
  `gen_state --check`.
- **Blocked-test partition** — the classification of every skipped test into
  `S0-BLOCKED` (would pass once S-0 mints `_v4` bytes),
  `STRUCTURAL-BLOCKED` (blocked by fixture design, which minting does not
  change), and `CRASH-BLOCKED` (the test aborts the interpreter). The whole
  arc turns on this partition being measured rather than assumed.
- **Firing** — one CI job failing with a given signature. A *class* is the
  set of firings sharing a root cause. Counting firings is how a flake earns
  a work-order row.
- **hC** — the SHA-256 digest of the exact bytes of Ed's step-6 confirmation
  table. It is the thing Ed confirms with his own eyes, and whether any code
  actually checks it is the subject of §2.4.

**The Sol G-2 refuter (execution lens, read-only) — REFUTED, 1 blocker / 3

### 2.7 Close-out: four defects the fix round found in itself

signature: **a surface the round moved on one side only.**

1. A hand-built `argparse.Namespace` in a lifecycle fixture never gained the
   new attribute — the sibling builder in `test_launch_window.py` was

2. A test pinned the public authoring signature as a 1-tuple while the

3. A synthetic registry shipped an empty allowlist and was correctly refused

## 14. Operational notes

**The ~50-minute background-Bash kill class.** The single most actionable
instrument finding of the arc. Long-running **background Bash commands** are
terminated at almost exactly 60 minutes; **Monitor-type tasks are not** —
they survived past 60 minutes repeatedly in the same session. Because the
app-hosted Codex turn dies with or shortly after its wrapper, **any Sol turn
longer than ~50 minutes fronted by a background Bash wrapper is structurally
doomed on this harness.** The adopted answer is A+B: Monitor-shell transport,
and sub-40-minute commit-first turns so that a kill costs minutes rather than
hours. Both killed runs had deferred their commits behind verification, which
is exactly why they cost their work.

## 2. What a refusal is for, and what each one has bought

A refusal is worth its cost when all three hold: (i) the condition it detects would corrupt a *reported* number or the blinding, (ii) nothing downstream would catch it, and (iii) the cost of a false refusal (a rerun, a review round, an escalation) is smaller than the expected harm. Ed's doctrine compresses this to "physics refuses; everything else is a flag". Table rows below apply it.

**What the stops found, by class.**

- *Check defects (runs 1, 2, 7).* Three of eight stops were R0's own checks being wrong. Each cost a fix lane with two or three model seats and a CI run of 1-2.5 h on this lane (items 186, 190, 195). The run-7 defect was a constant with "no derivation", exactly the kind of number the 2026-10-17 charge forbids ("no silly numbers: every value or bar carries a derivation", `docs/process_traces/2026-10-17-magistrate-52d50d1f/reg-gate-charge.md:27`).
- *Guard artefacts (runs 3, 4).* The guard judged one `ps` `%CPU` snapshot (`prewindow.busy_contaminants`, `joulewise/prewindow.py:23`, limit 5 %, `:5`). With about 100 samples per R0 and `mdworker_shared` respawning every 2-7 minutes, an unchanged re-run "would very likely be refused again" (item 189). The lead's own ruling in item 189 then made the SMC probe's host aggregate "journaled and flagged, not judged": the doctrine applied to half the guard.
- *Real but trivial contention (runs 5, 6).* After PR #691 the guard judges CPU-seconds per second over at least 30 s (`r0_host.py:147-166`, `prewindow.INTERVAL_S` = 30, `CPU_LIMIT_PERCENT` = 5.0). Both refusals were about 0.07 CPU-s/s for 36 s, during the *recovery* phase of a capture whose purpose is to classify temperature keys by their time constants under a 900 s GPU load. Seven per cent of one core for half a minute is a few tens of joules against a load of tens of kilojoules; it does not move a heatsink key's tau. Each refusal threw away 1,300-1,500 s of capture and the whole 900 s load. The remedy adopted was timing (item 193) and a `/tmp` prune (item 201). No design change was made to the refusal itself.
- *Launcher bug (run 0).* Caught by the cheapest check; nothing to prune.

**What the refusals protected.** R0 is "selection data, not R1, thermal validation or an arm authorization" (`docs/align/r0_desk_runbook.md:4-5`) and "a readiness check, not the energy measurement" (RUN_STATE item 192, lead disposition of cold findings 1-2). None is a reported quantity, none is blinded. Under the doctrine, every R0 refusal except "a measurement window is armed / a chain is running / the host is not the measurement Mac" is a flag.

**The one science catch came from reading, not refusing.** Run 7's inconclusive result, once the coverage bug was fixed in a diagnostic re-rank, still selected `Tg*` keys reading a fixed -2.75 °C placeholder and a `TaRT` still cooling through idle (item 195, "Opus HIGH, re-executed by the lead on the raw journal"). **Escalation rule.** Item 181's "third consecutive failure of the same check goes to Ed" fired once (item 194). Ed replied "go" in two minutes. The rule exists for science changes; applied to R0 contention it is a wait with no decision content.

## 8. Open questions

1. **Is the R0 SMC capture's contention sensitivity derivable?** Nobody has written down how much CPU contention, for how long, in which phase, shifts a heatsink key's tau or the last-60 s baseline. Until that number exists, the 0.05 CPU-s/s × 30 s rule is a convention, and the doctrine says a convention is a flag. A one-line derivation would settle whether rank 1 is "flag" or "refuse above X".
2. **Does W1 under High Power survive R1?** If the A-B-A legs do not adopt it (council: ≥ 25 % longer t_decline), the whole High Power path is dead code for W1 and rank 2 becomes a deletion rather than a simplification.
3. **D5 on the release hold.** Which side is wrong (L9's floor binding or the producing tooling) is still undecided by the probe. Until it is, the block-5 release is held by a refusal whose message does not name its cause (rank 11). Nothing here reads or infers its value.
4. **The R1 night is longer than W1.** The no-redo worst case (56,207 s) exceeds the W1 window cap (44,640 s) by 3.2 h. Whether a 15.6-25 h rehearsal is proportionate to the window it rehearses is a design question for the council, outside this audit's remit, but the number should be in front of them.
5. **Who owns the pruning PRs?** Every row in section 7 touches a frozen or reviewed lane (`pilot10-benign`, the R1 integration line, or `main`'s Block-5 code under a cited rebind). The R1 lane is in the magistrate's hands for arming; rank 1 and rank 2 change arm-path code and should land as one reviewed PR each, not as part of the arm.

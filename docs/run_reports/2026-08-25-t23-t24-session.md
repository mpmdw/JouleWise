**Terms used below, each built once here because they recur.**

- **The `_v4` transaction** — the governed sequence that freezes the
  campaign plan and its supporting files, installs the fourth-generation
  registry, issues the campaign family, mints its freeze receipts, and
  records the operator's hand-checked confirmation of exactly which bytes
  were frozen. It runs once, for real, on the measuring machine.
- **S-0, the clone proof** — the first step of that transaction: the same
  sequence, executed at the lead's bench against a throwaway clone of the
  repository, so that a defect is found before real measurement data
  depends on it. Nothing downstream — windows, data, the paper's §6 —
  exists until S-0 passes.
- **The runsheet** — `docs/process_traces/2026-08-22-t20/s0-runsheet-r4.md`,
  the executable script S-0 follows, revised in place through revisions r1
  … r6. Its §§1.1–3.10 are the transaction proper; §4 is a battery of
  tamper probes; §4.10 is fixation; §5 is the acceptance read.
- **An estate** — one cut of the clone proof: a fresh clone at a named
  BASE commit, executed until it either completes or halts. Ten were cut
  in all. An estate's custody (its transcripts and logs) is preserved
  read-only whatever its outcome.
- **Failure semantics, and STRUCK** — the runsheet's own rule for what a
  halt means. If an *environment or dependency precondition was false* —
  the instrument itself was wrong — the estate is **STRUCK**: its cure
  lands on main, the instrument is **re-ratified**, and the next estate
  restarts from §1.1. A STRUCK estate's positive results may still stand
  as *evidence of executability* (proof the steps run), which is never
  claim custody (evidence a measurement is admissible). An
  **ordinary-defect** halt is one where no estate precondition was false —
  the estate's greens stand.
- **hC** — the SHA-256 digest of the exact bytes of the step-6
  confirmation table: the table Ed reads with his own eyes before anything
  is published, and whose digest he types back. It is the one input in the
  whole transaction the automation is forbidden to supply for itself.
- **Seat, refuter, lens** — a *seat* is one reviewer instance; a *refuter*
  is a seat whose job is to break the work rather than approve it. A
  **contract lens** asks whether the artifact does what the ruling said an
  artifact should do; a **semantic** or **execution lens** asks whether it
  actually works. **REFUTED** means an attack succeeded.
- **Delta re-audit** — a re-attack on the *fix round itself*, run because
  fix rounds demonstrably introduce their own defects. A **joint** delta
  re-audit re-attacks two streams' final heads *together*, which is the
  only way to see a defect that exists solely in the join.
- **Kernel, and live rows** — `docs/process/state_kernel.json`, the
  machine-readable list of open work orders. A *live row* is an open one;
  the count is quoted in commit messages and checkable with
  `scripts/gen_state.py --check`.

## 5. CODEX-BRIDGE-SANDBOX-01 (#191)

An off-critical-path row, cured while estates were between cuts. The
defect, recorded live on 2026-08-05: `scripts/codex-bridge review` wrote
`observer_sandbox=read-only` into every observer row, but the non-app
review launch carried **no sandbox flag at all**. The audit trail asserted
an enforcement the launch never received. `new` had the mirror-image
weakness — the mode spelled as a literal at the launch site while the
recorded value lived in a separate variable.

The cure makes `observer_sandbox` the only place a mode is named, feeding
`sandbox_args=(-s "$observer_sandbox")` into every non-app launch (`new`,
`resume`, `review`); `resume` was included deliberately, because adding a
manifest `sandbox` field while leaving `resume` unbound would have shipped
a brand-new field that misstates resume exactly the way the observer row
misstated review. Flag position was verified against the installed CLI:
`-s` is an option of `codex exec` itself, so the array sits before the
subcommand word.

Evidence is three-layered, and the third layer is the interesting one:
**argv capture** (exactly one sandbox flag per mode, the expected value,
positioned before the subcommand word), **records** (the manifest row and
every observer row equal the captured value), and a **source-level guard**
(no launch site names a mode literally). The third is the "cannot drift
apart" clause made mechanical rather than asserted. Defect-shaped proof:
with the script reverted to `733914e1` the four new tests fail 3F/1E and
review's captured argv comes back with no `-s` — the reported defect
verbatim; restored, `Ran 9 tests … OK`.

The PR's own "owed, and deliberately not done here" section is worth
preserving: the kernel row was fenced off from that session and left open
(it is closed in this arc's wave); `scripts/test_timings.json` was left
untouched so its harvested-maximum provenance stays true; and *that the
flag takes effect* rests on the same argument the bridge already relies on
for `-m`/`-c` in review mode, rather than on a live quota-spending review
run.

## 10. Escalation triggers, and how they were spent

The standing rule is that two consecutive rounds failing with the **same
signature** are evidence of a structural problem, and the next spend is a
**consult, not round three**. The arc's value here is that the trigger was
invoked *and* declined, each time with the reasoning written down — which
is what makes it a rule rather than a mood.

**It FIRED once, on the D-153 sweep.** The synthesis states it outright:
the trigger fired on the second same-signature defect. The signature was
*consumers written against interfaces and orderings that D-153 and the
step-6 contract later moved out from under them, with no assertion class
capable of noticing*, and three instances had surfaced — S0-O3 (§3.8
consumes a record §4.10 produces); the F1/`118` byte-pin probe grepping a

nineteen-item cure. Fixing the symptom would have bought one of nineteen.

**It was explicitly NOT MET twice, both times with reasons on the
record.** After the r5 refuter round: "F1 and F2 are cure-round defects,
not a recurrence of the sweep's epoch/supply signature… this is the first
fix round on this stream and the defect class (over-assertion introduced
while composing cures) differs from the consult's root cause… a SECOND
failed fix round on these same formulations would meet the trigger and go

not. A failure of THIS round's edits would meet the trigger." Both
declines name in advance the condition that would flip them — which is the
only form of decline that is auditable.

locality lens is re-sited, **not retired**". Dropping a lens is on the

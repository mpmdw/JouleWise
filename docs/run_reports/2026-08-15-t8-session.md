## Process lesson minted: the wake-source failure class

**Rule (memory `turn-end-wake-source-rule`, recorded 2026-08-15):** never end a
turn with zero live background work.

**Mechanism.** The session only re-invokes on background-task notifications. A
turn that ends with a summary plus a promised queue — "next: X, then Y" — and
**zero live background work has no wake source**: the loop dies silently until
Ed taps. End-of-block summaries feel like natural stopping points and the
harness's "end turn when task complete" instinct fires, but under D-128/D-127
(run the loop; never idle between taps) **a block boundary is a LAUNCH point,
not a stop.**

**Two occurrences in T8, both costing hours:**

1. **~7 h** — five refuter relay agents wedged without ever launching their Sol
   runs; the watcher had died and `.status` is not liveness, so nothing woke the
   loop. The five were killed at 07:15 PT and relaunched from the lead shell,
   where **all five finished inside 13 minutes** (423–760 s each,
   manifest-verified). Seven hours of wall clock bought thirteen minutes of work.
2. **Phase-0 wrap** ended with "then Phase 1 launches" and nothing in flight —
   Ed's second prompt of the day was *"why was there a big gap again that i had
   to message to start work going again???"*

**Applied rule.** Before ending any turn: is at least one background job or
agent live that will notify? If not, **launch the next queued work first**
(there is always queued work under D-128) or schedule a fallback heartbeat.
Promised next steps in prose are not a wake source. This extends
`background-jobs-must-be-tracked` and `proactive-inflight-polling` from "track
what you launch" to **"never hold zero launches."**

The same-signature relay failure also produced a standing council consequence,
already ratified in the verdict: **refuter fleets get output-keyed liveness
checks with a bounded first-checkpoint deadline.**

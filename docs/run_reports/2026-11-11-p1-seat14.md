**How I verified.** Where possible I used DOI-registry metadata: Crossref `api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`, plus `doi.org` content negotiation, which returns the publisher-deposited record. I also used the publisher or proceedings page.

**Style note.** Most entries in refs 1–21 carry `DOI:10.…` (1, 3, 4, 5, 10, 11, 17, 19, 20, 21). Entries with no DOI carry `arXiv:` IDs (6–9, 13, 14, 16, 18) or a URL (2). Refs 22–27 currently carry neither, so they are inconsistent with the rest of the list.

For content I used Roodman's companion review, arXiv:physics/0312102 (https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0312102 and its ar5iv HTML), because the full text of the 2005 review was not reachable from a primary host.

**Citation uses**

1. Line 180–181: "Blind analysis, in which the analysts cannot see the quantity of interest until the analysis is fixed, protects against tuning toward an expected answer [22]; Part 2 uses hidden multiplicative offsets in the manner of that literature, and both parts withhold energies until a recorded release."
   - First clause: **SUPPORTED**. Preventing experimenter's bias toward expected results is the review's stated purpose.
   - Second clause ("hidden multiplicative offsets in the manner of that literature"): **OVERSTATED (mild, provisional)**.
     - The methods this literature describes are the hidden signal box, the hidden answer and the hidden asymmetry.
     - The hidden answer is an *additive* unknown offset. KTeV "inserted an unknown offset into its fitting program", combined with a hidden random ±1 sign, and BaBar used a hidden offset plus a sign flip.
     - The companion review describes no continuous multiplicative scale factor. "Multiplicative offset" is also a contradictory phrase: an offset is additive.
     - Part 2's factors (uniform on [0.8, 1.25] per pack, [0.97, 1.03] per configuration) are an adaptation of the method, not the method itself.
     - Caveat: I could not read the 2005 full text, so I cannot exclude that it mentions scale blinding in passing.
2. Line 1060–1061: "…each model configuration's by a secret factor uniform on [0.97, 1.03], following the hidden-offset practice of blind analysis [22]."
   - **OVERSTATED (mild)**, for the same reason: the cited practice is an additive offset and a sign flip, and the paper applies a multiplicative factor.

**Proposed wording, optional but cheap.**
- Line 181: "…Part 2 hides energies behind secret multiplicative factors, a scale variant of that literature's hidden-offset method, and both parts…"
- Line 1061: "…adapting the hidden-offset method of blind analysis [22] to multiplicative factors."
- If a direct precedent for multiplicative blinding is wanted, the cosmology blinding literature has one (for example, scale-factor blinding in DES/KiDS). I have not verified a specific reference for it.

**Corrected line**
```
22. J. R. Klein and A. Roodman. "Blind Analysis in Nuclear and Particle Physics." *Annual Review of Nuclear and Particle Science* 55, 2005, pp. 141–163. DOI:10.1146/annurev.nucl.55.090704.151521.
```

The metadata is confirmed only through search-index records of the JSTOR entry: vol 6, no 2, 1979, pp. 65–70, stable 4615733. **The primary page itself could not be read.**

**Citation use**

- Line 411–412: "The two primary contrasts (decode and prefill) form one family corrected by Holm's method at α = 0.05 [26]; a contrast that cannot be estimated keeps the family size at two."
  - **SUPPORTED**. Holm's step-down procedure applies to any set of tests and controls the familywise error rate strongly with no assumption about dependence among them. With two tests it rejects the smaller p if p ≤ 0.025, then the other if p ≤ 0.05.
  - Keeping the family at two when a contrast cannot be estimated is a conservative registered choice. It is equivalent to giving the missing test p = 1, which is consistent with Holm.
  - Advisory only: Table 5 prints "Holm-adjusted p". Adjusted p-values for Holm's procedure are a later, standard formalization (commonly credited to Wright 1992), not something Holm 1979 itself reports. Citing [26] for the procedure is correct, and no change is required.

**Corrected line** (no change needed; the optional URL form follows)
```
26. S. Holm. "A Simple Sequentially Rejective Multiple Test Procedure." *Scandinavian Journal of Statistics* 6(2), 1979, pp. 65–70. https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615733.
```

URLs used: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/0304-4076(85)90158-7. For content: http://qed.econ.queensu.ca/working_papers/papers/qed_wp_537.pdf, the authors' version, which states it is the published J. Econometrics 29, 305–325 text with updated references. ScienceDirect returned 403.

**Citation use**

- Line 1042–1043: "Because errors in P̄ scale with P̄, primary intervals use HC3 heteroscedasticity-robust standard errors [27] with Student t at n − p degrees of freedom; the ordinary errors are printed beside them."
  - **SUPPORTED, with a precision caveat**. MacKinnon & White do name HC1, HC2 and HC3, and recommend HC3 ("HC3 is clearly the procedure of choice"), so the citation is correct and conventional.
  - However, *their* HC3 is the full jackknife, eq. (12):
    `((n−1)/n) (XᵀX)⁻¹ (XᵀΩ*X − (1/n) Xᵀu*u*ᵀX) (XᵀX)⁻¹`, with u*ₜ = ûₜ/(1−kₜₜ).
  - The "HC3" in modern software (statsmodels `HC3`, R `sandwich::vcovHC(type="HC3")`) is the simplified `(XᵀX)⁻¹ Xᵀ diag(ûₜ²/(1−hₜ)²) X (XᵀX)⁻¹`. That form drops the (n−1)/n factor and the γ̂γ̂ᵀ term, and is usually credited to Davidson & MacKinnon (1993) as an approximation of the jackknife.
  - The two forms differ by O(1/n). With small n, as in Part 2's per-boundary fits, the difference is not negligible.
  - If the analysis code uses the modern simplified form, which I did not check because it was out of scope, the sentence should say which form it uses.
  - Separately, "Student t at n − p degrees of freedom" is the paper's own choice: MacKinnon & White used normal critical values (1.960/2.576). The placement of [27] after "standard errors" already scopes the citation correctly.
- Suggested precision edit, if the code uses the modern form: "…HC3 heteroscedasticity-robust standard errors (the leverage-scaled form of MacKinnon and White's jackknife estimator [27]) with Student t…". Alternatively, add Davidson & MacKinnon (1993), *Estimation and Inference in Econometrics*, as a second cite. I did not verify a page for it.

**Corrected line**
```
27. J. G. MacKinnon and H. White. "Some Heteroskedasticity-Consistent Covariance Matrix Estimators with Improved Finite Sample Properties." *Journal of Econometrics* 29(3), 1985, pp. 305–325. DOI:10.1016/0304-4076(85)90158-7.
```

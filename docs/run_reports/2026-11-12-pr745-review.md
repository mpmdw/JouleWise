## Findings

### HIGH

**H1. Line 1759 (§11), last sentence, contradicts the sentence above it.**

> "Whether timing attribution limits the instrument in the registered sense, with all twelve ratios at least 2, is asserted neither way."

- "The registered sense, with all twelve ratios at least 2" is something the paper does assert: line 1757 says three ratios are below 2 and the condition "is therefore not met". The sentence reads as leaving that open.
- "Limits the instrument" is a third object. The registered statement is about dominating the detection floor, and line 1757 speaks of "these measurements". RL-4 (viii) bars "limits" wording for the measurement.

Replace the sentence with:

> "The registered statement that timing attribution dominates the detection floor needed all twelve ratios to be at least 2; three are not, so it is not made, and its opposite is not made either."

The first two sentences of the paragraph stand.

### MEDIUM

**M1. Line 899 (§6.3), "each published floor is a timing-bound floor" coins a fourth term that collides with the attribution floor.**

- The paper defines the attribution floor as "the largest first-kind timing bound over a cell's kept runs" (line 1824), and §11 calls it "an attribution floor from phase-edge timing". "Timing-bound floor" therefore names the attribution floor to a reader, not the detection floor.
- RL-4 (viii) allows three terms, each defined once.
- In the same paragraph, "is the term that produced each of the four published values" is stronger than the composition. Each published value is the guarded corner-widened floor plus the drift allowance (0.973 or 1.17 J, more than the repeatability-only floor itself). The ruled word is "set by" (§4.5, RL-66 F07).

Replace the first two sentences of the paragraph with one:

> "In plain terms: the clock-anchor timing bound, not run-to-run repeatability, sets each of the four published floors, and each is several times its repeatability-only value."

The last two sentences of the paragraph ("'Attribution-limited' here is the name of a floor class … and does not make.") stand.

**M2. Line 904, the untouched heading "6.4 Is the floor limited by timing attribution?" now conflicts with line 899.**

- The heading uses the floor class's word, "limited", for the dominance question. That is the conflation RL-70 edit 1 removed from the paragraph beneath it.
- The PR sharpens it: §6.3 now ends by saying "attribution-limited" is not the dominance statement, and the next line asks whether the floor is limited by timing attribution. A reader answers "yes, §6.3 just said so" and then reads that no statement is made.

Retitle:

> "### 6.4 Does timing movement at least double every floor?"

That is the question the section's first sentence answers. Nothing else cites the heading text; `OUTLINE-MAP-2026-11-11.md` line 314 is a dated map and stays as it is.

**M3. Line 924 (§6.4), three wording points in the first three sentences.**

- "Read together, Sections 6.3 and 6.4 say one thing." They say two things that do not conflict; §4.6 (lines 445-446) calls them separate quantities.
- "Set by the timing bound": the bare phrase means the operative timing bound b, in milliseconds, everywhere else in the paper (lines 1055, 1684, 1818). The floor source is the clock-anchor timing bound.
- "The broader registered statement" makes the floor class a narrower form of the dominance statement. They are separate quantities.

Replace the first three sentences with:

> "Sections 6.3 and 6.4 do not conflict. The four published floors are set by the clock-anchor timing bound, and a reader should use them as printed. The registered statement that timing attribution dominates the detection floor needed all twelve ratios to reach 2 and is not made; its opposite is not made either."

The near-threshold sentence and the point-diagnostic sentence stand.

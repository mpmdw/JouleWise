# Live stream ledger - S1 capstone scope/headline freeze (2026-07-09)

Purpose: record shape-changing decisions for stream S1. This stream's
footprint is limited to `docs/contracts/capstone_scope.md`, a short pointer
in `PROJECT_STATUS.md`, and this ledger. The stream worktree does not
self-commit; the lead commits and pushes the stream branch at checkpoints.

## Entries

### SC-1 - PROMOTE-TO-DECISION-LOG - Frozen capstone headline

Decision: freeze the capstone headline as "auditable, boundary-labeled local
LLM energy characterization on named hardware/runtime/model/workload stacks,"
with split inference explicitly gated as stretch rather than required
headline scope.

Evidence:

- `docs/reviews/2026-07-09-scientific-rigor-review.md` B4 and Appendix D
  Part C row "Science vs plumbing" require one headline plus two fallbacks.
- `PROJECT_STATUS.md` already carries the two-track framing: guaranteed
  local energy measurement first, split inference as the differentiating
  validating study when gates land.
- `docs/contracts/claims_ladder.md` D-037 contract caps reader-facing claims
  by evidence level and forbids cross-boundary winners without calibration.

Rationale: the headline is advisor-defensible because it is stack-bound,
boundary-labeled, and compatible with the current hardware reality. It does
not discard split inference; it makes split a gated upgrade.

### SC-2 - Contribution ladder position

Decision: adopt a three-rung contribution ladder: instrument/methodology,
filled measurement matrix, and data-contingent scientific findings.

Evidence:

- `docs/reviews/2026-07-09-scientific-rigor-review.md` settled convergence
  says the evidence architecture is the project's genuine contribution.
- Ed's 2026-07-09 position, supplied in the stream prompt, argues that the
  filled matrix becomes the end-goal novelty once workload, model, and
  hardware axes expand.
- `docs/contracts/claims_ladder.md` allows L2 same-boundary comparisons,
  L3 fitted matrix claims, and L4 only with replication/calibration evidence.

Rationale: I agree with Ed's novelty argument but make the dependency
explicit: auditability is not a substitute for the novel matrix; it is what
makes the matrix cells interpretable enough to count as evidence.

### SC-3 - Minimum viable capstone contract

Decision: make the minimum capstone a boundary-labeled local-characterization
artifact with protected audit rigor, and compose all cuts with R-012 rather
than restating the risk-register ladder.

Evidence:

- `docs/risk_register.md` R-012 already defines cut order and the worst-case
  minimum viable capstone.
- `docs/milestones.md` records that real supervisor, colloquium, final
  report, and hardware dates remain TBD.
- `docs/reviews/2026-07-09-scientific-rigor-review.md` Appendix D Part C row
  "Scope realism" asks for hard stop-lines, deliverables, and cut triggers.

Rationale: the scope contract should be enforceable without creating a
second competing descope ladder. R-012 remains the cut-order authority; the
new contract names the stop-lines that trigger use of that ladder.

### SC-4 - Single-unit limitation language

Decision: require reusable caption/table language that says one physical
unit characterizes a named stack only and does not establish vendor,
hardware-class, or unit-general claims.

Evidence:

- `docs/reviews/2026-07-09-scientific-rigor-review.md` Appendix D Part A
  item 3 and Part C row "One sample external validity" identify this as an
  advisor-bar limitation.
- `docs/contracts/claims_ladder.md` requires independent replication for
  L4 generalized findings.

Rationale: the limitation needs to appear where claims are consumed, not only
in a back-matter limitations section.

### SC-5 - Benchmark consumer and decision

Decision: define JouleWise consumers as local deployers, researchers, and
advisors/reviewers, with the decision each can make and the overreach each
must not infer.

Evidence:

- `docs/reviews/2026-07-09-scientific-rigor-review.md` Appendix D Part A
  item 10 and Part C row "Who uses it" require a first-page consumer and
  decision statement.
- `PROJECT_STATUS.md` Q1-Q6 and two-track framing imply these consumers but
  did not make the decision boundary explicit.

Rationale: naming the consumer turns the benchmark from "interesting
measurement machinery" into a decision-support artifact with clear claim
ceilings.

### SC-6 - Accepted reviewer fix round (renumbered from duplicate SC-4 at final-head review)

Decision: apply the lead-accepted R1 scope review fixes, with all seven
reviewer findings accepted and disposed in the capstone scope contract.

Dispositions:

- FIX-1: R-012 remains the single home of the minimum capstone floor; this
  contract only adds reporting stop-lines.
- FIX-2: Rung 2 is scoped empirical coverage conditioned on related-work
  review; ladder levels attach to claims drawn from the matrix, not to the
  dataset itself.
- FIX-3: The headline is an umbrella scope statement with separate
  per-result ceilings.
- FIX-4: Split claims require a named live-replay, synthetic-transfer, or
  analytical-composition method and the claims ladder; cross-boundary
  quantitative winners additionally require calibration.
- FIX-5: L3 fitted matrix wording expands to the full D-037 evidence list.
- FIX-6: Instrument contribution is available when contract conditions
  remain intact; strict validation is artifact consistency, not physical
  validation.
- FIX-7: Consumer-table comparison wording now requires same-boundary or
  calibrated-boundary comparison plus declared analysis plan and claim level.

Rationale: the fix round removes duplicate floors, avoids global headline
ceilings, and keeps claim levels tied to evidence under D-037 and R-012.
